L1 — joint-localization lemma for N insertions [R] (constant corrected, novelty restricted). HYPOTHESES: J satisfies (J); R_e(k) = r_e/(k - z_e), Im z_e != 0 (r_e arbitrary complex); Fset = {e: Im z_e < 0}, Bset = complement. STATEMENT. (a) Exact form: int_{R^N} prod_e dk_e/(2pi) J(k) prod_e R_e(k_e) = [prod_{e in Fset}(-i r_e) prod_{e in Bset}(+i r_e)] sum_{sigma: B_sigma = Bset, F_sigma = Fset} J^(sigma)(z_1,...,z_N); absolutely convergent at finite i eta; exact for each eta and in the limit. (b) Asymptotics: let kappa := min over contributing sectors and their gaps of |K_l(z)| and assume kappa >= 2 lambda_J. Then |J^(sigma)(z) - Jcal_sigma prod_l (-iK^B_l(z))^{-1} prod_l (iK^F_l(z))^{-1}| <= N 2^{N+1} M_sigma (lambda_J/kappa) prod_l |K_l(z)|^{-1}, with the dimensionally homogeneous M_sigma := sum_t |c_t| lambda_J^{-d_t} (the earlier M_sigma = sum_t |c_t| added coefficients of different dimension: a term with d_t extra states has c_t of d_t extra powers of energy). Hence the integral equals prod_e R_e(0) [sum_sigma w_sigma(z) Jcal_sigma + O(N 2^{N+1} max M_sigma lambda_J/kappa)], w_sigma = (prod_{e in F} z_e / prod_l K^F_l(z)) (prod_{e in B} z_e / prod_l K^B_l(z)). (c) sum_sigma w_sigma = 1: the standard eikonal identity sum_pi prod_m (sum_{l>=m} z_pi(l))^{-1} = prod_e z_e^{-1} (our App B), applied to the initial and final groups separately. For real Glauber poles each group has z_e of one sign, so w_sigma in [0,1] is the probability of an exponential race with rates |z_e| (final group: insertions follow V_H in the order the clocks ring, w_pi = prod z / prod_m sum_{l>=m} z_pi(l); initial group time-reversed, w_pi = prod z / prod_m sum_{l<=m} z_pi(l)). For Glauber factors kappa >= min_e a_e/(2|k_e^+|), so the relative error is O(lambda_J |k^+|/a). PROOF. (i) Absolute convergence at finite eta (Weinberg): on every subspace W the number of non-constant factors exceeds dim W (count the R_e and the gap containing the first variable of the support). (ii) Sector selection: if e in F_sigma but Im z_e > 0, k_e enters only final gaps (poles in the UHP) and R_e (pole in the UHP); the integrand is O(|k_e|^{-2}); close in the LHP: 0; mirror case for e in B_sigma with Im z_e < 0. (iii) Surviving sectors: integrate final variables one after another closing in the LHP (with the other variables real or already shifted to Im < 0, final-gap poles in k_f lie in the UHP): picks -i r_f at k_f = z_f; initial variables in the UHP: +i r_b. (iv) Quantitative bound: single-state gap |1/(A +- K) - 1/(+-K)| = |A|/(|K||A +- K|) <= 2 lambda_J/|K|^2 (|A| <= lambda_J <= |K|/2); telescoping over the N gaps of a single-state term gives <= N 2^N |c_t| (lambda_J/kappa) prod_l |K_l|^{-1}; a term with d_t >= 1 extra states is bounded by |c_t| 2^{N+d_t} kappa^{-d_t} prod |K_l|^{-1} = |c_t| lambda_J^{-d_t} 2^{N+d_t} (lambda_J/kappa)^{d_t} prod |K_l|^{-1} <= |c_t| lambda_J^{-d_t} 2^{N+1} (lambda_J/kappa) prod |K_l|^{-1}; sum over t. Signs: (-i)^m/i^m = (-1)^m matches prod R_e(0) = (-1)^m prod r/prod z, likewise for the initial group. (v) sum w = 1: the order regions partition R_+^m in the race integral; the rational identity extends to complex z. P5 — O(g^6): the direct-line ladder class is not unitarity-closed [A] (citations and qualification added). Take two on-shell eikonal (or massive) lines and the direct-line ladder graphs at O(g^6) (all gluons join the two lines, any crossings). So the ladder class is not closed under unitarity, and its defect is a positive operator. In DY, part of |A^lines|^2 corresponds to cut direct lines (inside G_0) and part to soft lines emitted and absorbed by the same spectator (outside G_0); gauge completion requires the three-gluon vertex. QUALIFICATION (new): the conclusion concerns the closure of G_0 under unitarity (SS-family bookkeeping), not a failure of factorization; 'E.33(c2) needed from O(g^6)' is relative to that bookkeeping. Consistent with Schwartz-Yan-Zhu, PRD 97 (2018) 096017 (for pure Glauber ladders, amplitude-level factorization-violating effects cancel at cross-section level for single-scale observables; the leading violation needs the Lipatov vertex) and with Rothstein-Stewart, JHEP 08 (2016) 025 (Glauber EFT soft operator whose one-gluon rule is the Lipatov vertex). NOVELTY: the formulation is new; the substance is standard (BFKL).

CMP — comparison with the graph-by-graph argument of Collins-Soper-Sterman / Collins / Diehl-Gaunt-Ostermeier-Ploessl-Schaefer (NEW section). SOURCES READ: DGOPS, JHEP 01 (2016) 076, arXiv:1510.08696v2, §4 (pp. 38-52; single DY in §4.3.1 and §4.3.4); CSS review hep-ph/0409313 (Adv.Ser.Direct.HEP 5 (1989) 1), §8.4 and §10. NOT ACCESSED: CSS NPB 308 (1988) 833 (abstract only) and Collins 2011 §14.4. THE ARGUMENT (DGOPS §4, single DY). (1) Each graph is decomposed into region approximants with subtraction terms, and each term is integrated over the FULL range of loop momenta. So the soft approximations (soft minus components set to zero in B and H, i.e. E.19-type approximations) are applied globally, and the soft-collinear overlaps at the rapidity ends are removed by the subtraction terms of the larger regions (DGOPS steps 1 and 4). (2) For a given graph and region all cuts are summed. The collinear factor A is written in LCPT (light-cone time x^+), and the remainder R contains all soft propagators. R summed over its compatible cuts and integrated over the soft l^+ is independent of how the soft vertices on A are split between amplitude and conjugate (DGOPS §4.3.4, via LCPT in x^- and the cut sum). (3) The final-state factor of A summed over all cuts and integrated over k^- then equals 0 or 1 (light-cone Cutkosky identity, DGOPS eqs. (4.18)-(4.20)). So the final-state pinches in l_j^- disappear and all A-poles in l_j^- lie in the LHP; l_j^- is deformed into the UHP to |l_j^-| ~ |l_jT|. This assumes the soft momenta can be routed through S so that the poles of R stay at |l^-| >~ |l_T| (DGOPS step 3: 'It would be desirable to have a formal proof'). The CSS review §8.4 gives the same picture: kappa^+ and kappa^- each meet a single pole in the Glauber region and can be deformed to |kappa^+-| ~ |k_T|. (4) After the deformations, Grammer-Yennie approximations and Ward identities give Wilson lines, and for inclusive DY the soft factor is unity. The argument needs an observable that is constant over the cuts of the final-state factor; otherwise it fails, cf. Gaunt, JHEP 07 (2014) 110 for E_T. DIFFERENCES WITH APP E. The colour factor of each forward graph is fixed (the different cuts of one forward graph are different side assignments with the same colour factor), and the jets' insertions are never localized at equal times, so no ordering weights arise: the Glauber region is avoided by deformation, not cancelled by side sums. App E's I(pi_A, pi_B) are the colour-stripped covariant ladder/crossed graphs (DEFS); the ordering-weight issues arise only when both jets are localized and cancellation is sought by side sums inside the window. CONCLUSION. We identify NO gap in the graph-by-graph argument for the coincident patterns studied here. In particular, T3's residual is generated at the B-collinear end |k^-| ~ Q (T3(e)), a region that in the subtraction formalism is removed by the subtraction term of the region in which that gluon is B-collinear. Consequently every statement in this report that an E.33(c)-type assumption is 'needed' or 'not needed' is relative to App E's two-time localization framework, not a statement about QCD factorization. The graph-by-graph argument has its own stated inputs: the routing assumption of DGOPS step 3, the validity of LCPT (zero modes, instantaneous vertices), and the region/subtraction formalism. App E's framework shares these in other forms (G1, G3', G6).
