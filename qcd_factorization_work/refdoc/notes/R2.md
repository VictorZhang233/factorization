## PART I. Faithful account of the segment

### §4 "A 级结果 I：e⁺e⁻ 湮灭总截面" (pp. 28–32)

**Intro (p. 28).** The total cross section is the simplest "factorization" result. It has no non-perturbative
factor; all dependence is in the hard coefficient, and non-perturbative effects appear only as power
corrections. Its non-perturbative part presupposes that QCD exists as a Wightman QFT (Assumption C.56). The
statements on power corrections also presuppose a non-perturbative OPE.

**§4.1 Statement.**
$$\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q)=i\int d^4x\,e^{iq\cdot x}\langle0|T\,J^\mu(x)J^\nu(0)|0\rangle=(q^\mu q^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}q^2)\,\Pi(q^2)\tag{4.1}$$
(tensor structure from $\partial_\mu J^\mu=0$), and at lowest order in the e.m. coupling
$$R(s)=12\pi\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi(s+i0).\tag{4.2}$$

**Theorem 4.1 (e⁺e⁻ total cross section), $n_f$ massless quarks.**
- (i) *Analyticity.* $\Pi(q^2)$ is analytic in the $q^2$-plane minus the positive real axis, and ${\rm Im}\,\Pi(s+i0)\ge0$.
  If $\int_1^\infty ds\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi(s)/s^2<\infty$ (equivalently, the Adler function is finite in the Euclidean
  region), then for every Euclidean subtraction point $-Q_0^2<0$
  $$\Pi(q^2)-\Pi(-Q_0^2)=\frac{q^2+Q_0^2}{\pi}\int_0^\infty ds\,\frac{{\rm Im}\,\Pi(s)}{(s+Q_0^2)(s-q^2-i0)}.\tag{4.3}$$
  (The subtraction is Euclidean because $\Pi(0)$ is IR divergent for massless quarks.)
- (ii) *IR finiteness at every order of PT.* At each order $R(s)$ is finite and a polynomial in $\alpha_s(\mu)$ and
  $\ln(s/\mu^2)$. At $\mu^2=s$:
  $$R(s)=N_c\sum_q e_q^2\Big[1+\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}+(1.9857-0.1153\,n_f)\Big(\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}\Big)^2+O(\alpha_s^3)\Big],\quad \alpha_s=\alpha_s^{\overline{\rm MS}}(s).\tag{4.4}$$
- (iii) *Power corrections, assuming a non-perturbative OPE.* In the Euclidean region the Adler function
  $D(Q^2)\equiv-12\pi^2Q^2\,d\Pi(-Q^2)/dQ^2$ obeys
  $$D(Q^2)=D_{\rm pert}(Q^2)+\frac{C_G(\alpha_s)\langle\frac{\alpha_s}{\pi}G^2\rangle}{Q^4}+\sum_q\frac{C_q(\alpha_s)\,m_q\langle\bar qq\rangle}{Q^4}+O(Q^{-6}),\tag{4.5}$$
  with no $1/Q^2$ term. Light-quark masses are kept only to display the quark condensate. $D_{\rm pert}$ then also
  contains $m_q^2/Q^2$ terms. In the strict massless limit both the $m_q\langle\bar qq\rangle$ terms and these terms vanish.
- (iv) *Local duality in the averaged sense.* For every $\Delta>0$,
  $$\bar R(s,\Delta)\equiv\frac{\Delta}{\pi}\int_0^\infty ds'\,\frac{R(s')}{(s'-s)^2+\Delta^2}=12\pi\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi(s+i\Delta).\tag{4.6}$$
  If one further assumes that the OPE of (iii), together with the asymptotic perturbative expansion of its
  coefficients, also holds in the complex $q^2$-plane away from the cut (this does not follow from the Euclidean
  statement), then for $\Delta\gg\Lambda^2_{\rm QCD}$ the right side can be computed with PT plus the OPE.

**Proof structure.**
- (i) Insert a complete set of states and use translation invariance and the spectral condition to define
  $(2\pi)^3\sum_n\delta^4(q-p_n)\langle0|J^\mu(0)|n\rangle\langle n|J^\nu(0)|0\rangle=(q^\mu q^\nu-g^{\mu\nu}q^2)\rho(q^2)\theta(q^0)$.
  Then $\Pi=\int ds\,\rho(s)/(s-q^2-i0)$ (plus subtractions) and ${\rm Im}\,\Pi=\pi\rho$. This assumes Wightman
  QCD (C.56). Contract with $-g_{\mu\nu}$. In the rest frame of $q$ the left side is $\sum|\langle0|\mathbf J|n\rangle|^2\ge0$
  (current conservation kills $J^0$) and the right side is $3q^2\rho$. Hence $\rho\ge0$, supported on $s\ge0$, and
  $\Pi$ is analytic as a Stieltjes-type integral. The axioms only give a tempered positive measure, hence finitely
  many subtractions. One subtraction converges iff $\int_1^\infty\rho/s^2<\infty$. In PT, $\rho$ grows like powers of $\ln s$.
  Non-perturbatively the condition follows from finiteness of $D$ at one Euclidean point, because
  $(s+Q^2)^{-2}\ge(2s)^{-2}$ for $s\ge Q^2$ and $\rho\ge0$.
- (ii) At $q^2=-Q^2<0$, Cor. 3.4 says no Feynman graph of $\Pi$ has a pinch on the real contour, so there are no IR
  divergences. Endpoint divergences are absent because zero-momentum lines end on off-shell lines. Strictly, what
  is used is the Euclidean IR power-counting theorem for massless integrals at non-exceptional momenta [80]
  Lowenstein–Zimmermann 1975, [81] Lowenstein 1976. UV divergences are removed by the constant subtraction of
  $\Pi$ plus coupling renormalization. Hence $D$ depends only on $Q^2/\mu^2$ and $\alpha_s(\mu)$, is independent of
  the subtraction constant, and obeys $(\mu\partial_\mu+\beta\partial_{\alpha_s})D=0$. Write $D=\sum_na_n(L)\alpha_s^n$
  with $L=\ln(Q^2/\mu^2)$. The RG fixes $\partial a_n/\partial L$ in terms of lower orders, so $a_n$ is a polynomial
  of degree $n-1$. Integrating, the order-$n$ coefficient of $\Pi(-Q^2)$ is a polynomial of degree $n$ in $L$ (plus a
  constant). Continue $-Q^2\to s+i0$, i.e. $L\to\ln(s/\mu^2)-i\pi$. The imaginary part is again a finite
  polynomial. The coefficients in (4.4) come from explicit calculation [25–27] (Chetyrkin–Kataev–Tkachov 1979;
  Dine–Sapirstein 1979; Celmaster–Gonsalves 1980). The $O(\alpha_s^2)$ cuts are shown in Fig. 18.
- (iii) Assume Assumptions C.56–C.58 (plus C.59 for asymptotic coefficient series; Remark C.62). Wilson OPE
  $TJ^\mu(x)J^\nu(0)=\sum_iC_i^{\mu\nu}(x)O_i(0)$ with $C_i\sim x^{d_i-6}$. Only colour-singlet Lorentz scalars have
  vacuum expectation values. Unit operator: $d=0$, perturbative, and with masses it carries $m_q^2/Q^2$. There is
  no gauge-invariant $d=2$ operator: $A_\mu A^\mu$ is not invariant, and $\bar qq$ has $d=3$ and enters only as
  $m_q\bar qq$ in the chiral limit. Next come $d=4$: $\alpha_sG^2$ and $m_q\bar qq$. So corrections start at $1/Q^4$.
- (iv) From (4.3), $\Pi(z)-\Pi(-Q_0^2)=\frac1\pi\int ds'\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi(s')[\frac1{s'-z}-\frac1{s'+Q_0^2}]$ and
  ${\rm Im}\frac1{s'-s-i\Delta}=\frac{\Delta}{(s'-s)^2+\Delta^2}$.

**§4.2 Direct KLN form and cut sums (p. 31).** By Cutkosky, $2\,{\rm Im}\,\Pi=\sum_C\Pi_C$. One-loop numbers are
given in units $\sigma_0C_F\alpha_s/(2\pi)$, with a common normalization factor $\to1$ dropped (convention of [33] ESW):
virtual (b) $=-2/\epsilon^2-3/\epsilon-8+\pi^2$ (the poles are the on-shell version of Prop. 3.5); real (c)
$=+2/\epsilon^2+3/\epsilon+19/2-\pi^2$. The sum is $3/2$, i.e. $\sigma_0\alpha_s/\pi$ for $C_F=4/3$.

**Proposition 4.2 (IR finiteness of the cut sum).** In the e⁺e⁻ total cross section, for a fixed uncut graph
$G$, the leading singular contributions of $\sum_CG_C$ near any pinch surface (jets or soft subgraph) cancel.
*Proof sketch.* Take a pinch surface $R$ with final-state jets $J_i$ and soft subgraph $S$ attached to $H$ in the
amplitude and $H^*$ in the conjugate. Near $R$, cuts pass only through jets and $S$; a cut through $H$ gives a final
state off $R$. At fixed hard parts, $\sum_C(J\otimes S)_C$ is the sum over all intermediate states of $J\otimes S$. By
unitarity $\sum_X|X\rangle\langle X|=1$ it equals the discontinuity of the unrestricted time-ordered ("forward")
matrix element in the total energy entering $J\otimes S$ along $q$. In that function, on-shell $\delta$'s are
replaced by $i0$ propagators, so it is analytic in the energy. At complex points $O(Q)$ off the real axis the
contours can be deformed away from the pinch (Fig. 19). The precise form is the largest-time equation or the
energy-denominator sums of light-cone-ordered PT [7] Sterman 1978, [1] Collins 2011.
The text then says explicitly that this is only a sketch: it explains the mechanism but gives no estimates in the
pinch neighbourhood. For the total cross section, Thm. 4.1(ii) already gives IR finiteness without it. In App. D
the "integrand after cut summation" representation and its bounds are Assumption D.27. In LCOPT the identity
summing over cut positions (largest-time pairing) is proven in Lemmas E.24, E.26 under Assumption E.18.

**Fig. 19 (p. 31).** (a) Near $R$, cuts $C,C',C''$ cross only $J_1,J_2,S$ (red $H,H^*$, blue jets, green soft).
(b) "Unitarity": the sum over these cuts is the Disc of the uncut forward combination (grey box). Inside it the
$\delta$'s become $i0$ propagators, analytic in the total energy, and the contour can be moved off the pinch at
distance $O(Q)$ from the real axis.

**Remark 4.3 (p. 32).** The essence is that the total cross section is completely inclusive in the final state, so
the final-state sum is removed by unitarity. All "soft factor = 1" arguments (§8, §9) are variants of this. For
Drell–Yan the Glauber region must be handled first, and App. E proves this only conditionally. If the measurement
restricts the final state (e.g. a rapidity gap), the argument fails. The text calls this the source of
non-global logarithms and of some factorization breaking.

**§4.3 Limits of rigour (p. 32).** The equalities in (i) and (iv) use only the Wightman axioms (spectral condition,
positivity) and the growth condition for one subtraction. They are non-perturbative theorems, conditional on "QCD
exists as a Wightman theory" (C.56; 4D QCD has not been constructed). Computing the right side of (iv) also needs
(iii) and the OPE off the cut. (ii) is a theorem at every order of PT. (iii) needs a non-perturbative OPE, which is
widely accepted but unproven: App. C states it as C.56–C.58, and the asymptotic coefficient expansion needs C.59
(vacuum case: Remark C.62). The OPE is itself asymptotic, and App. C concerns only Euclidean momenta. Its validity
at complex $q^2$ is an extra assumption here. On the cut, $R-{\rm OPE}$ ("duality violation") can be exponentially
small but oscillating, "e.g. $e^{-c\sqrt s/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}\sin(\dots)$", and is invisible in the Euclidean region.
So pointwise comparison needs the extra assumption of local quark–hadron duality, while the average (4.6) does not
[28] PQW 1976, [29] Shifman 2000.

### §5 "A 级结果 II：DIS 结构函数的算符乘积展开" (pp. 32–39)

**Intro.** DIS was the first process "proven" to factorize, by Wilson's OPE plus dispersion relations
[20–23] (Christ–Hasslacher–Mueller; Gross–Wilczek I/II; Georgi–Politzer). Advantage: besides the OPE, only
analyticity, positivity ($F_2\ge0$) and Lorentz covariance are used. Disadvantage: only moments, and no extension
to processes such as Drell–Yan. App. C handles the non-perturbative status precisely, summarized in Remark 5.7.

**§5.1 Analyticity of the forward Compton amplitude.** $\omega=1/x=2P\cdot q/Q^2$, with $T_{1,2}$ of (2.9) as
functions of $\omega$ at fixed $Q^2>0$.

**Lemma 5.1 (analytic structure and crossing).** $T_1(\omega),T_2(\omega)$ are analytic in the $\omega$-plane except
for cuts $\omega\ge1$ and $\omega\le-1$ on the real axis (and the elastic point). $T_1$ is even and $T_2$ is odd. On the cut
$$T_i(\omega+i0)-T_i(\omega-i0)=2i\,{\rm Im}\,T_i(\omega+i0)=4\pi i\,F_i(x=1/\omega),\qquad\omega>1.\tag{5.1}$$
*Proof.* Insert states. The term $\theta(z^0)\langle P|J(z)|n\rangle\langle n|J(0)|P\rangle$ contributes to the
imaginary part only for $(P+q)^2=p_n^2\ge M^2$, i.e. $M^2+Q^2(\omega-1)\ge M^2$, so $\omega\ge1$. The crossed term
needs $(P-q)^2\ge M^2$, so $\omega\le-1$. Elsewhere $T$ is analytic as a Cauchy-type integral. For $\omega>1$ only the
direct term contributes and ${\rm Im}\,T^{\mu\nu}=2\pi W^{\mu\nu}$. Crossing: $T^{\mu\nu}(P,q)=T^{\nu\mu}(P,-q)$. Under
$q\to-q$, $\omega\to-\omega$ and $\hat P^\mu\hat P^\nu/(P\cdot q)$ changes sign, so $T_2$ is odd and $T_1$ even.

**Fig. 20 (p. 33).** The $\omega$-plane: physical cut $\omega\ge1$ ($0<x\le1$) and crossed cut $\omega\le-1$, a small
counter-clockwise contour $C_0$ around the origin, deformed to the large circle $C_\infty$ (drawn as a dashed upper
half-circle) plus contours wrapping the cuts (upper lip to the right, lower lip to the left). $C_\infty$ vanishes for
$n\ge n_0$ (Lemma C.64). For $n<n_0$, including $n=2$, Assumption C.60 is needed. The moment theorem needs single
Taylor coefficients only, not convergence of the spin sum (Remark C.2).

**§5.2 OPE and twist expansion.** For $Q^2\to\infty$ at fixed $|\omega|<1$ the integral in (2.9) is dominated by
$z^2\to0$. No final state is on shell, so a local expansion applies:
$$i\!\int\! d^4z\,e^{iqz}TJ^\mu(z)J^\nu(0)=\sum_{n=2,4,\dots}\sum_i\Big[\Big(-g^{\mu\nu}+\frac{q^\mu q^\nu}{q^2}\Big)\frac{2^nq_{\mu_1}\cdots q_{\mu_n}}{(Q^2)^n}C^{(n)}_{1,i}
+\Big(g^\mu{}_{\mu_1}-\frac{q^\mu q_{\mu_1}}{q^2}\Big)\Big(g^\nu{}_{\mu_2}-\frac{q^\nu q_{\mu_2}}{q^2}\Big)\frac{2^nq_{\mu_3}\cdots q_{\mu_n}}{(Q^2)^{n-1}}C^{(n)}_{2,i}\Big]O_i^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}+\cdots\tag{5.2}$$
$$O_q^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}=\bar\psi_q\gamma^{\{\mu_1}iD^{\mu_2}\cdots iD^{\mu_n\}}\psi_q-\text{traces},\qquad
O_g^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}=F^{a\{\mu_1\alpha}(iD^{\mu_2}\cdots iD^{\mu_{n-1}})_{ab}F_\alpha^{b\,\mu_n\}}-\text{traces}\tag{5.3–5.4}$$
($\{\}$ = normalized symmetrization; "$\cdots$" = twist $\ge4$.) Twist $\tau=d-n$. A twist-$\tau$ operator is
suppressed by $(\Lambda^2/Q^2)^{(\tau-2)/2}$. For e.m. currents, charge conjugation leaves only even $n$ (for $q+\bar q$).
$$\langle P|O_i^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}|P\rangle=2A_i^{(n)}(\mu)\,(P^{\mu_1}\cdots P^{\mu_n}-\text{traces}).\tag{5.5}$$
Use $q_\mu P^\mu=\omega Q^2/2$ and $(g^\mu{}_{\mu_1}-\cdots)P^{\mu_1}=\hat P^\mu$, and drop traces (target-mass corrections
$M^2/Q^2$). This gives $T_1=2\sum_n\omega^n\sum_iC^{(n)}_{1,i}A^{(n)}_i$ and, for $|\omega|<1$,
$$T_2(\omega,Q^2)=4\sum_{n=2,4,\dots}\omega^{n-1}\sum_iC^{(n)}_{2,i}(Q^2/\mu^2,\alpha_s(\mu))A^{(n)}_i(\mu)+O(1/Q^2).\tag{5.6}$$
The sum over $n$ and the limit $Q^2\to\infty$ have been interchanged, so (5.6) is a formal series. Its rigorous
content is coefficient by coefficient (Thm. C.50), and that is all the moment theorem needs (Remark C.2).

**Example 5.2 (tree-level OPE, p. 34).** Handbag with quark momentum $k$:
$\frac{i(\slashed k+\slashed q)}{(k+q)^2+i0}=-\frac{i(\slashed k+\slashed q)}{Q^2}\sum_{m\ge0}\big(\frac{2k\cdot q+k^2}{Q^2}\big)^m$.
For $k=\xi P$, $2k\cdot q/Q^2=\xi\omega$, and the series converges for $|\omega|<1$ because $|\xi|\le1$ (Prop. 2.4). Each
$(2k\cdot q)^m$ gives $m$ covariant derivatives. The text says "the $\slashed q$ in $\slashed k+\slashed q$ combines with the
vertex $\gamma$'s into $\gamma^{\mu_1}$" (imprecise, see ERRORS E3). For a quark target $A^{(n)}=1$, and handbag plus
crossed graph give $T_2=4e_q^2\sum_{n\ \rm even}\omega^{n-1}=4e_q^2\,\omega/(1-\omega^2)$. Near $\omega=1$ this is
$\frac12/(1-\omega)$ times $4e_q^2$, with discontinuity $\pi i\,\delta(\omega-1)$ per unit, which reproduces
$F_2=e_q^2x\,\delta(1-x)$ through (5.1). Hence $C^{(n)}_{2,q}=e_q^2+O(\alpha_s)$. [Verified, `check_R2.py`.]

**§5.3 Theorem 5.3 (moment factorization of DIS, conditional).** Assume the OPE (5.2) holds non-perturbatively in
the precise sense of C.56–C.59. (In PT, the OPE at Euclidean non-exceptional momenta is a literature theorem [18]
Zimmermann 1973; for massless QCD, Thm. C.35; App. C rebuilds it given the momentum-space remainder estimate
C.24.) Then there is a $Q$-independent even $n_0$ such that for every even $n\ge n_0$
$$M_2^{(n)}(Q^2)\equiv\int_0^1dx\,x^{n-2}F_2(x,Q^2)=\sum_{i=q,g}C^{(n)}_{2,i}(Q^2/\mu^2,\alpha_s(\mu))A^{(n)}_i(\mu)+O(\Lambda^2_{\rm QCD}/Q^2).\tag{5.7}$$
Here $\Lambda^2$ stands for $M^2$, $mM$ (chiral-odd higher twist), and twist-4 matrix elements, and the remainder may
carry $\ln^kQ$ (Thm. C.66(ii)). With Assumption C.60 (growth of $T_2$ on the imaginary axis), (5.7) holds for all
even $n\ge2$, including the momentum sum rule $n=2$. Under C.57(c):
$$A^{(n)}_q(\mu)=\int_{-1}^1d\xi\,\xi^{n-1}f_{q/A}(\xi,\mu)=\int_0^1d\xi\,\xi^{n-1}[f_{q/A}+(-1)^nf_{\bar q/A}],\qquad A^{(n)}_g=\int_0^1d\xi\,\xi^{n-1}f_{g/A}.\tag{5.8}$$

*"Argument" (rigorous version: Thm. C.66).* The text flags two places where it is cruder than the rigorous proof:
(5.6) is used as a convergent series, and the large circle is dropped. *Moments:* the Taylor coefficient is
$\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{C_0}\frac{d\omega}{\omega^n}T_2=4\sum_iC^{(n)}_{2,i}A^{(n)}_i$. Deform $C_0$ to the cuts. For
$n\ge n_0$, $T_2/\omega^n$ decays and the circle vanishes. For $n<n_0$, in particular $n=2$, Assumption C.60 is
needed: Regge behaviour of $F_2$ at small $x$ only guarantees $\int F_2dx<\infty$, which does not exclude
fixed-pole-type terms invisible in $F_2$ (Remark C.65). The right cut gives $\frac1{2\pi i}\int_1^\infty d\omega\,\omega^{-n}4\pi iF_2$,
and the left cut gives the same by oddness and even $n$. So $4\int_1^\infty d\omega\,\omega^{-n}F_2=4\int_0^1x^{n-2}F_2=4\sum C^{(n)}_{2,i}A^{(n)}_i$.
Twist 4 gives $O(1/Q^2)$. *Moments vs. PDFs:* contract (5.5) with $\bar n_{\mu_1}\cdots\bar n_{\mu_n}$ (traces vanish
because $\bar n^2=0$): $\langle P|\bar\psi\gamma^+(iD^+)^{n-1}\psi|P\rangle=2A^{(n)}_q(P^+)^n$. In (2.11) use

Integrate by parts with $\partial_{w^-}W[w^-,0]=igA^+(w)W[w^-,0]$, so that
$\partial_{w^-}(\bar\psi W)=(\partial^+\bar\psi+ig\bar\psi A^+)W\equiv(\bar\psi\overleftarrow D^+)W$. This gives
$\int d\xi\,\xi^{n-1}f_{q/A}=\frac1{4\pi}\frac{2\pi}{P^+}\frac1{(P^+)^{n-1}}\langle P|\bar\psi(-i\overleftarrow D^+)^{n-1}\gamma^+\psi|P\rangle=\frac1{2(P^+)^n}\langle P|\bar\psi\gamma^+(iD^+)^{n-1}\psi|P\rangle$,
using that total derivatives vanish in forward matrix elements. Comparison gives (5.8); the $\xi$-range is
$[-1,1]$ by Prop. 2.4. The interchange of $\partial_{w^-}$ with the hadronic matrix element, and the existence of the
light-ray operator between hadron states, are non-perturbatively Assumption C.57(c). Renormalization: both sides
use the same scheme, and MS renormalization of the local operators "induces" that of the PDF (§5.4).

**§5.4 RG and DGLAP.**
$$\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}A^{(n)}_i=-\sum_j\gamma^{(n)}_{ij}(\alpha_s)A^{(n)}_j,\qquad\Big(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha_s}\Big)C^{(n)}_{2,i}=\sum_jC^{(n)}_{2,j}\gamma^{(n)}_{ji},\tag{5.9}$$
the second following from $\mu$-independence of $M_2^{(n)}$.

**Proposition 5.4 (DGLAP is the Mellin inverse of the OPE RG).** Define $\gamma^{(n)}_{ij}(\alpha_s)=-2\int_0^1dz\,z^{n-1}P_{ij}(z,\alpha_s)$. Then (5.9) is equivalent to
$$\mu^2\frac{d}{d\mu^2}f_{i/A}(x,\mu)=\sum_j\int_x^1\frac{dz}{z}P_{ij}(z,\alpha_s(\mu))f_{j/A}(x/z,\mu).\tag{5.10}$$
At one loop $P_{ij}=\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}P^{(0)}_{ij}$ with
$$P^{(0)}_{qq}=C_F\Big[\frac{1+z^2}{(1-z)_+}+\frac32\delta(1-z)\Big],\qquad\int_0^1dz\,z^{n-1}P^{(0)}_{qq}=C_F\Big[\frac32+\frac1{n(n+1)}-2\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1k\Big].\tag{5.11–5.12}$$
*Proof.* Take $\int_0^1dx\,x^{n-1}$ of (5.10). The right side becomes $\sum_j[\int z^{n-1}P_{ij}]A^{(n)}_j$, the left side
$\mu^2dA/d\mu^2=\frac12\mu dA/d\mu$. Compare with (5.9). The converse uses moment uniqueness (Lemma 5.5). (5.9) must be
read for all $n$: even $n$ fix the evolution of the C-even $f_q+f_{\bar q}$ (and $f_g$). $f_q-f_{\bar q}$ needs odd-$n$
operators, which are absent from the e.m. OPE "but whose renormalization defines $\gamma^{(n)}$ in the same way". At
small $x$ low moments may diverge, and then the equivalence is stated for $n\ge n_1$, with uniqueness applied to
$x^{n_1-1}f$. Moments of (5.11): $\int_0^1z^{n-1}\frac{1+z^2}{(1-z)_+}dz=\int_0^1\frac{z^{n-1}+z^{n+1}-2}{1-z}dz=-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac1k-\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\frac1k$.
$n=1$ gives zero (quark number) and $n=2$ gives $-\frac43C_F$ (momentum fraction). [All verified.]

**Lemma 5.5 (moment uniqueness).** Let $F$ have $\int_0^1|F|dx<\infty$. If $\int_0^1x^{n-2}F\,dx=0$ for all even

**Theorem 5.6 (x-space DIS factorization from the OPE, weak form, p. 38).** Assume Thm. 5.3 and
(Φ): there exist distribution-valued coefficient functions $C_{2,i}(z,Q^2/\mu^2,\alpha_s)$, depending only on the
parton type, such that $x\times$ (main term below) is a finite signed measure $\Phi_Q$ on $[0,1]$ whose even moments
$\int x^{n-2}d\Phi_Q$ ($n\ge n_0$) equal $\sum_iC^{(n)}_{2,i}A^{(n)}_i$. (This is condition (Φ) of Cor. C.52. In PT the
$C_{2,i}$ exist only order by order, so with truncation at $\alpha_s^K$, (Φ) holds up to $O(\alpha_s(Q)^{K+1})$.) Then
$$\frac{F_2(x,Q^2)}{x}=\sum_i\int_x^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi}C_{2,i}\Big(\frac x\xi,\frac{Q^2}{\mu^2},\alpha_s(\mu)\Big)f_{i/A}(\xi,\mu)+O\Big(\frac{\Lambda^2}{Q^2}\Big),\tag{5.13}$$
with the remainder holding only weakly. Viewing both sides times $x$ as measures, their difference integrated against
each $x^{n-2}$ ($n\ge n_0$ even) is $O(\Lambda^2/Q^2)$ up to $\ln^kQ$. With the uniform boundedness condition of
Cor. C.52(ii), it tends to zero against all $x^{n_0-2}\varphi(x)$, $\varphi$ continuous. (5.13) is not a pointwise or
uniform estimate. *Proof:* $C^{(n)}_{2,i}$ is target independent. Take a single parton $j$ in DR as target and define
$C_{2,i}(z)$ by $F_2^{(j)}/x=\sum_iC_{2,i}\otimes f_{i/j}$ (invertible because $f_{i/j}=\delta_{ij}\delta(1-\xi)+O(\alpha_s)$).
By the Mellin convolution theorem its even moments are $C^{(n)}_{2,i}$. At fixed order this needs the partonic OPE
to equal the moments of partonic structure functions (Prop. C.54 and its conditions). For hadrons, (Φ) and Thm. 5.3
give the first weak statement. Lemma 5.5 (and its measure version, Lemma C.51) needs exact equality of moments.
Remainders are not uniform in $n$, so pointwise $O(1/Q^2)$ does not follow; Cor. C.52 instead gives weak convergence
via Stone–Weierstrass.

**Remark 5.7 (what the proof does and does not prove).** (a) The OPE gives a power expansion per moment, and the
$O(1/Q^2)$ coefficient grows with $n$ (relative size $\sim nM^2/Q^2$; §14). Heuristically the x-space remainder is
$O(1/(Q^2(1-x)))$ rather than uniformly $O(1/Q^2)$, and factorization fails in the resonance region
$W^2=Q^2(1-x)/x\sim\Lambda^2$. The rigorous x-space statement is weak convergence only. (b) The OPE is not proven
non-perturbatively, and cannot be unconditionally at present. App. C takes the Zimmermann–Hepp forest-formula
convergence theorem as input, proves the PT OPE identity, the uniqueness and scaling of the coefficients, and makes
the momentum-space remainder estimate an explicit input (C.24). Non-perturbatively, it proves Thm. 5.3 (Thm. C.66)
under four groups of assumptions (Wightman-type existence, composite operators/RG, non-perturbative OPE, asymptotic
freedom), and it shows that fixed-$n$ moments do not need the spin sum (Remark C.2). Zimmermann proved the OPE of
renormalized composite operators with BPHZ [18]. The spin-summed light-cone expansion [19] at fixed order is
Prop. C.54, which requires a remainder estimate in forward on-shell kinematics that has no complete all-order proof
(Remark C.37). (c) The method does not extend to DY (two hadrons, no forward $\langle P|\cdots|P\rangle$) or to
$e^+e^-\to hX$ (an observed hadron is not a local-operator expectation value). Graphical methods are needed there.

**§6.1 Starting from the forward amplitude.** Prove factorization for $T^{\mu\nu}$ in the unphysical region

**Lemma 6.1 (factorized structure of a leading region).** For a leading region $R$ of a graph $\Gamma$ of
$T^{\mu\nu}$ with reduced graph $J_R,H_R$, near $R$:
$$\Gamma\simeq T_R\Gamma\equiv\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\,\frac14{\rm Tr}\big[H_R^{\mu\nu}(\hat k)\gamma^-\big]\,{\rm Tr}\big[\gamma^+J_R(k)\big]+(\text{longitudinal-gluon terms}),\qquad\hat k=(k^+,0,0_T).\tag{6.1}$$
*Proof.* By Cor. 3.8, $J_R$ couples to $H_R$ through a quark pair (or a transverse gluon pair, §6.4) plus any number
of longitudinal gluons. Replacing $k\to\hat k$ in $H_R$ costs $O(k^-/k^+,k_T/k^+)=O(\lambda)$. Dirac structure:
$J_R=\frac14\sum_\Gamma\Gamma\,{\rm Tr}[\Gamma'J_R]$ in a Clifford basis with dual basis $\Gamma'$. By the boost argument of
Lemma 3.7 the largest component is $\propto\gamma^-$ (scaling $\lambda^{-1}$), with dual $\gamma^+$ (${\rm Tr}[\gamma^+\gamma^-]=4$):
$\frac14\gamma^-{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+J_R]$. The others are suppressed by $\lambda$ (scaling statement; pointwise it is
D.7(A3)(iii)). The equally leading $\gamma^-\gamma_5$ vanishes for unpolarized targets by parity. $\sigma^{-j}$ can only be
$\propto k_T^j$ and integrates to zero because $H_R(\hat k)$ is $k_T$-independent. So ${\rm Tr}[HJ]\simeq\frac14{\rm Tr}[H\gamma^-]{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+J]$.

**Proposition 6.2 (factorization for a single region).** Summing the same class of leading regions over all graphs
and using Thm. 3.14 for longitudinal gluons:
$$\sum_\Gamma T_R\Gamma=\int_{-1}^1\frac{d\xi}{\xi}\,\hat H^{\mu\nu}_q(\xi P,q)\,f_{q/A}(\xi),\qquad\hat H^{\mu\nu}_q(\xi P,q)=\tfrac12{\rm Tr}[H^{\mu\nu}(\xi P)\,\xi P^+\gamma^-],\tag{6.2}$$
the amputated hard amplitude for an on-shell massless quark of momentum $\xi P$ (spin averaged), with $f_{q/A}$ from (2.11).
*Proof.* $H_R(\hat k)$ depends only on $k^+=\xi P^+$, and $\int d^4k=P^+\int d\xi\int dk^-d^2k_T$, the latter acting only on
$J_R$. By Thm. 3.14 (Fig. 23), the longitudinal gluons attached to $H_R$ in all ways reattach to a Wilson line along

$\int\frac{dk^-d^2k_T}{(2\pi)^4}{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+J(k)]=\int\frac{dw^-}{2\pi}e^{-ik^+w^-}\langle P|\bar\psi(0,w^-,0_T)\gamma^+W[w^-,0]\psi(0)|P\rangle_{\rm conn}=2f_{q/A}(\xi)$.

**§6.2 Overlapping regions and the subtraction method (pp. 40–43).** A graph has many nested leading regions. For

**Theorem 6.3 (subtraction formula, Collins).** Assume the contour condition (D.7 (A1)(a)) and (H1)–(H3). Then
$$\Gamma=\sum_RC_R\Gamma+O(\lambda^aL^K)\times(\text{leading power}),\qquad L\equiv\ln(1/\lambda),\tag{6.4}$$
with the error suppressed uniformly over the whole domain in an integral norm (Thm. D.10). For general graphs it is
conditional (D.14).
*Proof idea.* Let $r=\Gamma-\sum_RC_R\Gamma$. Fix $R_0$ and split the other regions into $R<R_0$ and $R\not\le R_0$. With
$X_{R_0}=\Gamma-\sum_{R<R_0}C_R\Gamma$, we have $C_{R_0}\Gamma=T_{R_0}X_{R_0}$ and pointwise
$$r=(1-T_{R_0})X_{R_0}-\sum_{R\not\le R_0}C_R\Gamma.$$
On the $R_0$-dominated set $U_{R_0}$ the first term is small by (H1) and the second by (H2); (H3) gives integrability.
The $U_{R_0}$ cover the domain (Lemma D.6), so $r$ is small everywhere. To verify (H1)(H2) on concrete graphs one
unfolds the recursion into chains $R_1>R_2>\cdots$ (like BPHZ forests) and groups chains by "contains $R_0$ or not"
and by $R\wedge R_0$ (Lemmas D.11–D.12, Cor. D.13). Single chain terms are generally non-integrable, so estimates
apply only to the grouped expressions.

**Remark 6.4 (why the naive termwise argument fails).** The common "proof" by induction on $R_0$ asserts that the
subtraction $C_R\Gamma$ of an incomparable $R$ is suppressed in $N_{R_0}$, and that $T_R$ preserves suppression. Both fail
term by term. In the one-loop Sudakov triangle (Prop. 3.5) the collinear regions $C_1,C_2$ are incomparable, yet
$T_{C_2}\Gamma$ is leading in $C_1$; only $C_{C_2}\Gamma=T_{C_2}(\Gamma-T_S\Gamma)$ is suppressed (Example D.1). $T_R$ is not pointwise
(it evaluates factors elsewhere), so suppression cannot be passed through it. Requirements belong on
$(1-T_{R_0})X_{R_0}$ and $C_R\Gamma$ only.

**Remark 6.5 (inputs process by process).** For DIS, $T_R$ is $k\to\hat k$ in $H_R$ plus the Dirac projection and the
longitudinal-gluon projection (3.10), with relative error $O(k_T/k^+,k^-/k^+)$. App. D lists all error terms for
hard–collinear, soft–collinear, TMD and exclusive approximations and proves their scaling under (A3)(A4) (Lemma D.25).
It lists the inputs for DIS (forward amplitude and cut graphs, Prop. D.28), $e^+e^-\to hX$ (D.29), thrust, DY

**§6.4 Gluon distribution and longitudinal gluons (p. 45).** When $J$ connects to $H$ via two transverse gluons
($(n_F,n_T)=(0,2)$ of Cor. 3.8), the same holds with the Dirac projection replaced by

$A^+=0$ gauge $F^{+j}=\partial^+A^j$). Each $F^{+j}$ carries $k^+=\xi P^+$. The $1/(\xi P^+)$ in (2.12) cancels one, and the
remaining $k^+$ is the boson number-density normalization.
**Remark 6.8 (light-cone gauge).** In $A^+=0$ there are no longitudinal gluons and seemingly no need for Ward
identities. But the propagator $-i[g^{\mu\nu}-(\bar n^\mu k^\nu+k^\mu\bar n^\nu)/k^+]/k^2$ has an unphysical pole at $k^+=0$,
which produces extra (rapidity) divergences in TMD factorization and soft analysis. Choosing its prescription is
equivalent to choosing Wilson lines to $\pm\infty$. So Collins proves in covariant gauge and then writes the
gauge-invariant Wilson-line form [1].

**§7.1 Quark-in-quark PDF.** Target: on-shell massless quark $p=(p^+,0,0_T)$, spin and colour averaged;

**Proposition 7.1 (one-loop quark PDF).** The real part in $d$ dimensions is
$$f^{(1),{\rm real}}_{q/q}(\xi)=\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{2\pi}\Big[\frac{1+\xi^2}{1-\xi}-\epsilon(1-\xi)\Big]\frac{(4\pi\mu^2)^\epsilon}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_0^\infty\frac{dk_T^2}{(k_T^2)^{1+\epsilon}}\,\theta(0<\xi<1),\tag{7.2}$$
with $(1-\epsilon)(1-\xi)$ from (a) and $2\xi/(1-\xi)$ from (b)+(c). The virtual part gives the $\delta(1-\xi)$ term, fixed by
quark-number conservation $\int_0^1f_{q/q}=1$. In pure DR the $k_T$ integral is scaleless and vanishes, with
$\int dk_T^2/(k_T^2)^{1+\epsilon}=1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}-1/\epsilon_{\rm IR}$. After $\overline{\rm MS}$ removes the UV pole,
$$f_{q/q}(\xi,\mu)=\delta(1-\xi)-\frac{a_s}{\hat\epsilon_{\rm IR}}P^{(0)}_{qq}(\xi)+O(\alpha_s^2),\qquad Z_{qq}=\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{a_s}{\hat\epsilon}P^{(0)}_{qq}(\xi)\ \ (f^{\rm bare}=Z\otimes f(\mu)).\tag{7.3}$$
*Proof.* Kinematics: $p-k=((1-\xi)p^+,-k^-,-k_T)$ on shell gives $k^-=-k_T^2/(2(1-\xi)p^+)$,
$k^2=2\xi p^+k^--k_T^2=-k_T^2/(1-\xi)$, and $\int dk^-2\pi\delta((p-k)^2)=\frac{2\pi}{2(1-\xi)p^+}\theta(0<\xi<1)$.
Graph (a): $\frac12\cdot\frac12{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+\slashed k\gamma^\mu\slashed p\gamma_\mu\slashed k]=-\frac{d-2}4{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+(2(p\cdot k)\slashed k-k^2\slashed p)]=-(d-2)k^2(k^+-p^+)=(2-2\epsilon)k^2p^+(1-\xi)$
(using $\gamma^\mu\slashed p\gamma_\mu=-(d-2)\slashed p$ and $2p\cdot k=k^2$). Dividing by $(k^2)^2$ and including phase space gives
$\propto(1-\epsilon)(1-\xi)/k_T^2$. Graph (b): Wilson vertex plus eikonal propagator give $\bar n^\mu/((1-\xi)p^+)$, and
$\frac12\cdot\frac12{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+\slashed k\gamma^+\slashed p]=\frac14\cdot2k^+{\rm Tr}[\gamma^+\slashed p]=2\xi(p^+)^2$, giving
$\propto\xi/((1-\xi)k_T^2)$; (c) = (b). Normalization: $C_F$, $g^2\mu^{2\epsilon}$, and
$\int\frac{d^{2-2\epsilon}k_T}{(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}}=\frac{\pi^{1-\epsilon}}{(2\pi)^{3-2\epsilon}\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int dk_T^2(k_T^2)^{-\epsilon}$ combine to
$\frac{\alpha_sC_F}{2\pi}\frac{(4\pi\mu^2)^\epsilon}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}$, with $\frac{(4\pi)^\epsilon}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\frac1\epsilon=\frac1{\hat\epsilon}+O(\epsilon)$. Sum:
$(1-\epsilon)(1-\xi)+\frac{2\xi}{1-\xi}=\frac{1+\xi^2}{1-\xi}-\epsilon(1-\xi)$; (d) $\propto\bar n^\mu\bar n_\mu=0$. Virtual: (e) does not
cross the cut, so its $k^+$ integral forces $\xi=1$. With (f) it is scaleless in pure DR. Their role is to make
$1/(1-\xi)$ a plus distribution and add $\frac32\delta(1-\xi)$, uniquely fixed by vector-current conservation because
$\int_0^1d\xi[\frac{1+\xi^2}{(1-\xi)_+}+\frac32\delta(1-\xi)]=0$. Renormalization: split the $k_T$ integral at $\mu$;
$k_T>\mu$ gives $\mu^{-2\epsilon}/\epsilon_{\rm UV}$ ($\epsilon>0$) and $k_T<\mu$ gives $-\mu^{-2\epsilon}/\epsilon_{\rm IR}$ ($\epsilon<0$);
$\overline{\rm MS}$ removes $a_sP/\hat\epsilon_{\rm UV}$. [All intermediate identities verified, including 4D numerical traces.]

**Corollary 7.2 (DGLAP kernel from operator UV divergence).** From $f^{\rm bare}=Z\otimes f(\mu)$,
$\mu\,df^{\rm bare}/d\mu=0$ and $\mu\,d\alpha_s/d\mu=-2\epsilon\alpha_s+O(\alpha_s^2)$ (so $\mu\,dZ/d\mu=-2a_sP^{(0)}_{qq}+O(\alpha_s^2)$):
$\mu^2\frac{d}{d\mu^2}f=a_sP^{(0)}_{qq}\otimes f+O(\alpha_s^2)$, i.e. (5.10). So the scale dependence comes entirely from UV
renormalization of the Wilson-line operator, and graphs (b)(c) give the $2\xi/(1-\xi)$ term: without the Wilson line
the operator is not gauge invariant and Feynman gauge would not give the right kernel.

**§7.2 Partonic structure functions and coefficient functions.**
**Lemma 7.3 (initial-state collinear limit).** For a tree amplitude $\mathcal M_{n+1}(p;k,\dots)$ with incoming quark
$p$ emitting on-shell gluon $k$, $p-k=zp+\dots$, $t=(p-k)^2\to0$, summed over spins and colours,
$$\overline{|\mathcal M_{n+1}|^2}\xrightarrow{t\to0}\frac{8\pi\alpha_s\mu^{2\epsilon}}{-t}\frac1z\hat P_{qq}(z,\epsilon)\overline{|\mathcal M_n(zp,\dots)|^2},\qquad\hat P_{qq}(z,\epsilon)=C_F\Big[\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}-\epsilon(1-z)\Big].\tag{7.4}$$
*Proof.* By direct tree computation [33] ESW, or as Thm. 6.6 at tree level for one splitting: the leading region
comes from Cor. 3.8, $J$ is the splitting $p\to(p-k)+k$, $H=\mathcal M_n$, longitudinal gluons go to the Wilson line by the
Ward identity. So the $t\to0$ pole coefficient is the integrand of the $k_T$ integral in Prop. 7.1 (with
$t=-k_T^2/(1-z)$), i.e. $\hat P_{qq}(z,\epsilon)$. The $1/z$ combines with the flux factor so that $|\mathcal M_n(zp)|^2$ gets the
subprocess flux $1/(2zp\cdot q)$ and becomes $\hat\sigma_n(zp)$.

**Proposition 7.4 (one-loop coefficient functions).** Quark target:
$$\frac{\hat F_{2,q}(z)}{z}=e_q^2\Big\{\delta(1-z)+a_s\Big[-\frac1{\hat\epsilon}P^{(0)}_{qq}(z)+P^{(0)}_{qq}(z)\ln\frac{Q^2}{\mu^2}+c_q(z)\Big]\Big\}+O(\alpha_s^2)\tag{7.5}$$
($-1/\hat\epsilon$ is the IR/collinear pole). With (7.3) and (6.6), the $\overline{\rm MS}$ coefficient function is
$$C_{2,q}(z)=e_q^2\Big\{\delta(1-z)+a_s\Big[P^{(0)}_{qq}\ln\frac{Q^2}{\mu^2}+c_q(z)\Big]\Big\},\tag{7.6}$$
$$c_q=C_F\Big[2\Big(\frac{\ln(1-z)}{1-z}\Big)_+-\frac32\Big(\frac1{1-z}\Big)_+-(1+z)\ln(1-z)-\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}\ln z+3+2z-\Big(\frac92+\frac{\pi^2}3\Big)\delta(1-z)\Big].$$
Gluon target, one term for each quark and antiquark flavour, $P^{(0)}_{qg}=T_F[z^2+(1-z)^2]$:
$$C_{2,g}(z)=e_q^2a_s\Big[P^{(0)}_{qg}\ln\frac{Q^2}{\mu^2}+T_F\Big((z^2+(1-z)^2)\ln\frac{1-z}{z}-1+8z(1-z)\Big)\Big].\tag{7.7}$$
*Proof sketch.* Cut graphs as in Fig. 30. The real two-body phase space in $d$ dimensions is
$\int d\Phi_2=\frac1{8\pi}(\frac{4\pi}{\hat s})^\epsilon\frac1{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\int_0^1dy[y(1-y)]^{-\epsilon}$, with $\hat s=Q^2(1-z)/z$,
$y=(1-\cos\theta^*)/2$, and $t=-2p\cdot k=-(\hat s+Q^2)y=-Q^2y/z$. By Lemma 7.3 the integrand goes like $1/y$ as $y\to0$
with residue $\hat P_{qq}(z,\epsilon)$; $\int_0^1dy\,y^{-1-\epsilon}(\dots)=-\frac1\epsilon(\dots)|_{y=0}+$finite gives $-\frac1{\hat\epsilon}P$ and the
log (from $(\mu^2/\hat s)^\epsilon$ with $\hat s\propto Q^2$). The $y\to1$ (final-state collinear) and $z\to1$ (soft) divergences
cancel against the virtual (Fig. 29(c); on-shell form factor with $-2/\epsilon^2-3/\epsilon$), which is the final-state KLN
cancellation. **The finite parts are not computed; they are quoted** ([30] BBDM, [31] Furmanski–Petronzio, [33]

**Remark 7.5 (meaning of the check).** (i) The IR pole $-P_{qq}/\hat\epsilon$ of the partonic structure function equals
the PDF's IR pole, so it cancels in $C$. This is universality: the same PDF pole appears in DY. (ii) The $\mu$
dependence $P\ln(Q^2/\mu^2)$ of $C$ cancels DGLAP. (iii) Soft and final-state collinear divergences cancel in real
plus virtual, as in Prop. 4.2. The $[\ln(1-z)/(1-z)]_+$ in $c_q$ generates at higher orders the
"$\alpha_s^n\ln^{2n-1}(1-z)$" large logs and needs threshold resummation (§14). [See ERRORS E5.]

## PART III. ERRORS (verified; page; correction)

**E1 (pp. 36–37, Prop. 5.4 and its proof). The single-kernel Mellin inversion is valid only at LO.**
The proposition defines $\gamma^{(n)}_{ij}=-2\int_0^1z^{n-1}P_{ij}(z)\,dz$ and asserts (5.9) ⟺ (5.10), with (5.9) to be read for
*all* $n$, even and odd. The proof takes $\int_0^1x^{n-1}$ of (5.10) and identifies $\int_0^1x^{n-1}f_j$ with $A^{(n)}_j$, but by
(5.8) $A^{(n)}_q=\int_0^1\xi^{n-1}[f_q+(-1)^nf_{\bar q}]$. For the C-even combination $q+\bar q$ with even $n$ this works only if
$P_{qq}$ acts identically on $q$ and $\bar q$. Beyond LO the even-$n$ (C-even) and odd-$n$ (C-odd) anomalous dimensions are
moments of different kernels, $P^{\pm}_{\rm NS}=P^V_{qq}\pm P^V_{q\bar q}$, and $P^V_{q\bar q}\neq0$ from NLO on (colour factor
$C_F(C_F-C_A/2)$, Curci–Furmanski–Petronzio 1980). No single function $P_{qq}$ on $[0,1]$ can have both sets of moments.
Numerical evidence (`checks/check_Pplusminus.py`): with $P^{V(1)}_{q\bar q}=C_F(C_F-\frac{C_A}2)[2p_{qq}(-x)S_2(x)+2(1+x)\ln x+4(1-x)]$,
$\int_0^1P^{V(1)}_{q\bar q}dx=0.71931\,C_F(C_F-\frac{C_A}2)=\frac12C_F(C_F-\frac{C_A}2)(\frac{13}2-6\zeta_2+4\zeta_3)\neq0$. So $\int P^+\neq0$,
whereas $\int P^-=0$ (number conservation).
*Correction:* state the equivalence separately: even-$n$ RG ⟺ DGLAP for the C-even set $(q_i+\bar q_i,\,g)$ with kernels
determined by their even moments (Lemma 5.5 applied to these kernels); odd-$n$ RG ⟺ DGLAP for $q_i-\bar q_i$ with $P^-$.
Complex-$N$ anomalous dimensions are defined by Carlson continuation from each parity separately. Matches our critic
O6 and blueprint P5.15.

**E2 (p. 41, Fig. 24 and the region set used in Thm. 6.3).** The caption says the two-rung ladder has "two leading
regions" $R_1,R_2$. The maximal region $R_0$ (both rungs hard, $J$ = external parton lines or hadron blob only) is
missing. Its term $C_{R_0}\Gamma=T_{R_0}(\Gamma-C_{R_1}\Gamma-C_{R_2}\Gamma)$ carries the genuine two-loop coefficient function. Without

lowest-order term) needs it. *Correction:* list three regions $R_2<R_1<R_0$, with the purely hard region maximal in the
poset (as in our blueprint D7.1).

## PART V. GAPS (missing or weak in the segment; what our monograph must do better)

**e⁺e⁻ (our Ch5).**
- G1. The identification of the *partonic cross section* (sum over physical cuts in DR) with $12\pi{\rm Im}\Pi$ is never
  argued. It needs order-by-order cutting rules in $D$ dimensions, the cancellation of ghost and unphysical
  polarizations (BRST), counterterm insertions, and the fact that the additive counterterm is real. Our c_ee §5,
  ingredient 3, tags this step [A].
- G2. The interchange of $\epsilon\to0$ with analytic continuation is glossed. The clean argument: each diagram is
  $c(\epsilon)(-q^2-i0)^{-L\epsilon}$, the Laurent coefficients are polynomials in $L$ vanishing for real $L$, hence identically.
- G3. Hypotheses of the Euclidean IR-finiteness theorem are not stated: renormalizable, no super-renormalizable
  couplings, renormalization not at zero momentum; DR version Breitenlohner–Maison 1977; gauge theories Poggio–Quinn 1976.
- G4. Missing: the explicit two-loop Euclidean $\Pi$ route to $1+\alpha_s/\pi$ (constant $\frac{55}{12}-4\zeta_3$); the
  contour formula $R=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint D(-z)dz/z$; the $\pi^2$-terms $r_3=d_3-\frac{\pi^2}3b_0^2d_1$; the group-theory form of
  $r_2$; the singlet term and Furry's theorem; regulator independence (massive quarks); Sterman's Minkowski-space proof.
  The one-loop real/virtual constants are quoted, not derived, and their normalization dependence is only implicit.

**DIS / OPE (our Ch6).**
- G5. The $\delta'(x)$ counterexample: even moments do not determine distributions supported at $x=0$. Lemma 5.5 is
  stated only for $L^1$ functions, yet partonic objects are distributions.
- G6. The Carlson-theorem issue and the $P^\pm_{\rm NS}$ distinction (see E1).
- G7. Gauge-theory OPE subtleties: mixing of $O_g$, $O_q$ with BRST-exact and EOM-vanishing operators (Joglekar–Lee 1976;
  Kluberg-Stern–Zuber 1975). Not mentioned in this segment.
- G8. Missing: positivity statements ($F_1,F_L\ge0$); explicit target-mass corrections (Nachtmann, Georgi–Politzer);
  twist-4 operator basis (Jaffe–Soldate, Ellis–Furmanski–Petronzio); the explicit LO solution of RG (non-singlet
  exponent $-2\gamma_{qq}(n)/\beta_0$, $n=2$ singlet eigenvalues, asymptotic $\langle x\rangle_q=3n_f/(16+3n_f)$); polarized OPE
  (Bjorken, Crewther); the moment table of one-loop Wilson coefficients; and the one-loop check that the hard-region
  expansion of the forward amplitude equals the moments of the cut-diagram result (our c_dis `ope_check.py`, exact in
  $\epsilon$). Some of these may be in the reference's §14 or App. C (outside this segment).
- G9. The light-cone expansion vs short-distance OPE distinction (our O4) is only implicit. The segment says
  "$z^2\to0$ dominates" and then uses only Taylor coefficients, without saying that fixed moments need only the
  Euclidean short-distance theorem.

**PDFs (our Ch7).**
- G10. In Prop. 7.1 the virtual graphs are not computed. The $\delta(1-\xi)$ term is fixed by the number sum rule. The
  individual Wilson-line real and virtual graphs have a $\xi\to1$ (rapidity) divergence not regulated by the factored
  $k_T$ integral in pure DR, cancelling only in $[2\xi/(1-\xi)]_+$. The quark self-energy contributes $-\frac12\delta(1-\xi)$ (in
  UV-pole units). Our c_pdf computes all pieces, also with a gluon-mass regulator, and checks gauge-parameter
  independence. Only $P_{qq}$ is derived; $P_{qg},P_{gq},P_{gg}$ from operator renormalization are absent.
- G11. The statement "MS renormalization of local operators induces that of the PDF" (p. 36) is asserted, not proven.
  Our App. L covers light-ray ↔ local moments to all orders in $\overline{\rm MS}$.

**DIS all orders (our Ch8–Ch9).**
- G12. Step 1 of Thm. 6.6 (combinatorial regrouping of $\sum_{\Gamma,R}C_R\Gamma$ into $\hat T\otimes f$) is three sentences, though

- G13. The finite parts $c_q$ and $C_{2,g}$ are quoted, not derived. Missing: the spacelike form factor

  average $1/(D-2)$ as part of the $\overline{\rm MS}$ gluon-PDF convention (with average $\frac12$, $C_{2,g}$ shifts by $+P_{qg}$); $F_L$
  ($C_L^q=2C_Fz$, $C_L^g=4T_Fz(1-z)$); DIS scheme; explicit $\mu_F,\mu_R$ independence at $O(\alpha_s)$; heavy-quark schemes.
  All are in our c_dis.
- G14. The one-loop ladder of Fig. 24 is used only qualitatively. No explicit two-region subtraction is computed in
  the segment (deferred to Example D.48).
