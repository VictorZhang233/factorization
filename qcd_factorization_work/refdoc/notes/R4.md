## PART I. Faithful account of the segment

### End of §11.3 "端点发散与因子化的失效" (p. 72, top)

The section (begun on p. 71) states: when the hard kernel is more singular at an endpoint than the distribution
amplitude vanishes, the convolution diverges; this signals the failure of hard–soft separation (a parton with
momentum fraction → 0 becomes soft). The list on p. 72:

- **(a) Twist-3 contribution to the pion form factor.** The hard kernel is more singular than $1/\bar x$ at the
  endpoint, while the twist-3 DA $\phi_p(x)\to1$ does not vanish there, so the convolution diverges
  ("$\int dx\,\phi_p/\bar x$-type terms already diverge logarithmically; more singular terms diverge more strongly").
- **(b) $B\to\pi$ form factor.** In SCET the leading-power form factor splits into a "soft overlap" term $\xi_\pi(E)$
  (not further factorizable) and a "hard spectator" term $\int T\otimes\phi_B\otimes\phi_\pi$. Writing the soft overlap
  as a convolution produces endpoint divergences [46, 47]. Hence the factorization of $B\to\pi$ is partial: one
  non-perturbative function $\xi_\pi$ is an input.
- **(c) Non-leptonic $B\to\pi\pi$.** "BBNS QCD factorization holds at $O(\alpha_s)$ and leading power [48]";
  annihilation and twist-3 "chirally enhanced" contributions have endpoint divergences, treated by parametrized models.
  Grade C.
- **(d) Large-angle elastic $pp$.** Besides the Brodsky–Farrar short-distance mechanism there is the Landshoff
  mechanism — three quark pairs scatter independently at different space-time points — a pinch solution of the Landau
  equations that dominates the power counting. Botts–Sterman [49] proved that Sudakov factors suppress it; the final
  scaling depends on the full treatment of the Sudakov factor.
- Recent progress: "rearranged" factorization and endpoint resummation; power-suppressed terms in $h\to\gamma\gamma$
  via a $b$-quark loop [50] now have a systematic subleading-power factorization formula; extension to $gg\to h$ under way.

### §12 "C 级与 D 级：猜想、部分证明与已知的破坏" — introduction (p. 72)

All previous proofs rest on (i) sufficient inclusiveness of the measurement with respect to soft radiation and
spectators (so that unitarity can be used), or (ii) participation of the hadrons as small colour singlets (colour
transparency). In hadron–hadron collisions producing coloured final states (jets, high-$p_T$ hadrons) neither holds fully.

### §12.1 Single-inclusive hadrons and jets in hadron–hadron collisions (C) (pp. 72–73)

**Conjecture 12.1 ($pp\to hX$ and $pp\to$ jets, collinear factorization).**
$$E_h\frac{d\sigma}{d^3p_h}=\sum_{a,b,c}\int dx_a\,dx_b\,\frac{dz}{z^2}\,f_{a/A}(x_a,\mu)\,f_{b/B}(x_b,\mu)\,\hat\sigma_{ab\to c}(\dots,\mu)\,d_{h/c}(z,\mu)+\text{power-suppressed},\tag{12.1}$$
with the same PDFs and FFs as in DIS and $e^+e^-$; an analogous formula (without FFs) for IR-safe jet cross sections.
"This formula is the basis of LHC physics, agrees with data at NNLO (in some cases N³LO), but has not been proven."
Difficulties listed:
- (a) Unlike DY, the hard scattering produces coloured final partons $c,d$. A Glauber gluon from a spectator of $A$
  can attach to $c,d$ (final) or $b$ (initial). For final-state Glauber exchange the unitarity argument (G2) needs the
  sum over the final states of $c,d$ and their fragments, but the observed hadron $h$ from $c$ is measured, so the sum
  over that jet is incomplete. In App. E language: the gap is exactly in the one-sided rewriting after the cut sum —
  final-state insertions on the observed jet can neither be cancelled by unitarity pairing nor rewritten as
  equal-time insertions (Rem. E.54).
- (b) Collins–Qiu [52]: with unintegrated $k_T$ the Glauber exchanges cannot in general be collected into Wilson lines
  depending on single hadrons (§12.3). For integrated collinear factorization one "believes" the effects cancel after
  $k_T$ integration (DY-type argument plus universality of final-state interactions in FFs [36]), but the argument has
  not been carried out completely.
- (c) Amplitude-level space-like collinear factorization is proven violated at one and two loops (§12.2), hinting at
  possible violations of the cross section at higher orders.

### §12.2 Space-like collinear limits and the breakdown of coherence (amplitude level, D) (p. 73)

For purely final-state (time-like) collinear limits, the $(n+1)$-parton amplitude factorizes as
${\rm Split}\otimes\mathcal M_n$ with a splitting amplitude depending only on the collinear partons ("strict collinear
factorization"). Catani–de Florian–Rodrigo [54] proved that when one collinear parton is in the initial state
(space-like limit) and other coloured initial and final partons are present, the one-loop splitting amplitude contains
$${\rm Split}^{(1)}\supset\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\,i\pi\sum_{j\in\text{非共线}}\pm\,\mathbf T_{\rm coll}\cdot\mathbf T_j\,\frac1\epsilon\cdots\tag{12.2}$$
"where $\mathbf T_j$ are the colour charges of the non-collinear partons and the sign depends on whether $j$ is
initial or final" ($\mathbf T_{\rm coll}$ is **not defined** in the text). These terms come from Glauber (Coulomb)
phases and make the splitting amplitude depend on the colours of the other partons; strict collinear factorization
fails at amplitude level. In $e^+e^-$ (final state only) or DIS/DY (one coloured initial/final system) colour
conservation reduces them to absorbable phases; in $pp\to$ jets it does not.

**Remark 12.2.** "For one- and two-loop cross sections these phase terms cancel after the colour sum (the one-loop
$i\pi$ term vanishes in $|\mathcal M|^2$)." Forshaw–Seymour–Siódmok [55]: for observables restricting final-state
radiation (energy "gaps" between jets) the effects do not cancel at higher orders, giving super-leading logarithms,
first at $\alpha_s^4L^5$ [56], recently resummed [57]. "Standard collinear factorization fails for such observables."
For fully inclusive jet cross sections it is open whether a violation appears at some higher order.

### §12.3 TMD factorization breaking in $pp\to h_1h_2X$ (D) (pp. 73–74)

**Theorem 12.3 (Collins–Qiu 2007; Rogers–Mulders 2010).** For $p+p\to h_1+h_2+X$ (two hadrons nearly back to back,
transverse imbalance measured) there is **no** factorization of the form
$$d\sigma\propto\sum H\otimes\tilde f_{a/A}\otimes\tilde f_{b/B}\otimes\tilde d_{h_1/c}\otimes\tilde d_{h_2/d}\otimes\tilde S$$
in which every TMD factor is an operator matrix element depending on one hadron only, with arbitrary
(process-dependent) Wilson-line structure.

Framing (p. 73–74): the negative statement holds as an explicit counterexample in a spectator model ("perturbative
non-abelian gauge theory with coloured spectators"); since a general proof using only gauge structure, Ward identities
and power counting would apply to the model too, such factorization cannot hold as a general theorem; strictly it
"excludes a general proof of this form" rather than computing real-hadron cross sections (App. E: Rem. E.54).

**Figure 47 (p. 74).** Spectator interactions in $pp\to h_1h_2X$: hadron $A$'s spectator (dashed) exchanges Glauber
gluons with the initial parton $b$ (green) or final partons $c,d$ (red); hadron $B$'s spectator likewise (blue).
Caption: the colour matrices act on the same hard colour space, do not commute, and cannot be distributed into Wilson
lines depending only on $A$ or only on $B$; crossings are planar projections, not vertices.

**"论证" (argument, p. 74).** A gluon $k$ from $A$'s spectator, collinear to $A$ (large $k^+$); for $j\in\{b,c,d\}$
$n_j\cdot k\simeq n_j^-k^+$; eikonal coupling to the hard amplitude (schematic; exact regions in [52, 53]):
$$\sum_{j\in\{b,c,d\}}\mathbf T_j\,\frac{n_j^\mu}{n_j\cdot k+i\eta_j0}\ \xrightarrow{\ \text{Glauber part}\ }\ -i\pi\sum_{j\in\{b,c,d\}}\eta_j\,\mathbf T_j\,n_j^\mu\,\delta(n_j\cdot k),$$
$\eta_j=\pm1$ according to $j$ initial (interacts before the collision) or final. In DY only $\mathbf T_b$ occurs,
rewritten as $-\mathbf T_a$ (colour conservation), i.e. a Wilson line in $A$'s PDF in $a$'s representation. In
$pp\to h_1h_2X$, "$\eta_b\mathbf T_b+\eta_c\mathbf T_c+\eta_d\mathbf T_d$ cannot be reduced via colour conservation
$\mathbf T_a+\mathbf T_b=\mathbf T_c+\mathbf T_d$ to an operator acting only on $a$, **because $\eta_b\neq\eta_{c,d}$**."
At one gluon this colour operator can be absorbed in a "colour-entangled", process-dependent Wilson line (generalized
TMD factorization). Rogers–Mulders [53]: one Glauber gluon from $A$'s spectator plus one from $B$'s; "explicit
calculation shows that the colour factor is of the form ${\rm Tr}[t^at^b\cdots t^at^b\cdots]$, different from the
${\rm Tr}[t^at^a\cdots]{\rm Tr}[t^bt^b\cdots]$ structure of any (A factor)×(B factor), the difference ∝ $[t^a,t^b]\neq0$;
zero in an abelian theory." Hence no TMD factorization in non-abelian QCD even with arbitrary process-dependent
Wilson lines. □ (See ERRORS E1, E2: the colour bookkeeping and the description of RM are wrong.)

**Remark 12.4.** The breaking does not affect (12.1): after $k_T$ integration the colour-entanglement effects are
believed to cancel, unproven. "Factorization" is not a universal property of QCD.

### §12.4 Hard diffraction (B in DIS, D in hadron collisions) (pp. 74–75)

Diffractive DIS $e+p\to e+X+p'$, outgoing proton with momentum fraction $1-x_{\mathbb P}$, rapidity gap. Collins
[58] (CSS standard) proved
$$d\sigma^{\rm D}=\sum_i\int d\xi\,f^{\rm D}_{i/p}(\xi,x_{\mathbb P},t;\mu)\,d\hat\sigma_{ei}(\xi,\dots)+\text{power-suppressed},\tag{12.3}$$
$f^{\rm D}$ = conditional distribution "parton $i$ in the proton, final state contains a proton of momentum $p'$",
defined by the same Wilson-line operator with $p'$ fixed in the intermediate states. Reason: DIS has one hadron; soft
couplings to the current jet cancel by unitarity; all proton-direction interactions go into $f^{\rm D}$.
Hadron colliders: the same formula fails — CDF diffractive dijets at the Tevatron ≈ one order of magnitude below the
prediction from HERA diffractive PDFs; reason = converse of Prop. 9.3: the gap restricts the spectators' final state,
spectator–spectator soft (Glauber) interactions fill the gap and no longer cancel (App. E: the gap projection does not
commute with spectator insertions; Rem. E.55); phenomenologically a gap survival probability $S^2\sim0.1$.

**Figure 48 (p. 75).** Top: amplitude structure; bottom: final-state particles vs pseudorapidity $\eta$ ($p'$ to the
right). (a) diffractive DIS $\gamma^*p\to Xp'$ with $f^{\rm D}_{i/p}$ and an intact gap; (b) $p\bar p\to p'+{\rm dijet}+X$:
a spectator of $\bar p$ (dashed) exchanges soft/Glauber gluons (red) with the $p'$ system, producing particles in the
gap (red ticks) or breaking $p'$; diffractive-PDF predictions must be multiplied by $S^2\sim0.1$.

### §12.5 NRQCD factorization (decay A\*, production C) (pp. 75–76)

Inclusive quarkonium decay as an OPE in NRQCD [59]:
$$\Gamma(H\to\text{light hadrons})=\sum_n\frac{2\,{\rm Im}f_n(\alpha_s)}{m_Q^{d_n-4}}\langle H|\mathcal O_n|H\rangle,\tag{12.4}$$
$\mathcal O_n=\psi^\dagger\mathcal K_n\chi\,\chi^\dagger\mathcal K_n'\psi$ four-fermion operators. "Analogue of §4: the rate is
$\langle H|{\rm Im}\mathcal T|H\rangle$, locality of $\mathcal T$ in the $1/m_Q$ expansion guaranteed by short-distance
annihilation (scale $1/m_Q$); as rigorous as the OPE (given NRQCD as EFT of QCD — this is the A\* of Table 1)"; the
non-perturbative OPE is again an App. C-type assumption.
Production:
$$d\sigma(H+X)=\sum_n d\hat\sigma(Q\bar Q[n]+X)\,\langle\mathcal O_n^H\rangle,\qquad \mathcal O_n^H=\chi^\dagger\mathcal K_n\psi\Big(\sum_X|H+X\rangle\langle H+X|\Big)\psi^\dagger\mathcal K_n'\chi.\tag{12.5}$$
Not a forward matrix element: the intermediate states contain the observed $H$; soft gluons exchanged between the
$Q\bar Q$ and other coloured particles may give IR divergences not absorbable into the (unmodified) $\langle\mathcal O_n^H\rangle$.

**Figure 49 (p. 76).** (a) colour-octet mechanism: hard subgraph (scale $1/m_Q$ or $1/p_T$) makes
$c\bar c[^3S_1^{(8)}]$ (+ recoil gluon); at long distance (grey "NR") emission of soft gluons with $E\sim m_Qv^2$ turns
it into the singlet $J/\psi$, probability $\langle\mathcal O_n^{J/\psi}\rangle$. (b) cut diagram: a soft gluon $k$
exchanged between the heavy pair (amplitude) and another coloured particle (recoil parton in the conjugate
amplitude), crossing the cut; Prop. 8.4 does not apply directly; NQS at NNLO: after supplementing octet operators with
Wilson lines to infinity the IR poles are absorbed.

**Conjecture 12.5 (NRQCD production factorization).** (12.5) holds for $p_T\gg m_Q$ **or** $\sqrt s\gg m_Q$, to all
orders in $\alpha_s$ and at leading power in the velocity.
NQS [60] verified at NNLO (two loops) that the IR divergences of gluon fragmentation to quarkonium are absorbed provided
the octet operators $\mathcal O_n^H$ are modified to include time-like Wilson lines to infinity (gauge invariance);
"**these Wilson lines do not change the numerical result at NNLO**, but may contribute non-trivially at higher
orders" (see ERRORS E3). No all-order proof.

### §12.6 Others: DPS and small-$x$ (p. 76)

- **DPS.** For double Drell–Yan, Diehl, Gaunt et al. [61] extended the CSS Glauber cancellation: Glauber exchanges cancel
  after summing over final states, supporting a factorization formula with double parton distributions (DPDs); DPD
  definitions must handle SPS/DPS double counting. Organisation as in §9; grade B′ "only in the CSS sense" (App. E's
  conditional reconstruction does not cover it); in App. E's framework the extension is expected under the same
  assumptions but needs extra treatment of two hard vertices, colour-correlated DPDs and the overlap with the
  $1\to2$ splitting region — not proven here (Rem. E.57).
- **High-energy ($k_T$) factorization.** For $x\to0$, cross sections as $k_T$ convolutions of unintegrated gluon
  distributions and off-shell hard coefficients (Catani–Ciafaloni–Hautmann [62], Collins–Ellis [63]), evolution by BFKL
  or BK/JIMWLK. Holds at LL; proven at leading order in the CGC framework for some processes (e.g. inclusive gluon
  production in $pA$); for NLL and general processes, status "similar to C".

### §13 "有效场论的观点：SCET 中的因子化" — introduction (p. 77)

SCET [64, 65, 67, 66] lifts the region analysis of §3 to the Lagrangian: each mode (3.7) a separate field; the hard
region integrated out into matching coefficients; factorization becomes a field redefinition and the proof is very
short. Price: the power-counting/overlap problems of §3.3 and §6.2, and the Glauber problem of §9, become assumptions
on the completeness of the EFT (§13.3). "Hence SCET arguments are not more rigorous than Collins' method: the unproven
inputs correspond essentially to the same set (numbered assumptions of App. D and E; SCET additionally needs all-order
matching)."

### §13.1 Leading Lagrangian and soft–collinear decoupling (pp. 77–78)

Normalization: SCET convention $n\cdot\bar n=2$, i.e. the light-like vectors of (2.2) replaced by $\sqrt2 n^\mu$,
$\sqrt2\bar n^\mu$, so $n\cdot v=\sqrt2 v^-$, $\bar n\cdot v=\sqrt2v^+$ (agrees with CONTEXT). $n$-collinear ($\bar n\cdot p\sim Q$);
$\xi_n=\frac{\slashed n\bar{\slashed n}}4\psi$, and $\frac{\slashed n\bar{\slashed n}}4=\frac12\gamma^-\gamma^+=\mathcal P_+$ (good
components of (2.4)) — checked ($\slashed n=\sqrt2\gamma^-$, $\bar{\slashed n}=\sqrt2\gamma^+$). Leading $n$-collinear quark Lagrangian:
$$\mathcal L_{\xi_n}=\bar\xi_n\Big[in\cdot D_n+g\,n\cdot A_s+i\slashed D_{n\perp}\frac1{i\bar n\cdot D_n}i\slashed D_{n\perp}\Big]\frac{\bar{\slashed n}}2\xi_n,\tag{13.1}$$
$D_n$ contains only $A_n$; the "soft" gluon $A_s$ (scale $\lambda^2Q$, i.e. ultrasoft in SCET_I language) appears only via
$n\cdot A_s$ — the eikonal coupling (3.11) in the Lagrangian (multipole expansion: at LP $A_s$ depends only on the
$\bar n\cdot x\propto x^+$ component at the position of $\xi_n$ — checked: the slow collinear coordinate is $x^+$; Cor. 3.17).

**Theorem 13.1 (BPS field redefinition [67]).** With
$$Y_n(x)=P\exp\Big(ig\int_{-\infty}^0ds\,n\cdot A_s(x+sn)\Big),\qquad \xi_n=Y_n\xi_n^{(0)},\qquad A_n=Y_nA_n^{(0)}Y_n^\dagger,\tag{13.2}$$
$\mathcal L_{\xi_n}[\xi_n,A_n,A_s]=\mathcal L_{\xi_n}[\xi_n^{(0)},A_n^{(0)},0]$; the same holds for the LP collinear-gluon
Lagrangian (with a collinear gauge fixing covariant w.r.t. the soft background, plus ghosts): the redefined collinear
fields decouple completely from soft gluons. "An algebraic identity of the multipole-expanded LP Lagrangian."
*Proof.* Path-ordered exponential: $n\cdot\partial Y_n=ig\,n\cdot A_sY_n$, i.e. $(n\cdot\partial-ig\,n\cdot A_s)Y_n=0$,
hence $Y_n^\dagger\,in\cdot D_s\,Y_n=in\cdot\partial$, $D_s\equiv\partial-igA_s$. In (13.1) $in\cdot D_n+g\,n\cdot A_s=in\cdot\partial+g\,n\cdot A_n+g\,n\cdot A_s$;
substituting, $Y_n^\dagger(in\cdot\partial+g\,n\cdot A_s+gY_nn\cdot A_n^{(0)}Y_n^\dagger)Y_n=in\cdot\partial+g\,n\cdot A_n^{(0)}$
(Leibniz + the equation for $Y_n$). $\bar n\cdot D_n$, $D_{n\perp}$ contain only collinear fields; at LP soft fields do
not contribute to them ($\bar n\cdot A_s\ll\bar n\cdot A_n$, $A_{s\perp}\ll A_{n\perp}$), and $Y_n$ is "constant" for
collinear fields (multipole expansion), so $Y_n^\dagger(\cdots)Y_n$ just replaces $A_n\to A_n^{(0)}$. □
(All identities checked; our `research/c_scet` verifies the key identity numerically for random su(3) fields.)

**Remark 13.2 (quantum level).** Lagrangian identity; in the path integral $Y_n$ is unitary and independent of the
collinear fields, Jacobian 1, decoupling holds to all orders in the LP EFT. It does not show that the EFT reproduces
QCD at LP (needs the three inputs of §13.3). The direction of $Y_n$ (from $-\infty$ or to $+\infty$) corresponds to
the $i0$ of the soft-gluon pole; the Lagrangian decoupling holds for both; the physical choice is fixed by the external
states (past-pointing for initial, future-pointing for final). The difference of the two eikonal propagators,
$\frac1{n\cdot k+i0}-\frac1{n\cdot k-i0}=-2\pi i\,\delta(n\cdot k)$ (checked), is supported at $n\cdot k=0$, "i.e. the
Glauber region (where the eikonal approximation itself fails)"; the correct prescription is decided by whether the
contour can be pushed out of the Glauber region (Lemma 8.2, 9.5), "which cannot be seen at the Lagrangian level"
(see E10).

**Figure 50 (p. 78).** BPS redefinition; ⊗ = operator vertex (e.g. the e.m. current matched to SCET). (a) before:
$\xi_n$ (solid) interacts with $A_n$ (blue); soft gluons $A_s$ (green) couple anywhere along the collinear line via
$n\cdot A_s$. (b) after: $\xi_n^{(0)},A_n^{(0)}$ decouple; all soft gluons attach to the soft Wilson line $Y_n$ (green

**Remark 13.3.** Thm. 13.1 and Cor. 3.17 have essentially the same proof (soft fields are pure gauge for collinear
particles, removed by the gauge transformation $Y_n$); Cor. 3.17 on QCD graphs after region approximations, Thm. 13.1

### §13.2 Example: thrust in $e^+e^-$ (pp. 78–79)

Thrust $T$, dijet region $\tau=1-T\to0$, an inclusive process with soft factor ≠ 1 (Rem. 8.5(ii)). Matching:
$\bar\psi\gamma^\mu\psi\to C(Q^2,\mu)\,\bar\xi_{\bar n}W_{\bar n}\gamma_\perp^\mu W_n^\dagger\xi_n$ ($W_n$ collinear Wilson line, from the
Ward identity of longitudinal collinear gluons, Thm. 3.14). After BPS ($W_n=Y_nW_n^{(0)}Y_n^\dagger$ from
$A_n=Y_nA_n^{(0)}Y_n^\dagger$) the current becomes $C\,\bar\xi^{(0)}_{\bar n}W^{(0)}_{\bar n}Y^\dagger_{\bar n}\gamma_\perp^\mu Y_nW^{(0)\dagger}_n\xi^{(0)}_n$
(checked). Both jets are final, so (Rem. 13.2) future-pointing $Y$ should be used: by Lemma 8.2/Cor. 8.3 the contour
can leave the Glauber region and the eikonal approximation with $+i0$ holds there. Since
$\tau=(p_a^2+p_b^2)/Q^2+k_s/Q+O(\tau^2)$ is additive at LP,
$$\frac1{\sigma_0}\frac{d\sigma}{d\tau}=H(Q^2,\mu)\int dp_a^2\,dp_b^2\,dk\,J(p_a^2,\mu)J(p_b^2,\mu)S_T(k,\mu)\,\delta\Big(\tau-\frac{p_a^2+p_b^2}{Q^2}-\frac kQ\Big),\tag{13.3}$$
$H=|C|^2$, $J$ = jet function,
$$S_T(k)=\frac1{N_c}\sum_{X_s}{\rm Tr}\Big[\langle0|\bar T(Y_n^\dagger Y_{\bar n})|X_s\rangle\langle X_s|T(Y_{\bar n}^\dagger Y_n)|0\rangle\Big]\,\delta\big(k-k_{\rm thrust}(X_s)\big).\tag{13.4}$$
The measured soft function is not 1 by unitarity; its non-perturbative part gives $O(\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/(Q\tau))$ power
corrections (§14); RG equations resum $\ln\tau$ to N³LL [70]. Since $e^+e^-$ has only final states, Glauber modes are
removed by contour deformation (Cor. 8.3); grade B: conditional theorem (Prop. D.30, Thm. D.46(b)) under Ass. D.7,
D.14, D.27, D.37, D.41, D.43 (cut-sum representation with event-shape condition (X3)); the additivity error of the
measurement is estimated explicitly; "zero-bin of collinear factors is scaleless = 0" (so subtracted = unsubtracted jet

### §13.3 Premises of SCET proofs (pp. 79–80)

Three inputs corresponding to the three difficulties of Collins' method:
- **(i) Mode completeness.** The EFT must contain all LP regions (≡ Thm. 3.12); in SCET argued by power counting and the
  method of regions; for general graphs the needed power-counting and contour inputs are Ass. D.7, D.14, "with no
  complete proof in the literature".
- **(ii) Zero-bin subtraction** [68]: collinear momentum integrals contain the soft region, subtracted to avoid double
  counting; EFT version of Thm. 6.3; "zero-bin = soft factor" proven at one loop, all orders conditional on the
  product-structure assumption (Prop. D.36, Ass. D.37).
- **(iii) Glauber modes.** Standard SCET Lagrangians lack Glauber modes; BPS decoupling fails when they are present.
  Rothstein–Stewart [69] constructed Glauber operators, $n$–soft–$\bar n$ part
  $$\mathcal L_G\supset\sum_{i,j=q,g}\mathcal O_n^{iB}\,\frac1{\mathcal P_\perp^2}\,\mathcal O_s^{BC}\,\frac1{\mathcal P_\perp^2}\,\mathcal O_{\bar n}^{jC}$$
  (plus collinear–soft terms): an instantaneous (local in $x^\pm$) transverse "potential" between collinear and soft
  operators, not absorbable into Wilson lines. Factorization requires showing that $\mathcal L_G$ effects cancel:
  trivial for $e^+e^-$ (final states only); for DY RS discussed it via time ordering, causality and final-state sums,
  consistent with CSS (G2)(G3), "but this is not a complete proof for all orders and all graphs" (§9.5; Rem. E.56). For
  $pp\to$ jets the mixing of $\mathcal L_G$ with Wilson lines is the origin of the coherence violation and SLLs of §12.2.

### §14 "幂次修正、红外重整化子与对数重求和" — introduction (p. 80)

Factorization theorems are statements about asymptotic expansions (LP term factorized). At each order of PT the
remainder has a bound $O(\lambda^aL^K)$ under App. D (Thm. D.10), $a>0$ possibly much smaller than the true power,
constants growing with the order; such per-order bounds cannot be summed in $\alpha_s$; non-perturbative remainders
($(\Lambda/Q)^p$) have no rigorous bounds except for DIS moments (App. C's four assumption groups, Thm. C.66).

### §14.1 Twist expansion (p. 80)

In DIS the OPE organizes power corrections (§5). Per order of PT the massless-QCD OPE is a literature theorem (Thm. C.35;
App. C reconstructs its exact identities in a model theory, Thm. C.25); non-perturbatively, assuming the OPE (Ass. C.58, Rem. 5.7):
$$M_2^{(n)}(Q^2)=\sum_iC^{(n)}_{2,i}A^{(n)}_i+\frac1{Q^2}\sum_jC^{(n),\tau=4}_{2,j}A^{(n),\tau=4}_j+O\Big(\frac1{Q^4}\Big),\tag{14.1}$$
($M_2^{(n)}\equiv\int dx\,x^{n-2}F_2$, eq. (5.7)); twist-4 operators: four-quark operators and quark–gluon operators of
type $\bar\psi\tilde F\gamma\psi$ (multi-parton correlations). Trace terms of twist-2 operators give target-mass
corrections: leading term in the $n$-th moment $n(n-1)M^2a_{n+2}/[(n+2)Q^2]$ with $a_m\equiv\sum_iC^{(m)}_{2,i}A^{(m)}_i$,
relative size $\sim nM^2/Q^2$, resummable with the Nachtmann variable [142] (coefficient **verified numerically** from
the Georgi–Politzer formula for $n=2,4,6$). Processes without OPE (DY, $e^+e^-\to hX$): NLP factorization must be proven
separately, typically with three-parton correlators (Qiu–Sterman); for DY at $O(1/Q^2)$ done only partially.

### §14.2 IR renormalons (pp. 80–81)

LP coefficient $C=\sum_nc_n\alpha_s^{n+1}$, $\alpha_s=\alpha_s(Q)$; convention
$\frac{d\alpha_s}{d\ln\mu^2}=-\frac{\beta_0}{4\pi}\alpha_s^2+O(\alpha_s^3)$, $\beta_0=11-\frac23n_f$,
$\Lambda^2_{\rm QCD}=Q^2\exp[-\frac{4\pi}{\beta_0\alpha_s(Q)}]$ (one loop). Large-$\beta_0$: $n$ fermion bubbles on one gluon line give
$$c_n\sim K\,n!\Big(\frac{\beta_0}{4\pi p}\Big)^nn^b\quad(n\to\infty),\tag{14.2}$$
$p$ fixed by the IR behaviour (integrand $\propto(k^2/Q^2)^p$ as $k\to0$ ⇒ IR renormalon at $p$). Same-sign factorial
divergence, not Borel summable; $B[C](u)=\sum c_nu^n/n!$ has a pole at $u=4\pi p/\beta_0$; ambiguity
$$\delta C\sim\exp\Big(-\frac{4\pi p}{\beta_0\alpha_s(Q)}\Big)=\Big(\frac{\Lambda^2_{\rm QCD}}{Q^2}\Big)^p.\tag{14.3}$$

**Figure 52 (p. 81).** (a) bubble chain on the gluon of a one-loop vertex graph (= running coupling at $k^2$); (b) Borel
plane, axis in units of $4\pi/\beta_0$: UV renormalons at $-1,-2$ (alternating, Borel summable); IR renormalons at
$1/2$ (⊗, only for some observables, e.g. event shapes), $1,2,3$ on the integration path; contours $\mathcal C_\pm$;
difference = (14.3).

**Proposition 14.1 (renormalon ↔ power corrections, large-$\beta_0$ statement).** In DIS, in the large-$\beta_0$
approximation (single bubble chain), the IR renormalon ambiguity of the twist-2 coefficient function at $p=1$ and the
UV renormalon ambiguity of the twist-4 matrix elements (source: with a cutoff $\mu$ the twist-4 operators mix
quadratically $\propto\mu^2$ with twist-2, giving $\mu^2/Q^2$ terms in (14.1)) are equal in magnitude and opposite in
sign, so the sum (14.1) is unambiguous. Beyond large $\beta_0$ this cancellation is a widely accepted but unproven
conjecture (Rem. C.69); App. C does not depend on it.
*Argument.* Hard cutoff $\mu_F$ separating PDF and coefficient in (5.13); the region $k<\mu_F$ moved into the PDF;
expanding the integrand $1+k^2/Q^2+\dots$ for $k^2\ll Q^2$: the leading term gives the log UV behaviour of the PDF,
the $k^2/Q^2$ term gives $\mu_F^2/Q^2$ — the quadratic UV divergence of twist-4 operators at cutoff $\mu_F$; in DR
($\overline{\rm MS}$) this term is absent and replaced by the $p=1$ renormalon; "two manifestations of the same
quantity"; explicit in large $\beta_0$ (systematics in [71]); generally relies on factorization of renormalon residues
(Rem. C.69 (1)(2)), "hence only an argument, not a proof". □ (See E11.)

**Remark 14.2 (heuristic, not a theorem).** The renormalon structure of the LP term indicates the lowest power
correction: a renormalon at $p=1/2$ (event shapes) ⇒ power corrections start at $\Lambda/Q$. Thrust: the
non-perturbative soft function gives exactly such a $1/Q$ correction, $S_T=S_T^{\rm pert}\otimes F_{\rm np}$ (convolution in
$k$ of (13.3)); with $\int dk\,k\,F_{\rm np}(k)\equiv2\Omega_1$ ($\Omega_1\sim\Lambda$ per hemisphere), for
$Q\tau\gg\Lambda$ the distribution shifts by $2\Omega_1/Q$: $d\sigma/d\tau\simeq d\sigma_{\rm pert}/d\tau\,(\tau-2\Omega_1/Q)$ [72, 73].
For DY $d\sigma/dQ^2$ the lowest renormalon of the LP coefficient in large $\beta_0$ is at $p=1$, so power corrections
are expected from $1/Q^2$ ("once controversial") — consistent with Beneke–Braun 1995.

### §14.3 Threshold and Sudakov resummation (p. 82)

For $x\to1$ (DIS) or $\tau\to1$ (DY), $\alpha_s^n[\ln^{2n-1}(1-z)/(1-z)]_+$ spoil fixed order. Resummation rests on a
nested factorization: DIS threshold — final state = soft radiation + one narrow jet:
$$C(z)\xrightarrow{z\to1}H(Q^2,\mu)\,\big[J\otimes S\big](Q^2(1-z),\mu)+O\big((1-z)^0\big),\tag{14.4}$$
(the DIS soft function depends on how soft contributions are split with the large-$\xi$ PDF; in some organizations it is
entirely absorbed into the PDF and $C=H\cdot J$). DY threshold: no final-state jet, $C(z)\to H(Q^2,\mu)S(Q(1-z),\mu)$.
RG equations give exponentiation in Mellin space [74, 75]. Same method as §8, §13; the soft function is constrained and
≠ 1; rigour not above the outer collinear factorization (DIS B, DY B′); Thm. D.46 does not treat the threshold region,
whose region analysis (power counting of soft and collinear regions as $1-z\to0$) is an extra input of type Ass.
D.14. Likewise Cor. 10.5 is a factorization nested in $q_T\ll Q$.

**Figure 53 (p. 82).** (a) DIS ($z=Q^2/2p\cdot q\to1$): small final-state mass $Q^2(1-z)/z$ (checked), narrow jet $J$
(virtuality $\lesssim Q^2(1-z)/z$) + soft radiation $S$ (momenta $\sim Q(1-z)$) connected by Wilson lines; (b) DY

### §14.4 Next-to-leading power (p. 82)

Classic result: Low–Burnett–Kroll [143, 144]: for massive charged particles soft-photon emission at $O(k^0)$ is fixed
by the non-radiative amplitude and its derivatives; for massless emitters (and soft gluons) collinear regions add
contributions, the theorem must be modified. NLP leading logs $\alpha_s^n\ln^{2n-1}(1-z)$ (no $1/(1-z)$) resummed for DY
and Higgs (e.g. [145]). NLP factorization formulas often have endpoint-divergent convolutions (mechanism of §11.3),
removed by operator-level "rearrangement" [50]. Status "between B and C, depending on the process".

### §15 Summary (pp. 83–86)

Five lessons: (1) the core of collinear factorization is gauge invariance (Thm. 3.14: "fields of other particles are
pure gauge at collinear resolution"; an identity after summing graphs, valid to all orders, the most solid part; the
product structure needed with subtractions remains Ass. D.37). (2) Soft-factor cancellation relies on inclusiveness
($\mathcal U^\dagger\mathcal U=1$ only for complete final-state sums); measuring $q_T$ or event shapes gives new
factorizations with non-trivial soft functions; gaps or coloured-fragment observation in hadron collisions: unproven
and in some cases violated (§12.2, §12.3, Rem. E.54, E.55). (3) The Glauber region is key for hadron–hadron collisions:
Grammer–Yennie (3.11) fails there; only causality (poles on one side, contour deformation) and unitarity (cut sums)
remove it; DY holds by CSS standard and conditionally under App. E (cancellation of several non-commuting insertions on
one spectator in the non-abelian theory open); fails for TMD $pp\to h_1h_2X$. (4) Exclusive processes use colour
transparency instead of unitarity (Lemma 11.3), provided endpoint convergence. (5) Factorization is asymptotic;
"rigorous" theorems only at each order in $\alpha_s$ at LP, and even then conditional on App. D power counting.

**§15.1 Status of the three key links.** OPE (§5), properties of Collins' subtraction (§6.2), Glauber cancellation in DY
(§9) were first only sketched; App. C, D, E rewrite them precisely; open inputs → numbered assumptions; main results are
conditional theorems.
- *OPE (App. C).* In PT, with the Zimmermann–Hepp forest-formula convergence theorem as external input: chain expansion,
  Zimmermann identities, exact OPE identity of bilocal insertions fully proven (each coefficient an absolutely
  convergent "hard" integral); momentum-space power estimate of the remainder ("momentum-space form of Zimmermann's
  coordinate-space theorem, no verbatim proof in the literature") = input Ass. C.24; under it derivative estimates,
  uniqueness and scaling proven; massless-QCD OPE cited (Thm. C.35). Twist-2 GI operators are only
  $O^{(n)}_q,O^{(n)}_g$, and "twist parity = chirality", fully proven; "the $J^\mu J^\nu$ OPE needs only GI and alien
  operators" under BRST-symmetric renormalization; fixed-$n$ moments do not need sums over all spins.
  Non-perturbatively (p. 85) under four assumption groups (Wightman existence, composite operators and RG, NP OPE,
  asymptotic freedom: Ass. C.56–C.59) Thm. 5.3 is a theorem for even $n\ge n_0$ (Thm. C.66); $n<n_0$ (incl. momentum
  sum rule) needs a growth condition (Ass. C.60); the x-space Thm. 5.6 only in the weak sense (Cor. C.52).
- *Collins subtraction (App. D).* The naive induction of §6.2 fails term by term (one-loop Sudakov triangle and two-loop
  ladder counterexamples, Rem. 6.4). Under conditions (H1)–(H3) at the level of subtraction terms the remainder is
  $O(\lambda^aL^K)$ uniformly in $\lambda,\epsilon$ in an integral norm (Thm. D.10); chain expansion, pairing
  decomposition, product formula, scaling of all error terms (under (A3)(A4), Lemma D.25), $i0$ compatibility (single
  soft momentum through same-direction jet lines, Lemma D.20), the algebraic identity of the TMD square-root soft
  factor and one-loop zero-bin = soft factor fully proven (all orders: Ass. D.37, Prop. D.36); the four subtraction
  terms of the one-loop triangle computed exactly and checked numerically. Thm. D.46 (DIS, $e^+e^-\to hX$, thrust, DY,
  TMD, exclusive) conditional (DY also Ass. D.31, its Glauber part from App. E). Open core: (H1)–(H3) for general
  graphs at all orders; contour existence and integral power-counting envelopes; single chain terms are generally not
  integrable (Ex. D.1, D.48), so term-wise sufficient conditions cannot hold — only grouped expressions.
- *Glauber cancellation (App. E).* For non-abelian QCD at all orders, colour-singlet inclusive DY (collinear, and TMD
  measuring only the pair $q_T$): a conditional version — after summing over all cuts compatible with the measurement,
  the integrand is weakly equivalent in a scale-adapted window (after integrating soft light-cone energies, error
  $O(\lambda^a)$) to a "one-sided" form from light-cone-ordered PT (each uncut soft momentum appears in each jet only in
  initial-state energy denominators or an entire-function support); then simultaneous contour deformation out of the
  Glauber region, eikonal approximation, Ward identities → past-pointing Wilson lines; collinear soft factor 1, TMD:
  past-pointing staples and $\tilde S(b_T)$. Four assumptions: LCPT applicability (Ass. E.18), integrable envelope of
  fibre-integration errors (E.34), Glauber–soft mixing (E.33), simultaneous deformation in general configurations
  (E.44); plus App. D power-counting inputs. For the graph class of Rem. E.52 (soft subgraph only of lines directly
  connecting the two jets, no uncut soft line to active lines from the hard vertex), under the first two assumptions
  and App. D's (A3), (A4)(ii): one-sided rewriting (abelian, or ≤1 local insertion per spectator side), deformation
  targets outside the soft-angle region, and cancellation of spectator–spectator exchange on the window core
  (non-coincident attachments; window-edge terms in the soft-angle region, Prop. E.31(iii)) are fully proven — weak
  equivalence in the window; even for this class, Ass. E.44(b)(c) is needed to reach Prop. D.32; in the abelian theory
  spectator exchanges cancel on the window core for any number of exchanges. Open: non-abelian cancellation of several
  non-commuting equal-time insertions on one spectator ("strict Glauber approximation: two exchanges leave a residual
  $-\frac14C_A\chi^2\mathbf T_A\cdot\mathbf T_B$"), general soft subgraphs and configurations, genuinely soft-scale
  spectator couplings. Not an unconditional proof.

### App. A "Landau 方程的证明" (pp. 86–87)

Proves Thm. 3.1, Feynman-parameter representation, following [12, 9] (ELOP; Sterman's textbook).
*Parameter representation.*
$$\prod_{j=1}^N\frac1{D_j+i0}=(N-1)!\int_0^1\prod_jd\alpha_j\,\frac{\delta(1-\sum_j\alpha_j)}{[\mathcal D(\alpha,k)+i0]^N},\qquad\mathcal D(\alpha,k)=\sum_j\alpha_jD_j(k,p).$$
$\mathcal D=k^{\sf T}{\sf A}(\alpha)k-2{\sf B}(\alpha,p)\cdot k+{\sf C}(\alpha,p)$, ${\sf A}$ an $L\times L$ matrix (times the metric). Gauss
integral over $k$ for fixed $\alpha$ (Minkowski Gaussian defined by $+i0$; in DR UV convergent for
${\rm Re}(N-Ld/2)>0$, else by continuation in $d$); stationary point
$$\frac{\partial\mathcal D}{\partial k_a^\mu}\Big|_{k_0}=2\sum_j\alpha_j\varepsilon_{aj}l_{j\mu}(k_0)=0,\quad a=1,\dots,L,\tag{A.1}$$
result
$$I\propto\int\prod_jd\alpha_j\,\delta\big(1-\textstyle\sum\alpha_j\big)\,\frac{\mathcal U(\alpha)^{N-(L+1)d/2}}{[\mathcal F(\alpha)-i0\,\mathcal U(\alpha)]^{N-Ld/2}}\times(\text{numerator polynomial}),$$
$\mathcal U=\det{\sf A}$, $-\mathcal F/\mathcal U=\mathcal D(\alpha,k_0(\alpha))$; Symanzik polynomials homogeneous of degrees $L$, $L+1$.
*Necessary conditions.* Integral over the simplex; integrand singular only at $\mathcal F=0$ ($\mathcal U>0$ inside).
Multidimensional Cauchy: if near $\alpha^*$ with $\mathcal F(\alpha^*)=0$ there is an endpoint-preserving complex
deformation $\alpha_j\to\alpha_j-i\epsilon\eta_j(\alpha)$ with ${\rm Im}(\mathcal F-i0\mathcal U)<0$ maintained, $I$ is
not singular there. $\mathcal F(\alpha-i\epsilon\eta)=\mathcal F(\alpha)-i\epsilon\sum_j\eta_j\partial_j\mathcal F+O(\epsilon^2)$;
choose $\eta_j=\alpha_j\partial_j\mathcal F(\alpha)$: vanishes on the boundary $\alpha_j=0$, and
${\rm Im}\mathcal F=-\epsilon\sum_j\alpha_j(\partial_j\mathcal F)^2\le0$. The deformation fails iff
$\sum_j\alpha_j(\partial_j\mathcal F)^2=0$, i.e. for each $j$:
$$\alpha_j=0\quad\text{or}\quad\frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial\alpha_j}=0.\tag{A.2}$$
(The $\delta(1-\sum\alpha)$ constraint by homogeneity: integrand × measure is of degree 0 under $\alpha\to t\alpha$
(checked: $L(N-(L+1)d/2)-(L+1)(N-Ld/2)+N=0$); projective contour; Cheng–Wu; deformed contour need not stay on
$\sum\alpha_j=1$, only projectively homotopic.)
*Back to momentum space.* Envelope theorem: $\partial_j(-\mathcal F/\mathcal U)=\partial\mathcal D/\partial\alpha_j|_{k_0}=D_j(k_0)$ (checked
symbolically for the one-loop triangle). At $\mathcal F=0$: $\partial_j\mathcal F=-\mathcal UD_j(k_0)$; so (A.2) ⇔ for
each $j$ $\alpha_j=0$ or $D_j(k_0)=0$ — condition (i); (A.1) is condition (ii); homogeneity
$\sum_j\alpha_j\partial_j\mathcal F=(L+1)\mathcal F$ ⇒ (i) implies $\mathcal F=0$. □
**Remark A.1.** Necessary conditions only, ELOP-level; two gaps: (a) assumes $\mathcal U(\alpha^*)\neq0$;
$\mathcal U=\sum_T\prod_{j\notin T}\alpha_j$ (spanning trees) vanishes only when the lines with $\alpha_j=0$ contain a loop
(correct) — the sub-loop momentum → ∞ (UV region, "second-type singularities"), handled after renormalization;
(b) from "a correctly oriented deformation near each point" to analyticity requires uniform existence in a
neighbourhood and patching into a global homotopy — needs Pham's homological methods, not done. Whether a Landau point
gives a singularity (and its strength) is decided by power counting (§3.3); for massless theories in the physical region
solutions form manifolds (pinch surfaces) — source of collinear and soft divergences; normal-variable scaling decides
leading contributions.

## PART III. ERRORS (verified; page; correction)

**E1 (p. 74, argument for Thm. 12.3 — wrong colour bookkeeping; verified, `checks_R4.py`).** The text uses the eikonal
factor $\sum_j\mathbf T_j\,n_j^\mu/(n_j\cdot k+i\eta_j0)$ with a uniform numerator and $\eta_j=\pm1$ for initial/final,
together with colour conservation written as $\mathbf T_a+\mathbf T_b=\mathbf T_c+\mathbf T_d$, and claims that
$\eta_b\mathbf T_b+\eta_c\mathbf T_c+\eta_d\mathbf T_d$ is irreducible "because $\eta_b\neq\eta_{c,d}$". With exactly these
conventions the combination **is** reducible: $\eta_b(\mathbf T_b-\mathbf T_c-\mathbf T_d)=-\eta_b\mathbf T_a$ (checked on the
$qq\to qq$ colour space). The same literal bookkeeping would give the *same* sign for the Glauber phase in SIDIS
($\eta_c\mathbf T_c=-\mathbf T_a$) and DY ($\eta_b\mathbf T_b=-\mathbf T_a$), i.e. **no Sivers sign flip**, and it contradicts the
explicit Collins–Qiu result, whose eqs. (4.1)–(4.3) give the total Glauber factor $i\pi(g_1+2g_2)\delta(l^+)$ — the
initial-state line $k_2+l$ and the final-state line $k_4-l$ of the same flavour **add** ($2g_2$), they do not cancel.
*Correct version.* With a uniform eikonal numerator the colour charges must be the all-outgoing (Catani–Seymour)
ones, $\sum_{j}\mathbf T_j=0$ over all partons ($\mathbf T_a+\mathbf T_b+\mathbf T_c+\mathbf T_d=0$); then (with $k$ flowing into the
lines) initial lines have $1/(n\cdot k+i0)$, final lines $1/(n\cdot k-i0)$, and the Glauber part is
$\propto\mathbf T_b-\mathbf T_c-\mathbf T_d=\mathbf T_a+2\mathbf T_b$ — irreducible (residual norm 0.89 after projecting on $\mathbf T_a$).
Equivalently, in the "in = out" convention ($\mathbf T_a+\mathbf T_b=\mathbf T_c+\mathbf T_d$) the $\delta$-parts of initial and final
eikonal factors carry the **same** sign and the principal-value parts opposite signs, giving
$\mathbf T_b+\mathbf T_c+\mathbf T_d=\mathbf T_a+2\mathbf T_b$ (CQ: $g_1+2g_2$); SIDIS → $+\mathbf T_a$, DY → $\mathbf T_b=-\mathbf T_a$ (sign flip ✓).
Irreducibility arises because $b$ and $\{c,d\}$ enter with the same sign in the Glauber part, not because the $\eta$'s differ.

**E2 (p. 74, description of Rogers–Mulders and Collins–Qiu; checked against the papers).**
(i) CQ's model is **abelian** with two unrelated couplings $g_1,g_2$ and a massive gluon (CQ §II: "The gauge group is
abelian"); the text calls the counterexample model a "perturbative non-abelian gauge theory with coloured spectators" —
true for RM, not CQ. (ii) RM's counterexample is a **double transverse-spin (double Sivers) asymmetry** in a spectator model
with a **colour-singlet hard exchange**; graphs with one extra gluon vanish because ${\rm Tr}_C[t^a]=0$; two gluons from one
spectator on the far side of the hard part give the colour factor ${\rm Tr}_C[t^at^b]\,{\rm Tr}_C[t^bt^a]=T_F^2(N_c^2-1)$ and
force any generalized TMD factorization to contain colour-traced Wilson loops $U^{n_1}_{(\square)}$ (RM eqs. (25)–(30));
these loops give zero at single-gluon order, so generalized factorization predicts **zero** for graphs with one gluon from
each spectator, whereas the actual sum (RM Fig. 8, 16 graphs) has the non-zero colour factor $T_F^2(N_c^2-1)$ (RM eq. (34))
and a non-vanishing integral (RM eq. (38)). The text's "${\rm Tr}[t^at^b\cdots t^at^b\cdots]$ vs ${\rm Tr}[t^at^a\cdots]{\rm Tr}[t^bt^b\cdots]$,
difference ∝ $[t^a,t^b]$" is not what RM compute. (iii) The decisive unpolarized two-gluon counterexample to *standard*
$k_T$ factorization is Collins 2007 (arXiv:0708.4410), not cited.

**E3 (p. 76, NQS result misreported; checked against hep-ph/0501235 and hep-ph/0509021).** "These Wilson lines do not
change the numerical result at NNLO" is false. NQS find uncancelled IR poles at NNLO, $\propto\alpha_s^2\,v^2/\epsilon$
(PLB eq. (9)), "not matched by conventional NRQCD matrix elements"; the gauge-completed octet operators "generate
precisely the same pole terms", so the Wilson lines are **necessary** at NNLO (at relative order $v^2$) — the conventional
and gauge-completed matrix elements differ there. Correct statement: the gauge completion is immaterial at NLO and for
colour singlets; at NNLO it is required and restores factorization (and the completed matrix element is universal for
arbitrary recoiling jets at this order, PRD §6.6); beyond NNLO non-rotationally-invariant terms $\propto(q\cdot l)^2/P^2$
could require additional non-local operators (PLB p. 6; PRD §7).

**E4 (p. 73, Remark 12.2 — needs qualification).** "For one- and two-loop cross sections these phase terms cancel
after the colour sum." One loop: correct (anti-Hermitian, $P^{(1,R)}$ strictly factorized, CdFR (178)–(180)). Two
loops: CdFR find a Hermitian non-abelian operator in $P^{(2,R)}$ for ≥5 coloured partons, with "implications on the
non-abelian structure of logarithmically-enhanced terms (starting from NNLO)" (CdFR abstract); FSS show that its
expectation value vanishes on pure-QCD tree amplitudes (relatively real colour components; one step of their argument
unproven) but **not** when relative phases between colour flows exist (QCD+EW, s-channel W/Z width) — there violation
starts at two loops; for non-inclusive pure-QCD observables violation starts at three loops (FSS abstract). Mass-
singularity (PDF) factorization may be affected from N³LO (CdFR). GHMMS 2024 (JHEP 01 (2025) 090): effects "appear to
cancel at the level of cross sections in pure QCD up to NNLO".

**E5 (p. 72 item (c) and Table 1 row "B→π, B→ππ: C").** "BBNS QCD factorization holds at $O(\alpha_s)$ and LP [48]"
understates the literature: BBNS argued LP factorization to all orders in $\alpha_s$ (explicit one loop; two-loop IR
analysis in NPB 591 (2000) 313); SCET derivations at LP to all orders (Chay–Kim, NPB 680 (2004) 302; Bauer–Pirjol–Rothstein–Stewart,
PRD 70 (2004) 054015, with the charming-penguin caveat). For $B\to\pi$ the SCET factorization of $F^{B\pi}$ into soft
overlap + hard spectator is a theorem-level statement at LP (BPS 2003 abstract: "We prove a factorization theorem for
heavy-to-light form factors"; Beneke–Feldmann 2004); only the further factorization of $\xi_\pi$ fails. Lumping both
under C conflates [A]-level LP statements with [V]/[C] power corrections.

**E6 (p. 75 and Table 1, NRQCD decay "A\*: as rigorous as the OPE … conditional on App. C-type assumptions").** The decay
rate is a Minkowskian quantity at a fixed physical point; unlike DIS (moments via dispersion relations) or the Adler
function, there is no variable to continue to the Euclidean region, so a non-perturbative statement needs a local
quark–hadron duality assumption not contained in App. C's Euclidean OPE assumptions. Perturbatively it is [A] (BBL's own
words: rigour "comparable to DIS"; all orders in $\alpha_s$ at each order in $v$). Also, (12.4) suppresses the NRQCD cutoff
dependence of $f_n$ and $\langle\mathcal O_n\rangle$.

**E7 (p. 76, Conjecture 12.5 statement).** BBL's conjecture concerns all inclusive production processes with $m_Q$ as
hard scale and holds order by order in $v$ (finite set of LDMEs at each order); "for $p_T\gg m_Q$ **or** $\sqrt s\gg m_Q$,
at leading velocity power" is not the standard form and the $\sqrt s\gg m_Q$ alternative has no basis. For
$p_T\gg m_Q$ the established structure is a two-step one: QCD factorization into single-parton (LP) and double-parton
($m_Q^2/p_T^2$, NLP) fragmentation (Kang–Qiu–Sterman, PRL 108 (2012) 102002; Kang–Ma–Qiu–Sterman 2014), followed by
NRQCD factorization of the fragmentation functions — the setting of NQS. Not mentioned.

**E8 (pp. 73, 84 — "gap/non-global observables ($pp$): D" and "standard collinear factorization fails").** Over-stated /
out of date. What is established: strict collinear factorization of amplitudes [V]; SLLs exist at fixed order and have
been resummed. Becher–Hager–Jaskiewicz–Neubert–Schwienbacher, "Factorization restoration through Glauber gluons",

**E9 (p. 73, (12.2) — undefined/misleading $\mathbf T_{\rm coll}$; normalization checked against CdFR via our
`research/c_glauber`).** CdFR's one-loop space-like result contains $\frac{i}{\epsilon}4\mathbf T_2\cdot\mathbf T_3f_I(\epsilon;z_2)$
($f_I\to-\pi$) inside $\mathbf I_C\propto g_S^2c_\Gamma$; using CdFR (68), the non-factorizing part is
$\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}\frac{i\pi}{\epsilon}\sum_{j\in NC}{\rm sign}(s_{j2})\,\mathbf T_2\cdot\mathbf T_j$, where $\mathbf T_2$ is the charge of the

not the total colour of the collinear system. So the normalization $\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}i\pi\frac1\epsilon$ is right, but
$\mathbf T_{\rm coll}$ must be defined as $\mathbf T_2$; genuine colour correlations need ≥4 coloured partons (for 3 partons the term
is ∝ 1 but process dependent via the $i0$: DIS $f(\epsilon;z_2-i0)$ vs DY $f(\epsilon;z_2+i0)$).

**E10 (pp. 77–78, Remark 13.2 — "the correct prescription cannot be seen at the Lagrangian level").** Imprecise. The
Lagrangian identity holds for both $Y_n$ and $Y_n^{(+)}$, but the LO SCET Feynman rules themselves (the $i0$ of the
