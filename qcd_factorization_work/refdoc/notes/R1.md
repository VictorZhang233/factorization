## 1. Front matter (pp. 1–5)

### 1.1 Title and abstract (p. 1)
Title: 「QCD 中的因子化定理：证明、论证与适用边界」, dated 2026-10-10. The abstract says that factorization writes
cross sections as convolutions of perturbative hard coefficients with universal nonperturbative matrix elements
(PDFs, FFs, DAs, soft functions), with errors that are powers of $\Lambda_{\rm QCD}/Q$. It then classifies the
results into five classes:

- **(i)** Results from analyticity, unitarity and the OPE: the $e^+e^-$ total cross section and the DIS moments.
  - They are theorems at every order of PT. The perturbative OPE is cited: Zimmermann for massive theories, and
    the literature quoted in App. C for massless QCD.
  - "Strictly", the OPE holds at Euclidean non-exceptional momenta. The remainder estimates in forward on-shell
    partonic kinematics are listed as inputs in App. C.
  - Nonperturbatively they are theorems under explicit assumptions (existence of QCD, nonperturbative OPE). For
    DIS this covers even moments $n\ge n_0$.
- **(ii)** All-order, leading-power graphical proofs. Power counting on general graphs and contour deformation are
  listed as assumptions. Members:
  - collinear factorization in DIS and $e^+e^-\to hX$;
  - TMD factorization in SIDIS and $e^+e^-\to h_1h_2X$;
  - hard exclusive meson electroproduction (no longitudinal-photon qualifier).
- **(iii)** Drell–Yan, collinear and TMD. The key step, the Glauber cancellation, is proven only conditionally in
  the document.
- **(iv)** Conjectured or partially proven: hadron–hadron single-inclusive hadron and jet production; NRQCD
  production.
- **(v)** Proven broken:
  - TMD factorization for multi-hadron final states in hadron–hadron collisions;
  - hard diffraction in hadron colliders;
  - "相干性破坏" (coherence violation) in space-like collinear limits.

The common skeleton named in the abstract:
1. Landau + Coleman–Norton give the pinch surfaces.
2. Power counting "by Lorentz boost and dimensional analysis" selects the leading regions.
3. Grammer–Yennie plus Ward identities collect longitudinal gluons into Wilson lines. The document gives a "pure
   gauge field" (纯规范场) form of the argument.
4. Unitarity removes the soft factor.
5. Collins' subtraction method organizes the order-by-order subtraction formula.

Explicit calculations promised: the one-loop PDF, DIS coefficient functions, the Sudakov form factor, the
time-reversal proof of the Sivers sign flip, and colour transparency in the eikonal approximation. All diagrams are
in TikZ.

The three appendices C, D, E are described in long, nested sentences:
- **App. C.** Perturbative OPE in massive model theories, with the Zimmermann–Hepp forest-formula convergence
  theorem as the only external input and momentum-space remainder estimates as an explicit input. For massless
  QCD the perturbative OPE is cited. The DIS moments get a conditional nonperturbative theorem.
- **App. D.** A precise form of Collins' subtraction method:
  - a subtraction theorem proved under conditions at the level of subtraction terms;
  - a counterexample to the naive term-by-term induction;
  - conditional factorization theorems per process.
- **App. E.** A conditional reconstruction of the Glauber cancellation in colour-singlet inclusive DY, built on
  light-cone-ordered PT, unitarity of the sum over cuts and simultaneous contour deformation.
  - Complete proof only for one class of graphs. In that class the soft subgraph consists only of lines directly
    joining the two jets, and no uncut soft line attaches to active lines from the hard vertex. In the non-abelian
    case each spectator must also carry at most one local insertion.
  - The proof holds under two technical assumptions plus part of App. D, in a "scale-adapted window" outside the
    "soft-angle region", and in a weak-equivalence sense.
  - Even for this class, going from the Glauber cancellation to DY factorization needs part of the deformation
    assumption. The cancellation of non-commuting insertions on one parton in the non-abelian theory remains open.

Closing sentence: everything unproven is listed as a numbered assumption, so classes (ii) and (iii) are conditional
theorems.

## 2. §1 Introduction (pp. 6–9)

### 2.1 §1.1 Problem (p. 6)
There is a hard scale $Q\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}$ with $\alpha_s(Q)\ll1$. Claim at leading power:
$$
\sigma=\sum_{i,j}\int d\xi_A\,d\xi_B\,f_{i/A}(\xi_A,\mu)\,f_{j/B}(\xi_B,\mu)\,\hat\sigma_{ij}\Big(\xi_A,\xi_B,\frac Q\mu,\alpha_s(\mu)\Big)+\mathcal O\Big(\Big(\frac{\Lambda_{\rm QCD}}{Q}\Big)^p\Big)\qquad(1.1)
$$
with three properties:
- (a) $\hat\sigma_{ij}$ is independent of the hadrons, computable order by order with partonic external states,
  and IR finite order by order.
- (b) $f_{i/A}$ is a gauge-invariant operator matrix element, universal, and depends only on $A$, $i$, $\xi$, $\mu$.
- (c) The remainder is suppressed by a positive power $p$ that depends on the process.

Physical picture: no quantum interference between scales. The hard scattering takes $\sim1/Q$; internal hadron
dynamics takes $\sim1/\Lambda$ in the rest frame and is further time-dilated. The difficulties named are soft and
collinear logs in every graph, entanglement of gluons by the non-abelian interaction, and the "Glauber region".
Validity depends on inclusiveness and colour structure.

### 2.2 §1.2 Definition 1.1 — grades of rigor (pp. 6–7)
- **A (nonperturbative structure theorem).**
  - Depends only on Wightman-type general properties (Lorentz covariance, spectral condition, locality,
    unitarity) and the existence of the OPE.
  - Perturbative OPE: Zimmermann [17,18] (massive); massless QCD (Euclidean, non-exceptional) cited as Thm C.35.
  - App. C rebuilds the exact identities in massive model theories, with the forest-formula convergence theorem as
    the only input and the momentum-space remainder estimate as Assumption C.24.
  - Nonperturbatively, existence of the OPE cannot be proven. Under Assumptions C.56–C.59, App. C proves DIS moment
    factorization for all even $n\ge n_0$ (Thm C.66); $n<n_0$, including the momentum sum rule, also needs the
    growth assumption C.60.
  - Members: $e^+e^-$ total (§4); DIS moments (§5); inclusive quarkonium decays, "taking NRQCD as the EFT of QCD"
    (§12.5).
- **B (all-order PT proof).** For any Feynman graph, leading power has the form (1.1) with gauge-invariant
  operators and IR-finite hard coefficients.
  - The standard is that of Collins–Soper–Sterman: the combinatorial part (regions, Ward identities, subtraction)
    is regarded as complete, while power counting on general graphs and contour deformation (error estimates near
    pinch surfaces) are given as arguments.
  - App. D shows that even the combinatorial part needs sharpening. The naive term-by-term induction fails
    (Ex. D.1), and the subtraction theorem holds only under conditions (H1)–(H3) at the level of subtraction terms
    (Thm D.10).
  - App. D states the geometric–analytic input and (H1)–(H3) as Assumptions D.7 and D.14. Further inputs are
    Assumptions D.27 (cut-sum representation), D.37 (product structure of Ward identities) and D.43 (multiplicative
    renormalization). Under these it proves the factorized structure (Thm D.46). So B results are conditional
    theorems.
  - Nonperturbative extension rests on a "桥接假设" (bridge hypothesis): the PT structure of IR sensitivity (pinch
    surfaces and power counting) carries over, so all $\Lambda$ sensitivity sits in hadronic matrix elements.
  - Members: collinear DIS and $e^+e^-\to hX$ (§§6, 8); TMD for SIDIS and $e^+e^-\to h_1h_2X$ (§10); hard exclusive
    meson electroproduction (§11).
- **B′ (key step rests on delicate arguments).** Same structure as B, but one step (for DY, the Glauber
  cancellation) exists in the literature only for specific graph classes and as general arguments.
  - App. E gives a conditional reconstruction under Assumptions E.18, E.33, E.34, E.44 and the App. D inputs.
  - Only one graph class is proven completely without Glauber-specific assumptions: it uses only E.18, E.34 and
    App. D's (A3), (A4)(ii). The statement is a weak equivalence inside the scale-adapted window and outside the
    soft-angle region (Remark E.52).
  - Even then, going from the Glauber cancellation to DY factorization (Prop. D.32) needs E.44(b)(c). The
    non-abelian cancellation of several non-commuting insertions on one spectator (E.33(c)) is open.
  - Members: DY collinear and TMD (§9).
- **C (conjecture).** Low-order checks and heuristics, but no all-order proof, or a known gap. Members:
  $pp\to hX$ and $pp\to$ jets collinear; NRQCD production (§12).
- **D (proven broken).** An explicit counterexample or a general breaking mechanism. Members: TMD for
  $pp\to h_1h_2X$; hadron–hadron hard diffraction; amplitude factorization in space-like collinear limits (§12).

**Remark 1.2 (p. 8).**
- B proofs are perturbative. The remainder of (1.1) cannot be defined within PT.
- The leading-power series is (generally believed) asymptotic, and its Borel non-summability (IR renormalons)
  cancels against ambiguities of the power corrections. This is verified in large-$\beta_0$ and similar computable
  cases, but unproven in general (§14).
- Most precise form: "at every order in $\alpha_s$, and at leading power in $\Lambda/Q$, a certain equality holds."

### 2.3 §1.3 General strategy, five steps (p. 8)
1. Pinch analysis. Landau gives the pinch surfaces and Coleman–Norton the classical picture, which yields the
   reduced diagrams: $H$, jets $J_i$, soft $S$ (§3).
2. Power counting. Each jet joins $H$ through one physical parton plus any number of longitudinal gluons; soft
   lines join jets only through gluons; no soft line touches $H$ (§3.3).
3. Approximations. Collinear momenta are replaced by their large components in $H$; small components of soft
   momenta are dropped in jets (eikonal). This double counts in overlaps, so subtractions are needed (§6).
4. Ward identities turn longitudinal collinear gluons and eikonal soft gluons into Wilson lines, which makes the
   matrix elements gauge invariant (§3.5).
5. Remove the soft factor (unitarity gives $S=1$ for collinear factorization; it combines into TMD factors for TMD
   factorization), then organize everything with Collins' subtraction (§6.2, App. D).

For hadron–hadron collisions, step 3 fails in the Glauber region. One must first prove cancellation after the sum
over final states, or deform out of the region (App. E; §§9, 12).

### 2.4 §1.4 DIS parton picture (p. 8–9)
- $q=l-l'$, $Q^2=-q^2$, $x=Q^2/(2P\cdot q)$.
- Handbag: a quark with $\xi P$ absorbs the photon, and the on-shell condition $(\xi P+q)^2=0$ gives $\xi=x$ (target
  mass neglected).
- $F_2(x)=\sum_qe_q^2\,x\,f_{q/A}(x)$, summed over quarks and antiquarks. Stated only, not derived.
- QCD generalizes this at every order by defining PDFs with Wilson lines and introducing $\mu$ and log evolution.

## 3. §2 Conventions, kinematics, operator definitions (pp. 10–14)

### 3.1 §2.1 Light-cone coordinates (p. 10)
$$
g^{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(1,-1,-1,-1),\quad v^\pm=\frac{v^0\pm v^3}{\sqrt2},\quad v\cdot w=v^+w^-+v^-w^+-\boldsymbol v_T\cdot\boldsymbol w_T,\quad v^2=2v^+v^--\boldsymbol v_T^2\qquad(2.1)
$$
$$
n^\mu=(1,0,\boldsymbol 0_T),\ \ \bar n^\mu=(0,1,\boldsymbol 0_T),\ \ n\cdot\bar n=1,\ \ n\cdot v=v^-,\ \ \bar n\cdot v=v^+\qquad(2.2)
$$
in $(+,-,T)$ components, so $v^\mu=v^+n^\mu+v^-\bar n^\mu+v_T^\mu$. These are the same as our c_wilson (C4) vectors
$n_A,n_B$, normalized to 1, not to SCET's 2. The boost along $+z$ is
$$
v^+\to e^yv^+,\quad v^-\to e^{-y}v^-,\quad \boldsymbol v_T\to\boldsymbol v_T .\qquad(2.3)
$$
- $P_A=(P_A^+,M_A^2/(2P_A^+),\boldsymbol 0_T)$; $B$ moves along $-z$.
- $\gamma^\pm=(\gamma^0\pm\gamma^3)/\sqrt2$, with $(\gamma^\pm)^2=0$ and $\{\gamma^+,\gamma^-\}=2$.
- Projectors:
$$
\mathcal P_+=\tfrac12\gamma^-\gamma^+,\quad \mathcal P_-=\tfrac12\gamma^+\gamma^-,\quad \mathcal P_++\mathcal P_-=1 .\qquad(2.4)
$$
- Good components are $\psi_+=\mathcal P_+\psi$, bad ones $\psi_-=\mathcal P_-\psi$. For a fast $+z$ quark,
  $u(p)\simeq\sqrt{p^+}\times$(good only). Under (2.3) good components scale as $e^{y/2}$, bad ones as $e^{-y/2}$.
  All verified (§5, check 2).

  with points far from $x$ to the right; $W^{(+)}$ is future-pointing, $W^{(-)}$ past-pointing. In CONTEXT notation

### 3.4 §2.4 Operator definitions (pp. 12–14)
**Definition 2.1 (collinear PDF, unrenormalized), p. 12.**
$$
f_{q/A}(\xi)=\int\frac{dw^-}{4\pi}e^{-i\xi P^+w^-}\langle P|\bar\psi(0,w^-,\boldsymbol 0_T)\gamma^+W[w^-,0]\psi(0)|P\rangle,\quad W[w^-,0]=\mathcal P\exp\Big(ig\int_0^{w^-}dy^-A^+_a(0,y^-,\boldsymbol 0_T)t^a\Big)\qquad(2.11)
$$
The Wilson line is straight along $\bar n$; colour is contracted and spin averaged. The definition is identical to
CONTEXT. The gluon PDF is
$$
f_{g/A}(\xi)=\sum_{j=1,2}\int\frac{dw^-}{2\pi\xi P^+}e^{-i\xi P^+w^-}\langle P|F_a^{+j}(0,w^-,\boldsymbol 0_T)W^{\rm adj}_{ab}[w^-,0]F_b^{+j}(0)|P\rangle\qquad(2.12)
$$
which is identical to BLUEPRINT §0.4(5).

**Definition 2.2 (FF), p. 13.** With $k^+=p^+/z$ and $d=4-2\epsilon$:
$$
d_{h/q}(z)=\frac{z^{d-3}}{4N_c}\sum_X\int\frac{dw^-}{2\pi}e^{ik^+w^-}\,{\rm Tr}\big[\gamma^+\langle0|W_{\bar n}^{(+)\dagger}(w)\psi(w)|h(p),X\rangle\langle h(p),X|\bar\psi(0)W_{\bar n}^{(+)}(0)|0\rangle\big]\qquad(2.13)
$$
- $w=(0,w^-,\boldsymbol 0_T)$, Tr over colour and Dirac indices, hadron along $+z$ with $p_T=0$ (hadron frame).
- **The normalization was checked explicitly** (check 4). Taking "hadron" = quark and $X$ = one gluon at
  $O(\alpha_s)$ in $A^+=0$ gauge gives exactly $\frac{\alpha_s}{2\pi}C_F\frac{1+z^2}{1-z}\int dl_T^2/l_T^2$. This is
  the correct number density, so $z^{d-3}/(4N_c)$ is right.
- **Our BLUEPRINT §0.4(8)** states $1/(4N_cz)$ "in the frame with $p_{h,T}=0$". That gives the result times
  $1/z^2$, so our convention is wrong. The free-quark check $d_{q/q}=\delta(1-z)$ cannot detect this.

**Remark 2.3 (p. 13).**
- In $A^+=0$ gauge, $W=1$ and (2.11) becomes the light-front number operator, the number density of good-component
  quarks with fraction $\xi$.
- Light-cone gauge leaves residual transformations depending only on $x^+$ and $\boldsymbol x_T$. The $1/k^+$ poles
  need a prescription, and the prescription corresponds to boundary conditions at light-cone infinity.
- The collinear-PDF line $W[w^-,0]$ is a finite segment, equal to 1 in $A^+=0$ gauge, so it is independent of those
  boundary conditions (Remark 3.15).
- TMD fields have a transverse separation, so the line must go through infinity and the prescription changes the
  definition (§10).

**Proposition 2.4 (support), p. 13.** $f_{q/A}(\xi)=0$ for $|\xi|>1$. For $0<\xi\le1$ it is the quark number
density; for $-1\le\xi<0$, $f_{q/A}(\xi)=-f_{\bar q/A}(-\xi)$.

*Proof (pp. 13–14).*
1. Work in $A^+=0$ gauge and use $\gamma^0\gamma^+=\sqrt2\mathcal P_+$ (verified), so
   $\bar\psi\gamma^+\psi=\sqrt2\psi_+^\dagger\psi_+$.
2. Insert a complete set of states, use translations
   $\bar\psi(0,w^-,0_T)=e^{i\hat P^+w^-}\bar\psi(0)e^{-i\hat P^+w^-}$, and integrate over $w^-$:
   $$f_{q/A}(\xi)=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\sum_X\delta((1-\xi)P^+-P_X^+)|\langle X|\psi_+(0)|P\rangle|^2,$$
   which is verified, positive normalization factors dropped.
3. The spectral condition $P_X^+\ge0$ gives $\xi\le1$, and $f\ge0$ for the unrenormalized distribution.
4. For $\xi<0$, write $\bar\psi(w)\psi(0)=-\psi(0)\bar\psi(w)+$ anticommutator. On the light cone the anticommutator
   is $\propto\delta(w^-)$ and gives only a hadron-independent disconnected constant, removed by normal ordering.
   The same steps give $\delta((1+\xi)P^+-P^+_{X'})$, hence $\xi\ge-1$, and minus the antiquark density.
5. Jaffe [24] gives the equivalent graphical argument: the $k^-$ poles lie on both sides only if $|\xi|\le1$.

**Remark 2.5 (p. 14).** The "poles on both sides" argument also shows that PDFs depend only on light-cone
correlations, i.e. they are integrated over the parton's $k^-$ and $k_T$. This is the converse of "hard part depends
only on $k^+$".

## 4. §3 IR structure (pp. 14–27)

### 4.1 §3.1 Landau equations and pinches (pp. 14–15)
The $L$-loop scalar integral is
$$
I(p)=\int\prod_{a=1}^L\frac{d^4k_a}{(2\pi)^4}\frac{N(k,p)}{\prod_{j=1}^N(l_j^2-m_j^2+i0)}\qquad(3.1)
$$
with singular surfaces $D_j\equiv l_j^2-m_j^2=0$. By Cauchy, singularity can only come from (i) a pinch or (ii) an
endpoint. The text states that (ii) "does not occur for loop integrals (domain $\mathbb R^{4L}$; behaviour at
infinity is a UV problem)".

**Fig. 7 (p. 14).** $k^-$ plane with poles $a-i0$ and $b+i0$:
- (a) at finite separation the contour can be deformed;
- (b) poles squeeze the real axis from both sides, so the contour is pinched;
- same-side poles can always be avoided.

**Theorem 3.1 (Landau [10]), pp. 14–15.** A necessary condition for (3.1) to be possibly singular at external $p$
(in the physical region, as $i0\to0$) is the existence of real $k$ and $\alpha_j\ge0$, not all zero, with
$$
{\rm (i)}\ \alpha_jD_j(k,p)=0\ \forall j,\qquad {\rm (ii)}\ \sum_{j\in{\rm loop}\ a}\alpha_j\varepsilon_{aj}l_j^\mu=0\ \forall a.\qquad(3.2)
$$
Solutions form the pinch surfaces. *Proof:* App. A. The idea given here is that with Feynman parameters
$\mathcal D(\alpha,k)=\sum\alpha_jD_j$, the $k$ integral is pinched at the stationary point $\partial\mathcal D/\partial k_a^\mu=0$
(condition ii), and the $\alpha$ integral on the simplex is pinched at endpoints $\alpha_j=0$ or at
$\partial\mathcal D/\partial\alpha_j=D_j=0$ (condition i). See E10.

**Theorem 3.2 (Coleman–Norton [11]), p. 15.** Solutions of (3.2) correspond one-to-one to classical processes:
- lines with $\alpha_j\ne0$ are on-shell point particles with velocity $l_j^\mu/l_j^0$ and local collisions at
  vertices;
- $\Delta x_j^\mu=\alpha_jl_j^\mu$, and (ii) says the displacements around each loop sum to zero.

*Proof:* assign $x_v$ with $x_{v'}-x_v=\alpha_jl_j$; (ii) is single-valuedness of the positions; conversely define
$x_v$ along a spanning tree. $\alpha_j\ge0$ means propagation to the future along the momentum (for $l^0_j>0$).
$\alpha_j=0$ lines shrink to points (hard, localized subgraphs).

**Remark 3.3 (p. 15).** Two kinds of solution matter for massless particles.
- (a) Collinear: parallel on-shell massless lines (one line splitting into two co-moving lines) need no third line
  to bring them back.
- (b) Soft, $l_j\to0$. At $l_j=0$, $\alpha_jl_j=0$ for any $\alpha_j$. In the scaling limit $l_j\to0$,
  $\alpha_j\to\infty$ with $\alpha_jl_j$ finite, (ii) no longer constrains the relative position of the ends, while
  $\alpha_jl_j^2\to0$ keeps (i). A zero-momentum particle can "propagate" between any two points.
- "All other pinches are combinations of these two with hard vertices" (asserted, not proven).
- The scaling-limit description is correct; our c_regions §1.5 says the same.

**Corollary 3.4 (no pinch in the Euclidean region), p. 15.**
- Statement: if all external momenta are Euclidean (e.g. $q^2<0$ for the two-point function, or more generally
  $(\sum_{i\in S}p_i)^2<0$ for every non-empty proper subset $S$, so massless external lines are off shell), the
  integral has no pinch on the real $k$ contour.
- *Proof:* by Thm 3.2 a pinch is a classical process with on-shell, positive-energy particles. Their total momentum
  is timelike or lightlike, which contradicts spacelike partial sums. The only exception is a zero-momentum
  particle, but if a line has zero momentum and all lines at its ends are on shell, the external momenta must be on
  shell — a contradiction.
- **The corollary is false as stated** — see E1.

**Fig. 8 (p. 16).** Coleman–Norton space–time pictures ($z$ horizontal, $t$ vertical):
- (a) Collinear pinch $C_1$ of the Sudakov vertex (timelike $\gamma^*\to q\bar q$, $m\to0$). The off-shell antiquark
  $p_2+k$ shrinks into the vertex $O$; the quark $p_1-k$ and gluon $k$ run along the same light ray and rejoin at

- (b) Soft pinch: the scaling limit with $x_1-x_2$ spacelike.

### 4.2 §3.2 Example: regions of the Sudakov form factor (pp. 15–17)
$$
I=\int\frac{d^dk}{i\pi^{d/2}}\frac1{(-k^2)(-(p_1-k)^2)(-(p_2+k)^2)},\qquad -p_1^2=-p_2^2=m^2,\ \ -(p_1+p_2)^2=Q^2\gg m^2\qquad(3.3)
$$
- (3.3) is called "Euclidean kinematics, external lines off shell and spacelike" (see E3); the timelike case
  follows by analytic continuation.
- As $m\to0$ the Landau solutions are said to be:
  - the "hard point ($\alpha_k=0$, all lines off shell)" (see E2);
  - $C_1$ ($k\parallel p_1$), $C_2$ ($k\parallel p_2$), and the soft point $S$ ($k=0$).
- With $p_1$ along $+$, $p_2$ along $-$ and $\lambda=m/Q$, the regions scale as
$$
{\rm hard}:Q(1,1,1);\quad C_1:Q(1,\lambda^2,\lambda);\quad C_2:Q(\lambda^2,1,\lambda);\quad S:Q(\lambda^2,\lambda^2,\lambda^2).\qquad(3.4)
$$

**Proposition 3.5 (pole cancellation among regions), pp. 16–17.**
- Statement: in DR, write the leading power of (3.3) as $I=I_h+I_{c1}+I_{c2}+I_s$, each region expanded and
  integrated over all of $\mathbb R^d$ (method of regions, citing [16] = Beneke–Smirnov 1998). Then
$$
I_h\propto\frac1{Q^2}\Big(\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2}\Big)^\epsilon,\quad I_{c1,2}\propto\frac1{Q^2}\Big(\frac{\mu^2}{m^2}\Big)^\epsilon,\quad I_s\propto\frac1{Q^2}\Big(\frac{\mu^2Q^2}{m^4}\Big)^\epsilon,\qquad(3.5)
$$
  each with $1/\epsilon^2$ coefficients $c,-c,-c,c$ and $c=1$, so
$$
I=\frac1{Q^2}\Big[\ln^2\frac{Q^2}{m^2}+\mathcal O(\ln^0)\Big].\qquad(3.6)
$$
- *Proof:*
  1. Each region has one scale, so its $\mu$ dependence follows from dimensional analysis.
     - Hard: $m\to0$, scale $Q^2$.
     - $C_1$: the third propagator becomes $-2p_2^-k^+$ and $k^+$ scaling leaves $m^2$.
     - Soft: the eikonal propagators are $m^2+2p_1^+k^-$ and $m^2-2p_2^-k^+$, with scale
       $(m^2)^2/|2p_1^+2p_2^-|\simeq m^4/(2Q^2)$.
  2. Known hard region: $I_h=\frac1{Q^2}(\frac{\mu^2}{Q^2})^\epsilon\frac{\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(1-\epsilon)^2}{\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}\frac1{\epsilon^2}$,
     so $c=1$. This is correct, as checked on the page image.
  3. $I$ is finite for $m\ne0$. With $a=\ln(\mu^2/Q^2)$, $b=\ln(\mu^2/m^2)$ and soft log $2b-a$:
     - cancelling $1/\epsilon^2$ needs $\sum c=0$;
     - cancelling the logs in $1/\epsilon$ needs $c_ha+(c_1+c_2)b+c_s(2b-a)=0$, so $c_s=c_h$ and $c_1+c_2=-2c_h$;
     - symmetry gives $c_1=c_2=-c_h$.
  4. Double log: $\tfrac12[a^2-2b^2+(2b-a)^2]=(a-b)^2=\ln^2(Q^2/m^2)$. Single poles give only single logs.
- **Verified** (check 1):
  - $1/\epsilon^2$ coefficients $(1,-1,-1,1)$.
  - Closed forms: $H$; $C_{1,2}=-(\mu^2/m^2)^\epsilon\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma^2(-\epsilon)/\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)$;
    $S=-(\mu^2Q^2/m^4)^\epsilon\Gamma(1+\epsilon)\Gamma(\epsilon)\Gamma(-\epsilon)$.
  - Sum $=\frac1{Q^2}[\ln^2(Q^2/m^2)+\pi^2/3]$; the exact integral agrees to $O(t\ln^2t)$, $t=m^2/Q^2$, down to
    $t=10^{-10}$.
  - So (3.6) is right, and the omitted constant is $\pi^2/3$.

**Fig. 9 (p. 17).**
- Left: the one-loop Sudakov graph.
- Right: the $(\ln k^+,\ln k^-)$ plane with the corners hard $(0,0)$, $C_2$, $C_1$, $S$ $(\ln\lambda^2,\ln\lambda^2)$;
  the diagonal $k^+k^-\sim m^2$ through the "soft centre" $(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)$; the shaded triangle

**Remark 3.6 (p. 17).** The example shows all elements of factorization:
- (i) hard depends only on $Q$;
- (ii) collinear depends only on $m$ and not on the other leg;
- (iii) soft depends on the combination $m^4/Q^2$ (soft–collinear coupling), so the form factor is not simply
  hard × collinear × collinear and a soft function is needed;
- (iv) each region has UV and IR poles and the sum is finite;
- for collinear factorization $S=1$ by unitarity (Prop. 8.4), but not for TMDs.

$$
\begin{array}{lll}

**Lemma 3.7 (jet–hard power counting), p. 18.** Setup: jet $J$ along $+$ joined to $H$ by $n_F$ quark lines and
$n_B$ gluon lines (their propagators belong to $J$), with fixed external lines. Among the gluons, $n_+$ contract with
$H^-$ ($J^+H^-$, longitudinal), $n_T$ with transverse components, and $n_-$ with $H^+$; quarks are taken as good
components. Then $J\otimes H\sim\lambda^{p_J}$ with
$$
p_J=n_F+n_T+2n_--c_J ,\qquad(3.8)
$$
where $c_J$ depends only on the fixed external lines, and each bad-component quark adds $+1$.

*Proof (pp. 18–19).*
1. Boost with $y=\ln\lambda$. All components become $O(\lambda Q)$, virtualities $O(\lambda^2Q^2)$, and $M$,
   $\Lambda$ are $O(\lambda Q)$. So $J$ has the single scale $\lambda Q$ and scales as $(\lambda Q)^{D_J}$.
2. An amputated connected Green function has dimension $4-E_b-\tfrac32E_f$. Unamputated lines toward $H$ add $-2$
   (gluon) and $-1$ (quark), so $D_J=4-3n_B-\tfrac52n_F-d_{\rm ext}$.
3. Boosting back gives factors $\lambda^{-1}$ per upper $+$, $\lambda^{+1}$ per upper $-$, $\lambda^{\mp1/2}$ per
   good/bad spinor index.
4. There are $n_F+n_B-1$ J–H loops, each with measure $\lambda^4$; $H\sim\lambda^0$.
5. Sum: $p_J=4(n_F+n_B-1)+(4-3n_B-\tfrac52n_F-d_{\rm ext})-n_++n_--\tfrac12n_F+\dots=n_F+n_T+2n_--c_J$.

Verified (check 3); identical to our c_regions §2.4 with $f^g+t+2u+2f^b-e_J$.

**Corollary 3.8 (leading jet connections), p. 19.**
- Statement: the minimum of $p_J$ over connections has exactly one physical parton (a good-component quark or a
  transverse gluon) per amplitude entering $H$, plus any number of longitudinal gluons. For the DIS forward
  amplitude ($c_J=2$), $(n_F,n_T)=(2,0)$ or $(0,2)$ give $p_J=0$, and "all other combinations are suppressed by at
  least $\lambda^1$".
- *Proof:*
  - $p_J$ is independent of $n_+$ and increases with $n_F$, $n_T$, $n_-$.
  - Fermion number makes $n_F$ even.
  - "Colour singlet and angular momentum exclude $n_F=n_T=0$": with only longitudinal gluons, they decouple by the
    Ward identity, so there is no hard scattering.
  - Hence the minimal non-trivial combinations are $(2,0)$ (quark) and $(0,2)$ (gluon); $c_J=2$ so that the parton
    model is $O(\lambda^0)$.
- **Incomplete or wrong** — see E4.

**Lemma 3.9 (soft subgraph), p. 20.** Setup: $S$ has $N_b$ soft gluon lines and $N_f$ soft quark lines; $E_J^B$
($E_J^F$) soft gluon (quark) ends attach to jets and $E_H^B$ ($E_H^F$) to $H$. Including the extra jet propagators,
$$
p_S=2E_H^B+3E_H^F+E_J^F .\qquad(3.9)
$$
Soft gluons attached only to jets are not suppressed; soft quarks and soft lines attached to $H$ are suppressed.

*Proof:*
- Ingredients: measure $\lambda^8$, gluon propagator $\lambda^{-4}$, quark propagator $\lambda^{-2}$, three-gluon
  vertex $\lambda^2$, other vertices $\lambda^0$; each jet attachment adds a jet propagator $\lambda^{-2}$ with an
  $O(1)$ numerator $\sim p^\mu$; attachments to $H$ add no singular denominator.
- Count: $L_S=N_b+N_f-V_S$, so
  $p_S=8L_S-4N_b-2N_f-2(E_J^B+E_J^F)+2V_3$.
- Line ends: $2N_b=3V_3+4V_4+V_{qg}+E_J^B+E_H^B$ and $2N_f=2V_{qg}+E_J^F+E_H^F$, which gives (3.9).
- Verified (check 3), and identical to our formula. The "$O(1)$ numerator" argument is only literally true for
  soft gluons, but the result is right for soft quarks too.

### 4.4 §3.4 Collinear approximation, soft approximation, Grammer–Yennie (p. 23)
The approximation operator is $T_R$.

**Collinear approximation (acting on $H$).** For an $A$-collinear gluon, with $\hat k=(k^+,0,\boldsymbol 0_T)=k^+n$:
$$
H_\mu(k)J_A^\mu(k)\simeq H^-(\hat k)J_A^+(k)=H_\mu(\hat k)\frac{\hat k^\mu\bar n_\nu}{\bar n\cdot k}J^\nu_A(k)\equiv H_\mu(\hat k)K^\mu{}_\nu(k)J^\nu_A(k)\qquad(3.10)
$$
Here $K$ is the longitudinal ("scalar") projection, and there is no $i0$. For quark lines the approximation is of
the form $H(\hat k)\mathcal P_+J(k)$.

**Soft approximation (acting on jets).** A soft gluon $k\sim\lambda^2Q$ on $J_A$ gives
$S_\mu J_A^\mu\simeq(n\cdot S)(\bar n\cdot J_A)$, and $k\cdot J_A\simeq k^-J_A^+$, with the dropped term
$k_T\cdot J_{AT}$ of relative size $\lambda$:
$$
S_\mu(k)J_A^\mu(k)\simeq S_\mu(k)\frac{n^\mu k_\nu}{n\cdot k\pm i0}J_A^\nu(k).\qquad(3.11)
$$
- This is called "the K part of the Grammer–Yennie decomposition [15]": $g^{\mu\nu}=K^{\mu\nu}+G^{\mu\nu}$,
  $K^{\mu\nu}=n^\mu k^\nu/(n\cdot k)$, with $G$ suppressed in the leading region. See E9 on the attribution.
- On the $J_A$ side the polarization is $\propto k^\nu$, so a Ward identity applies.
- In the Glauber region, $k\cdot J_A\simeq k^-J^+_A$ fails and (3.11) fails.

**Fig. 15 (p. 24).**
- (a) Collinear approximation: H and $H(\hat k)$, with the arrow-gluon contracted with $\hat k^\mu$.
- (b) Grammer–Yennie: $S\,g\,J=S\,K\,J+S\,G\,J$, where the K part goes to an $n$-Wilson line via the Ward identity,
  and the G part is suppressed by $\lambda$ in the soft region but not in the Glauber region.

### 4.5 §3.5 Ward identity: longitudinal gluons into Wilson lines (pp. 23–27)
The introduction states that the proof uses a "pure gauge field". Its second step identifies the sum over
attachments in ordinary gauges with background-field gauge covariance. For $H$ with internal gluons this needs
Slavnov–Taylor identities with ghosts, which are cited ([1] ch. 11, Remark 3.16), not re-proven.

**Theorem 3.14 (decoupling of longitudinal gluons), p. 23.** Let $H$ be the hard subgraph of a colour-singlet
current, in the collinear approximation (3.10), with $n$ $A$-collinear longitudinal gluons ($k_1..k_n$) attached
and a pair of $A$-collinear quark external lines ($\hat l$, $\hat l'$). Summing over all attachments to $H$,
including its external quark lines, gives $H$ without longitudinal gluons times an $\bar n$ Wilson line carrying
all the gluons:
$$
\sum_{\text{attachments}}H^{-\cdots-}_{a_1\cdots a_n}(\hat k_1,\dots,\hat k_n;\hat l)=H(\hat l_{\rm tot})\otimes\big[\bar n\text{ Wilson line, $n$-gluon vertex}\big]_{a_1\cdots a_n}.\qquad(3.12)
$$
In coordinate space, the quark field entering $H$ becomes $\psi(y)\to W[-\infty,y^-]\psi(y)$ (or $W[+\infty,y^-]$).

*Proof (pp. 23–25), three steps.*
1. **Step 1 — pure gauge.**
   - $H$ depends only on $\hat k$ and couples only to $A^+$, so its vertices see
$$
\mathcal A^\mu_{\rm eff}(y)=n^\mu\mathcal A(y^-),\qquad \mathcal A(y^-)=\int\frac{dk^+}{2\pi}e^{-ik^+y^-}\tilde A^+(k^+)\qquad(3.13)
$$
     which has only a $+$ component and depends only on $y^-$.
   - Its only covariant component is $\mathcal A_-$, and $\partial_\mu\mathcal A_-\ne0$ only for $\mu=-$. Hence
     $F_{--}=0$, $F_{\mu-}=\partial_\mu\mathcal A_-=0$ for $\mu\ne-$, and the commutator vanishes, so
     $F_{\mu\nu}[\mathcal A_{\rm eff}]=0$. This is correct.
   - Explicitly,
$$
U(y^-)=\mathcal P\exp\Big(ig\int_{-\infty}^{y^-}dz^-\mathcal A(z^-)\Big),\quad \partial_-U=ig\mathcal AU,\qquad(3.14)
$$
     and $\mathcal A_{\rm eff\,\mu}=-\frac ig(\partial_\mu U)U^\dagger$, the gauge transform of zero (with
     $A\to UAU^\dagger-\frac ig(\partial U)U^\dagger$). Verified consistent with (2.5)–(2.6).
2. **Step 2 — gauge covariance.**
   - In background-field gauge, a Green function in the pure-gauge background with external $\psi$ equals the
     zero-background Green function with $\psi\to U^\dagger\psi$. The other end is the gauge-invariant current, so
     nothing else transforms.
   - Caveat stated in the text: the original graphs are in Feynman gauge, whose three- and four-gluon couplings and
     gauge fixing differ from background-field ones.
   - For $H$ with only quark lines and colour-singlet vertices (Fig. 16), step 2 is checked directly via the
     telescoping (3.15).
   - With internal gluons the two computations agree only after summing all attachments, which needs BRST
     Slavnov–Taylor identities with ghosts. This is cited ([1] ch. 11, [2]).
3. **Step 3 — expansion.**
   - $U^\dagger(y^-)\psi(y)|_{\mathcal A^1}=-ig\int_{-\infty}^{y^-}dz^-\mathcal A\psi\to(-igt^a)\frac i{k^++i0}\cdots=\frac{gt^a}{k^++i0}\cdots$.
     Verified, with $k^+$ flowing in.
   - This is the eikonal propagator $1/(\bar n\cdot k)$ with vertex $\bar n^\mu t^a$, i.e. the Wilson-line rule.
   - Higher orders give $\prod_i1/(k_1^++\dots+k_i^++i0)$ with ordered colour matrices.

**Remark 3.15 (boundary conditions and $i0$), p. 25.**
- The lower limit $\pm\infty$ in (3.14) is arbitrary: the two choices differ by a "constant colour matrix
  $U(+\infty)$, i.e. a global gauge transformation" (see E7).
- For the collinear PDF the two Wilson lines combine, $U(w^-)U^\dagger(0)=W[w^-,0]$, so (2.11) is unique. In
  momentum space the $\pm i0$ make no difference after the $k^+$ integration.
- For TMDs a transverse link at infinity is needed, and future versus past matters (§10).

**Remark 3.16 (relation to graph-by-graph Ward identities), p. 26.** At tree level:
$$
\frac{i(\slashed l+\hat{\slashed k})}{(l+\hat k)^2}(ig\hat{\slashed k}t^a)\frac{i\slashed l}{l^2}=-gt^a\Big[\frac{i\slashed l}{l^2}-\frac{i(\slashed l+\hat{\slashed k})}{(l+\hat k)^2}\Big]\qquad(3.15)
$$
- (3.15) follows from $\hat{\slashed k}=(\slashed l+\hat{\slashed k})-\slashed l$, and is verified numerically.
- Summing positions on a quark line in $H$ telescopes. The end terms combine with $J$ propagators into the eikonal
  factor $1/\hat k^+$.
- In the non-abelian case, attachments to internal gluons and ghosts need BRST Slavnov–Taylor identities ([1]
  ch. 11, [2]). The pure-gauge argument gives the final path-ordered form, while its step 2 relies on these
  identities.
- In App. D, the validity of Ward identities on integrands already modified by other approximation operators, and
  the product structure, is Assumption D.37.

**Corollary 3.17 (soft Wilson lines), p. 26.** In the soft approximation (3.11), soft gluons on $J_A$, summed over
attachments, equal attachments to an $n$ Wilson line (along $A$'s own direction) times $J_A$ without soft gluons. So
the soft subgraph is a vacuum expectation value of Wilson lines, e.g. for two jets
$$
S=\frac1{N_c}\langle0|{\rm Tr}\big[W_{\bar n}^\dagger W_n\big]\cdots\big[W_n^\dagger W_{\bar n}\big]|0\rangle .\qquad(3.16)
$$
This is schematic: the "$\cdots$", the positions, the ordering/cut and the conjugations are unspecified (see E8).

*Proof:* as for Thm 3.14 with roles exchanged. $J_A$ sees the soft field only through $k^-$, so the field depends
only on $y^+$ and has only the component $n\cdot A=A^-$ ($\mathcal A_{\rm eff}\propto\bar n^\mu$). It is pure gauge at
$J_A$'s resolution, and the Wilson line is along $n$. The colour-singlet external hadron passes its colour index at
the active parton to the Wilson line, and the step-2 caveat applies.

## 6. ERRORS (verified; page, problem, correction)

**E1 (p. 15, Corollary 3.4 and its proof) — false as stated. Severity: moderate.** The doc relies on this corollary
for its "A-level" Euclidean arguments.
- Zero-momentum massless lines satisfy the Landau equations for any external kinematics: $\alpha_j>0$, $l_j=0$ and
  all other $\alpha=0$ solve (3.2). Example: the doc's own integral (3.3) with spacelike invariants has
  $\mathcal F=m^2\alpha_0(\alpha_1+\alpha_2)+Q^2\alpha_1\alpha_2$, which vanishes, together with all
  $\alpha_j\partial_j\mathcal F$, at the three corners of the simplex ($k=0$, $k=p_1$, $k=-p_2$).
- The proof's exception clause covers only zero-momentum lines whose neighbours are on shell. It misses
  zero-momentum lines attached to off-shell ($\alpha=0$) lines.
- Correct statement: "in the Euclidean non-exceptional region the only Landau solutions are sets of massless lines
  carrying zero momentum. These are IR convergent by power counting (Lowenstein–Zimmermann-type IR power counting),
  so the integral is IR finite and real-analytic there. For massive internal lines, $\mathcal F>0$ on the closed
  simplex and there is no solution at all."
- Our c_regions §1.4 Remark (a) and §1.8 have it right ("Massless graphs always have the trivial corner solutions …
  power counting decides").

**E2 (p. 16, list of Landau solutions; p. 17, Fig. 9 caption) — the "hard point ($\alpha_k=0$, all lines off
shell)" is not a Landau solution.**
- With all lines off shell, (i) forces all $\alpha_j=0$, which the theorem excludes. The hard region is the absence
  of a pinch (c_regions §1.6: "edge $\alpha_0=0$ and interior: no solution for $Q^2\neq0$ — this is the hard
  configuration").
- The list also omits the soft-quark corners $k=p_1$, $k=-p_2$ (zero-momentum quark lines, power suppressed).

**E3 (p. 16) — "取欧氏运动学（外线离壳且类空）" is a misnomer, and Prop. 3.5's proof rests on an unproven
premise.**
- With $p_1^2=p_2^2=-m^2$ and $(p_1+p_2)^2=-Q^2$, $Q^2\gg m^2$ cannot be realized by Euclidean vectors, since then
  $|p_1+p_2|_E^2\le4m^2$. The invariants are spacelike ($\mathcal F>0$) but the momenta are Minkowskian.
- So neither Cor. 3.4 nor the Euclidean large-momentum theorems (Smirnov) cover the Sudakov limit. "Sum of regions =
  leading power" has no general proof here (Semenova–Smirnov–Smirnov 2019 do not cover the multi-facet case;
  c_regions §3.9, note 8).
- Prop. 3.5's "proof" therefore rests on three assumptions:
  - (a) the regions expansion holds, cited only;
  - (b) each region's Laurent series starts at $1/\epsilon^2$ with the single-scale form $e^{\epsilon L}(c/\epsilon^2+d/\epsilon+\dots)$,
    assumed;
  - (c) the $C_1$ region integral exists. In fact it has no common convergence strip in $\epsilon$ and is defined by
    analytic continuation of separately convergent factors (c_regions §3.2).
- The conclusion is true (verified), and the constant $\pi^2/3$ is omitted. Correction: compute all four regions
  explicitly, state the status [P] (verified at one loop), and give the constant.

**E4 (p. 19, Corollary 3.8; inherited by Thm 3.12's "$p(R)\ge0$" on p. 22) — super-leading configurations omitted.
Severity: moderate–high for the gluon channel.**
- By (3.8) with $c_J=2$: $(n_F,n_T)=(0,0)$ has $p_J=-2$ and $(0,1)$ has $p_J=-1$. So the claim "the minimum of $p_J$
  is at one physical parton per side; all other combinations are suppressed by $\ge\lambda$" is false.
- The proof disposes of $(0,0)$ by a one-line Ward-identity remark and never mentions $(0,1)$. Note $n_F=0$ is even,
  so fermion-number conservation does not exclude it.
- In Feynman gauge these graphs are individually enhanced. They cancel only in gauge-invariant sums (Ward identity +
  colour neutrality; CSS 1989 §8.6; our blueprint T3.10), and their $O(\lambda)$, $O(\lambda^2)$ remainders are
  leading power.
- Those remainders combine into the gauge-invariant gluon operator $F^{+j}=\partial^+A^j-\partial^jA^++\dots$. For
  a gluon as the only active parton, $k\cdot H=0$, the K term vanishes, and the G term
  $\propto k^+A^j-k^jA^+$ is leading (c_wilson §6.2 "Exception — gluon as active parton").
- So the bookkeeping "$n_T$ transverse gluons" is not gauge-invariant in the gluon channel. Our c_regions P7 lists
  "gluon jet with one transverse and only scalar gluons: $-\tfrac12$, super-leading" (Sterman units).
- Correction: state the super-leading configurations explicitly and prove (or cite, [A]) their cancellation, with the
  leftover building $F^{+j}F^{+j}$.

**E5 (p. 11, Fig. 4) — Wilson-line Feynman rules ambiguous and, with the doc's (2.7), inconsistent.**
- The table gives vertex $igv^\mu t^a$ and propagator $i/(v\cdot k\pm i0)$, "± future/past", without a
  momentum-routing convention and without distinguishing $W$ from $W^\dagger$.
- First-order expansion of all four lines (check 2):
  - The doc's rule is exactly the rule for $W_v^{(\pm)\dagger}(x)=W[\pm\infty v,x]$ with $k$ flowing **out** of
    the line into the gluon.
  - For the objects actually defined in (2.7), $W_v^{(\pm)}(x)=W[x,\pm\infty v]$, one gets the opposite vertex sign
    $-igv^\mu t^a$ with $k$ out. With $k$ in, the vertex is $+ig$ but the $i0$ assignment is reversed (past
    $\to+i0$).
- The table's own last row (incoming quark absorbing $k$: $-gp^\mu/(p\cdot k+i0)$) is a past-pointing line with

- Correct universal rule (c_wilson §2.5, [R]): vertex $igt^av^\mu$ for orientation $+v$ and $-igt^av^\mu$ for

  segment reaching infinity. Equivalently, with $n$ future-pointing and $K$ the momentum flowing in between the
  segment and the infinite end: past $i/(n\cdot K+i0)$, future $-i/(n\cdot K-i0)$.

**E6 (p. 23 eq. (3.10); p. 25 step 3) — $i0$ missing or not derivable.**
