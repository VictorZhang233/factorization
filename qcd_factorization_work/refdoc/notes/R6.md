(i) shows by the simplest example why the naive term-by-term induction of 注 6.4 fails (D.1), sharpens (P1)(P2) to

(iv) one-loop Sudakov triangle: all four subtraction terms as (after $k^-$ residues) absolutely convergent iterated integrals for
$0<\epsilon<1$, computed exactly without "scaleless integrals vanish"; two-loop ladder: product structure of the remainder.

## 1. D.1 Problems of the naive induction (pp.128–130)

**Set-up (off-shell triangle (3.3) of Prop. 3.5).** Light-cone components $(+,-,T)$:
$p_1=(P,-m^2/2P,\mathbf 0_T)$, $p_2=(m^2/2P',-P',\mathbf 0_T)$, so $p_1^2=p_2^2=-m^2$, $\tilde Q^2\equiv2PP'=Q^2+O(m^2)$.
(Exactly: $Q^2\equiv-(p_1+p_2)^2=\tilde Q^2+2m^2+m^4/\tilde Q^2$ — verified.)
$$
D_g=-k^2,\quad D_1=-(p_1-k)^2=m^2+2p_1\!\cdot\!k-k^2,\quad D_2=-(p_2+k)^2=m^2-2p_2\!\cdot\!k-k^2\qquad\text{(D.1)}
$$
all with $-i0$; $\Gamma=1/(D_gD_1D_2)$; measure $\mu^{2\epsilon}\int d^dk/(i\pi^{d/2})$. Regions $S<C_1,C_2<H$. With
$$
e_1\equiv m^2+2Pk^-,\ h_1\equiv2Pk^-,\ t_1\equiv2Pk^--k^2;\quad e_2\equiv m^2+2P'k^+,\ h_2\equiv2P'k^+,\ t_2\equiv2P'k^+-k^2;
$$
$$
T_S:(D_1,D_2)\mapsto(e_1,e_2),\quad T_H:(D_1,D_2)\mapsto(t_1,t_2),\quad T_{C_1}:D_2\mapsto h_2,\quad T_{C_2}:D_1\mapsto h_1\qquad\text{(D.2)}
$$
($i0$ as in the original denominators). Each $T_R$ also acts on already-approximated denominators: $T_He_i=h_i$, $T_St_i=h_i$,
$T_{C_1}e_2=h_2$, ... (verified: $T_S$ drops $-(m^2/P)k^+-k^2$ from $D_1$; $D_i|_{m=0}=t_i$; $h_i-t_i=k^2=-D_g$).

**Example D.1 (counterexample to term-by-term claims).** In $C_1$, $k\sim Q(1,\lambda^2,\lambda)$, $\lambda=m/Q$:
$D_g\sim D_1\sim\lambda^2Q^2$, $D_2\sim Q^2$.
- (a) *Single approximants of incomparable regions are not suppressed, not even integrable.* $T_{C_2}\Gamma=1/(D_gh_1D_2)$:
  $T_{C_2}$ removes the mass regulator of line 1; the $D_g$ pole $k^-=(k_T^2-i0)/2k^+$ (lower) and the $h_1$ pole $k^-=+i0$
  (upper) pinch at $k^-=k_T=0$, $k^+\sim Q$ → massless collinear divergence $\int d^{2-2\epsilon}k_T/k_T^2$ (only $\epsilon<0$).
  Only $C_{C_2}\Gamma=T_{C_2}(\Gamma-T_S\Gamma)=\frac1{D_gh_1}(\frac1{D_2}-\frac1{e_2})$ is suppressed; using
  $e_2-D_2=(m^2/P')k^-+k^2=(m^2/P')k^--D_g$:
  $$C_{C_2}\Gamma=\frac{m^2}{2PP'}\frac1{D_gD_2e_2}-\frac1{h_1D_2e_2}$$ (verified), each term contains only one of the two
  pinching poles; the first carries $m^2$, the second occupies a small $(k^-,\mathbf k_T)$ phase space in $C_1$.
- (b) *The approximation must be accurate on subtraction terms too.* $C_S\Gamma=T_S\Gamma=1/(D_ge_1e_2)$ is leading in $C_1$
  ($\sim\lambda^{-4}Q^{-6}$); so "after subtracting smaller regions $T_{C_1}$ is accurate" needs $T_{C_1}$ accurate on $T_S\Gamma$:
  $(1-T_{C_1})T_S\Gamma=\frac1{D_ge_1}(\frac1{e_2}-\frac1{h_2})$, relatively $O(m^2/Qk^+)$.
- (c) *Single chain terms are generally not in $L^1$.* $T_{C_1}\Gamma=1/(D_gD_1h_2)$ at $k=(a^2\kappa^+,\kappa^-,a\boldsymbol\kappa_T)$,
  $a\to0$ (gluon collinear to $p_2$, rapidity divergence of the $\bar n$ eikonal line): integrand $\sim a^{-4}$, measure
  $\sim a^{4-2\epsilon}$ → needs $\epsilon<0$; genuine pinch (pole of $h_2$ at $k^+=+i0$ vs $D_g$ pole $k^+=(k_T^2-i0)/2k^-$).
  UV direction $k=(\kappa^+,a\kappa^-,\sqrt a\boldsymbol\kappa_T)$, $a\to\infty$ → needs $\epsilon>0$. Hence $T_{C_1}\Gamma$ is not
  absolutely integrable for any $\epsilon$; same for the scaleless $T_{C_1}T_S\Gamma$; the massless on-shell triangle $T_H\Gamma$
  converges only for $\epsilon<0$, incompatible with $\epsilon>0$ needed by UV convergence of the subtraction terms. Only the
  $C_R\Gamma$ and certain grouped combinations are integrable. (All scalings re-derived by R6: correct.)

**Lessons (p.129–130).** (a) object of accuracy: $T_{R_0}$ must be accurate on $X_{R_0}\equiv\Gamma-\sum_{R<R_0}C_R\Gamma$ ⇒ (H1);
(b) larger/incomparable regions can only be suppressed as whole subtraction terms ⇒ (H2); (c) integrability: only subtraction
terms and grouped combinations may enter norms; single chain terms get "values" only by continuation (Ass. D.17) ⇒ (H3), (A1);
(d) neighbourhood size: fixed-size neighbourhoods give $O(1)$ relative error at their boundary; must take $\lambda$-dependent
neighbourhoods of size $\lambda^\beta$, $0<\beta<1$, so that adjacent approximations are simultaneously accurate in the
transition zone (Def. D.5); (e) contour: the soft approximation holds only after deforming away from the Glauber region
(注 3.11), and approximants have new (eikonal) singularities; one must justify integrating on the same contour and that it is
far enough from Glauber (Lemma D.20, (A4)(iii)); (f) UV: $T_R$ extends integrals to all momenta, single $C_R\Gamma$ are UV
divergent; regularize, show the sum is finite, and that divergences are absorbed by factor renormalization (D.7).

## 2. D.2 Set-up: regions, neighbourhoods, power suppression (pp.130–134)

**Definition D.2 (regulated integral and power suppression).** $Q=1$. $\Gamma$ is an *integrand*: one graph, or a sum of graphs
with a common loop-momentum routing (cross sections: Ass. D.27); UV subdivergences removed at integrand level (Ass. D.41), so
$\Gamma$ is a function on loop-momentum space, not a formal sum of graphs and counterterm graphs. $\lambda\in(0,\lambda_0]$ power
parameter (IR regulator/$Q$, e.g. $m/Q$; under Ass. D.27 also a measured variable, e.g. $\tau^{1/2}$ or $q_T/Q$),
$L\equiv\ln(1/\lambda)$; $d=4-2\epsilon$, $\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0]$. $\Omega$: $dL_\Gamma$-dimensional contour (real space or a
complex deformation), $\mu$ measure (incl. Jacobian); $\mathcal V$: finite sums of rational functions of loop momenta
(denominators: propagators with $i0$ and eikonal denominators) times measurement functions. For $F\in\mathcal V$ and measurable
(possibly $\lambda$-dependent) $A\subset\Omega$: $F\in\mathfrak S(A)$ ("power suppressed on $A$") iff $\exists C,a>0,K$:
$$\int_A|F|\,d\mu\le C\lambda^aL^K\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\lambda_0],\ \epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0]\ \text{uniformly.}\qquad\text{(D.3)}$$
"Leading power" = $O(L^K)$. $\mathfrak S(A)$ is linear; $A'\subset A\Rightarrow\mathfrak S(A)\subset\mathfrak S(A')$;
$\mathfrak S(A_1)\cap\mathfrak S(A_2)\subset\mathfrak S(A_1\cup A_2)$.
*Why integral norm:* errors of measurement functions (e.g. $\theta(\tau-\tau(X))$) are $O(1)$ on a thin layer — pointwise bounds
fail, integral bounds can hold; the proofs only use the triangle inequality. *Why uniform in $\epsilon$ needs a single integrand:*
with $\overline{\rm MS}$ counterterm graphs the cancellation is not pointwise, $\int|\Gamma|\sim1/\epsilon$, (D.3) impossible.

**Definition D.3 (regions and partial order).** Two collinear directions $n$ (A), $\bar n$ (B) (one-sided processes like DIS:
only A). Modes $\{S,A,B,H\}$ with $S<A<H$, $S<B<H$, $A,B$ incomparable — a lattice, $A\wedge B=S$, $A\vee B=H$.
(i) A region $R$ assigns to each internal line $j$ a mode $R(j)$; external lines A or B by direction (colourless, e.g. photon:
H). Vertex mode = sup of modes of adjacent lines (incl. external). $\mathsf H_R,\mathsf J_{R,A},\mathsf J_{R,B},\mathsf S_R$ =
lines and vertices of that mode; line $e$ "enters $Y$ from $X$" if $e$ has mode $X$ and an endpoint is a vertex of $Y$.
(ii) Pinch set in the massless limit
$$\pi_R=\{k:\ l_j(k)=0\ (R(j)=S),\ l_j(k)\in\mathbb Rn\ (R(j)=A),\ l_j(k)\in\mathbb R\bar n\ (R(j)=B)\},\qquad\text{(D.4)}$$
an affine subspace (possibly empty). (iii) Per-line order $R'\le R$ iff $R'(j)\le R(j)\ \forall j$; per-line meet
$(R\wedge R')(j)=R(j)\wedge R'(j)$. (iv) $\mathcal R$: finite set containing all leading regions of Thm 3.12, each with
$\pi_R\ne\emptyset$; may (and for (A2)(A4) often must) contain subleading assignments; maximum $R_{\max}$ (all hard),
$\pi_{R_{\max}}=$ whole space.
*Convention:* per-line meets can create non-canonical assignments, e.g. a soft line with both ends at hard vertices; the rule of
Def. D.19 (soft line entering $\mathsf H_R$ set to 0 in $\mathsf H_R$) would give scaleless UV divergences $\int d^dk/k^2$,
violating (H3); hence $T_R$ does **not** replace soft lines with both ends in $\mathsf H_R$ (treated as hard); pinch set still by
labels (Lemma D.4 unaffected). Soft lines attached only to one jet: subleading (proof of Table 4 row 6), may stay in $\mathcal R$;
with rapidity-regularized TMD approximators they must stay.

**Lemma D.4 (meets and lattice structure).** (a) $R'\le R\Rightarrow\pi_{R'}\subseteq\pi_R$. (b) $\pi_{R\wedge R'}=\pi_R\cap\pi_{R'}$.
(c) If $\pi_R\cap\pi_{R'}\ne\emptyset$ and $R\wedge R'\in\mathcal R$, then $R\wedge R'$ is the greatest lower bound of $R,R'$ in
$\mathcal R$; if $\pi_R\cap\pi_{R'}=\emptyset$ they have no common lower bound in $\mathcal R$.
*Proof:* line by line: $\{0\}\subset\mathbb Rn$, H imposes nothing; $\mathbb Rn\cap\mathbb R\bar n=\{0\}$; (c) from per-line
order and (a)(b). — correct.

$k^-$ deformation is limited by the soft line's own pole to $\kappa\sim k_T^2/|k^+|$, giving exactly $|k^+k^-|\gtrsim k_T^2$

graph class $\mathcal G_0$ and graded configurations, Prop. E.43, general case = Ass. E.44(a)); second inequality: soft-approximated
jet denominator not closer to zero than the natural virtuality $v_A(p)$ — reasonable in IR-regulated (off-shell external) amplitudes
at finite distance from true pinches, verifiable in the triangle (Ex. D.47), unproven in general. (A4)(iii) replaces the common
claim "$\kappa\sim|\mathbf k_T|$", which contradicts the soft line's pole position.

## 3. D.3 The precise subtraction theorem (pp.134–138)

$T_R$ ($R\in\mathcal R$) are linear maps on $\mathcal V$ (App. D.4); subtraction terms by (6.3):
$C_R\Gamma=T_R(\Gamma-\sum_{R'<R}C_{R'}\Gamma)$. For $R_0\in\mathcal R$:
$$X_{R_0}\equiv\Gamma-\sum_{R<R_0}C_R\Gamma,\qquad C_{R_0}\Gamma=T_{R_0}X_{R_0}.\qquad\text{(D.8)}$$
Identities in $\mathcal V$ are identities of rational functions (× measurement functions), valid a.e. pointwise on $\Omega$; they
involve no integrability.

**Definition D.9 (Collins conditions).** For $R_0\in\mathcal R$:
- (H1)$_{R_0}$ accuracy: $(1-T_{R_0})X_{R_0}\in\mathfrak S(U_{R_0})$.
- (H2)$_{R_0}$ suppression of non-smaller regions: $\forall R\in\mathcal R$, $R\not\le R_0$ ($R>R_0$ or incomparable):
  $C_R\Gamma\in\mathfrak S(U_{R_0})$.
- (H3) integrability: $\Gamma\in L^1(\Omega)$ with $\int_\Omega|\Gamma|d\mu$ uniformly bounded for $\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0]$;
  $\forall R\in\mathcal R$, $\forall\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0]$: $C_R\Gamma\in L^1(\Omega)$.
(H1) is the precise (P1): on $U_{R_0}$, $T_{R_0}$ is accurate not on $\Gamma$ but on $\Gamma$ minus all smaller subtraction terms
(Ex. D.1(b)); (H2) the precise (P2) (Ex. D.1(a) is $R_0=C_1$, $R=C_2$); (H3) only for $\Gamma$ and subtraction terms (Ex. D.1(c)).

**Theorem D.10 (precise subtraction theorem).** Assume (A1)(a), (H3), and (H1)$_{R_0}$, (H2)$_{R_0}$ for every $R_0$. Then
$r\equiv\Gamma-\sum_RC_R\Gamma\in\mathfrak S(\Omega)$:
$$\int_\Omega\Big|\Gamma-\sum_{R\in\mathcal R}C_R\Gamma\Big|d\mu\le C\lambda^aL^K\quad\forall\lambda\in(0,\lambda_0],\ \epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0].\qquad\text{(D.9)}$$
In particular $\int\Gamma=\sum_R\int C_R\Gamma+O(\lambda^aL^K)$, each $\int C_R\Gamma$ absolutely convergent and equal to the
real-space integral with each denominator's own $i0$.
*Proof:* split $\mathcal R$ into $R<R_0$, $R=R_0$, $R\not\le R_0$; pointwise
$$r=X_{R_0}-T_{R_0}X_{R_0}-\sum_{R\not\le R_0}C_R\Gamma=(1-T_{R_0})X_{R_0}-\sum_{R\not\le R_0}C_R\Gamma\qquad\text{(D.10)}$$
∈ $\mathfrak S(U_{R_0})$ by (H1)(H2); finite cover (Lemma D.6) ⇒ $r\in\mathfrak S(\Omega)$; (H3) ⇒ linearity of the integral;
last sentence by (A1)(a). *The proof is deliberately elementary: all content is in (H1)(H2).* — R6: correct.

**Lemma D.11 (chain expansion).** A chain is a totally ordered $c=\{R_1>R_2>\dots>R_j\}$ ($j=|c|\ge0$),
$T_c\equiv T_{R_1}T_{R_2}\cdots T_{R_j}$ (largest leftmost, acts last), $T_\emptyset=1$; for $\mathcal P\subset\mathcal R$:
$\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}\equiv\sum_{c\subset\mathcal P}(-1)^{|c|}T_c$. Then
$$C_R\Gamma=\sum_{c:\max c=R}(-1)^{|c|-1}T_c\Gamma,\qquad X_{R_0}=\mathcal O_{\downarrow R_0}\Gamma,\qquad r=\mathcal O_{\mathcal R}\Gamma,\qquad\text{(D.11)}$$
$\downarrow R_0=\{X\in\mathcal R:X<R_0\}$; for totally ordered $\mathcal P=\{P_1<\dots<P_n\}$:
$\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}=(1-T_{P_n})\cdots(1-T_{P_1})$. *Proof:* induction along the order; $(R',c')\mapsto\{R\}\cup c'$ is a bijection
onto chains with max $R$ and length $\ge2$, $-(-1)^{|c'|-1}=(-1)^{|c|-1}$. — R6: verified on the Fig. 63 poset.

**Lemma D.12 (pairing decomposition).** Assume (A2). Fix $R_0$; $\uparrow R=\{X:X>R\}$; $\mathcal I'$ ($\mathcal I''$) = regions
incomparable with $R_0$ with $\pi_R\cap\pi_{R_0}\ne\emptyset$ ($=\emptyset$); $R_\wedge\equiv R\wedge R_0$. Pointwise
$$r=\mathcal O_{\uparrow R_0}\big[(1-T_{R_0})X_{R_0}\big]-\sum_{R\in\mathcal I'}\mathcal O_{\uparrow R}T_R\big[(1-T_{R_\wedge})X_{R_\wedge}\big]-\sum_{R\in\mathcal I''}\mathcal O_{\uparrow R}T_R\Gamma.\qquad\text{(D.12)}$$
*Proof:* split chains: $\mathcal C_1$ all elements comparable with $R_0$ ($c_>\sqcup c_0\sqcup c_<$); $\mathcal C_2'$ / $\mathcal C_2''$:
let $R^*(c)$ be the smallest element incomparable with $R_0$; elements below $R^*$ are $<R_0$, hence $\le R_\wedge$ by Lemma
D.4(c); for $R^*\in\mathcal I''$ there are none. — R6: verified for $R_0=(C_1,C_1)$ on the Fig. 63 poset.
Remark: the $\mathcal O_{\uparrow R}$ are alternating sums; expanding them regenerates non-integrable terms (Ex. D.48: on $U_{R_2}$,
$T_H(1-T_2)\Gamma$ is not integrable, while $\mathcal O_{\uparrow R_2}(1-T_2)\Gamma=r$). The decomposition says which combinations
must be estimated; it does not reduce the problem to single-approximation errors.

**Corollary D.13 (product formula).** Assume all pairwise pinch sets intersect, (A2), and
(Com) $T_RT_{R'}=T_{R'}T_R$ ($T_R$ idempotent); (Abs) for incomparable $R,R'$: $T_RT_{R'}=T_RT_{R\wedge R'}T_{R'}$. Then (factors
commute)
$$C_R\Gamma=T_R\prod_{R'<R}(1-T_{R'})\Gamma,\qquad X_{R_0}=\prod_{R<R_0}(1-T_R)\Gamma,\qquad r=\prod_{R\in\mathcal R}(1-T_R)\Gamma,\qquad\text{(D.13)}$$
and (H1)$_{R_0}$, (H2)$_{R_0}$ are respectively **equivalent** to
$$\prod_{R\le R_0}(1-T_R)\Gamma\in\mathfrak S(U_{R_0}),\qquad G_{R_0}(R)\equiv T_R\!\!\prod_{R'\in\downarrow R\cup\{R_0\}}\!\!(1-T_{R'})\Gamma\in\mathfrak S(U_{R_0})\ \ (R\not\le R_0).\qquad\text{(D.14)}$$
*Proof:* $T_{\mathcal S}=\prod_{R\in\mathcal S}T_R$; (Com)(Abs) ⇒ $T_{\mathcal S}=T_{\mathcal S\cup\{x\wedge y\}}=T_{\bar{\mathcal S}}$
(meet closure). For $\mathcal P=\downarrow R$ or $\mathcal R$ (meet-closed): $\prod(1-T_R)=\sum_{\mathcal K}T_{\mathcal K}\sum_{\mathcal S:\bar{\mathcal S}=\mathcal K}(-1)^{|\mathcal S|}$
over meet-closed $\mathcal K$; with $\mathcal M\subset\mathcal K$ the meet-irreducibles: $\bar{\mathcal S}=\mathcal K$ iff
$\mathcal M\subset\mathcal S\subset\mathcal K$, inner sum $=(-1)^{|\mathcal M|}\sum_{\mathcal A\subset\mathcal K\setminus\mathcal M}(-1)^{|\mathcal A|}=0$
unless $\mathcal K=\mathcal M$, i.e. $\mathcal K$ is a chain ⇒ $\prod(1-T_R)=\mathcal O_{\mathcal P}$. For incomparable $R$:
$T_R(1-T_{R_\wedge})T_{R_0}=0$ by (Abs)(Com), so $C_R\Gamma=G_{R_0}(R)$. — R6: proof correct (it is a Möbius-function argument on
the meet-semilattice); verified on the 9-element poset with a coordinate-zeroing model satisfying (Abs).
Remarks: in (D.14) $(1-T_{R_0})$ supplies the small factor on $U_{R_0}$ (Lemma D.25); the other $(1-T_{R'})$ supply no smallness
but prevent new unregulated pinches when $T_R$ removes IR regulators — why single chain terms $T_c(1-T_{R_0})\Gamma$ are not
integrable while $G_{R_0}(R)$ is (Ex. D.47, D.48). (Com)(Abs) hold exactly in the scalar triangle and, with adapted routing, for
momentum substitutions; for gauge-theory polarization projections (Abs) may fail by a transverse coupling of a direction-flipping
line (Lemma D.23(iii)) — then Cor. D.13 cannot be used literally.

**Assumption D.14 (Collins conditions on general graphs).** For every integrand $\Gamma$ of the process (single graph, or family
with common routing) and its $\mathcal R$: (H1)–(H3) hold.
Discussion: this is what Collins [1] ch. 10 power counting asserts and what App. D does **not** prove for general graphs. Provable:
(i) under (A3)(A4) $T_{R_0}$ is accurate on $\Gamma$ itself on $U_{R_0}$ (Lemma D.26) = (H1) for minimal $R_0$; (ii) under (Com)(Abs)
(H1)(H2) ⇔ grouped power counting (D.14); (iii) in the D.9 examples the grouped expressions and their small factors can be written,
closed forms of subtraction terms/remainder and region integrals hold exactly; but uniform integral bounds (and literal (H3) on the
deformed $\Omega$) need a contour deforming $k^+$ and $k^-$ simultaneously, not written. Obstacles in general: term-by-term
sufficient conditions are false (Ex. D.48); (Abs) may fail for polarization projections; without adapted routing (Com) only modulo
loop-momentum shifts (Remark D.24).

**Remark D.15 (forest formula analogy).** Chain expansion (D.11) resembles Zimmermann's forest formula [78] but differs: the $R$
operation sums over all forests (pairwise nested or disjoint renormalization parts) of $\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}(-t_\gamma)$,
overlapping subgraphs never together; here chains correspond only to nested families — "disjoint" elements have no counterpart;
incomparable regions never appear in one chain even if their pinch sets intersect; their interplay is handled through the meet
(Lemma D.12), like overlapping divergences in BPHZ. Cor. D.13: under (Com)(Abs) the chain expansion is the product
$\prod(1-T_R)$, as the forest formula for a single nested string. As in BPHZ, rigorous estimates only for the whole product (or
suitable groupings), never for expanded single terms. (Consistent with our REVIEW O10.)

**Proposition D.16 (removing $\epsilon$).** Under Thm D.10, if $\int\Gamma$ is analytic near $\epsilon=0$ and (A1)(b) holds, then
$F(\epsilon)\equiv\sum_R\int C_R\Gamma$ has no pole at $\epsilon=0$ and $|\int\Gamma-F|_{\epsilon=0}\le C\lambda^aL^K$.
*Proof:* $F$ meromorphic; on $(0,\epsilon_0]$, $F=\int\Gamma-\int r$ is bounded (uniform (D.9) + continuity of $\int\Gamma$); a
pole of order $k\ge1$ would give $\epsilon^kF\to c\ne0$. Bound survives $\epsilon\to0^+$. — correct. Meaning: UV poles of single
$C_R\Gamma$ cancel in the sum over $R$, graph by graph; their organisation into renormalization constants: D.7.

**Assumption D.17 (values of single chain terms).** There is a linear rule $T_c\Gamma\mapsto\langle T_c\Gamma\rangle$ (e.g. besides DR,
raise every denominator changed by an approximator to $(\cdot)^{1+\delta}$, continue in $(\epsilon,\delta)$, then $\delta\to0$) with
(D1) $\sum_{c:\max c=R}(-1)^{|c|-1}\langle T_c\Gamma\rangle=\int_\Omega C_R\Gamma\,d\mu$ (compatible with the absolutely convergent
subtraction terms); (D2) scaleless chain terms have value zero.
Used only to identify subtraction-term values with term-by-term region-method values ([16,129]): "subtracted jet function = unsubtracted"
(Prop. D.30), "hard coefficient = on-shell form factor with IR poles removed" (Thm D.46); no rigorous estimate uses it. (D2) is the
usual DR convention; (D1) is non-trivial because single chain terms are not absolutely convergent for any $\epsilon$ (Ex. D.1(c)), so
$\int C_R\Gamma=\sum\pm\int T_c\Gamma$ is not linearity of the integral. Verified in the triangle (Ex. D.47).

## 4. D.4 Explicit approximators (pp.138–142)

Light-cone projections $\pi_+(v)=(\bar n\cdot v)n=(v^+,0,\mathbf0_T)$, $\pi_-(v)=(n\cdot v)\bar n=(0,v^-,\mathbf0_T)$ (here
$n\cdot\bar n=1$, $n^+=1$, $\bar n^-=1$ — note: different normalization from our SCET convention $n\cdot\bar n=2$); idempotent,
commuting, $\pi_+\pi_-=0$; $\pi_+(k)=\hat k$ (Lemma 6.1).

**Definition D.18 (hard–collinear approximation).** For each line $e$ entering $\mathsf H_R$ from $\mathsf J_{R,A}$ (momentum $k_e$):
(i) *momentum substitution:* in all factors of $\mathsf H_R$ (propagators, vertices, measurement functions depending only on
$\mathsf H_R$ momenta) $k_e\to\pi_+(k_e)$; $\pi_-(k_e)$ for lines from $\mathsf J_{R,B}$; set IR regulators (light quark masses,
external off-shellness) to zero in $\mathsf H_R$. (ii) *projections:* quark lines (fermion number into $\mathsf H_R$): insert
$\mathcal P_+=\frac12\gamma^-\gamma^+$ between $\mathsf H_R$ and $\mathsf J_{R,A}$; out-flowing: $\mathcal P_-$ (since
$\overline{\psi_+}=\bar\psi\mathcal P_-$); gluon lines: the constant matrix
$$\mathcal P_A{}^\mu{}_\nu=g_T{}^\mu{}_\nu+n^\mu\bar n_\nu=\delta^\mu_\nu-\bar n^\mu n_\nu,\qquad g_T{}^\mu{}_\nu=\delta^\mu_\nu-n^\mu\bar n_\nu-\bar n^\mu n_\nu,\qquad\text{(D.15)}$$
i.e. $H_\mu J^\mu\to H_\mu\mathcal P_A{}^\mu{}_\nu J^\nu=H^-J^+-\mathbf H_T\cdot\mathbf J_T$: first term = longitudinal K term of
(3.10) ($n^\mu\bar n_\nu=\pi_+(k_e)^\mu\bar n_\nu/\bar n\cdot k_e$ for $\bar n\cdot k_e\ne0$), second = transverse (physical).
B: $n\leftrightarrow\bar n$, $\mathcal P_+\leftrightarrow\mathcal P_-$. $\mathcal P_\pm^2=\mathcal P_\pm$; $K=n\otimes\bar n$:
$g_TK=Kg_T=0$, $K^2=K$ ⇒ $\mathcal P_A^2=\mathcal P_A$. (All verified with explicit Dirac matrices / metric.)
*On i0:* $T_R$ creates no new denominators; $1/\bar n\cdot k$ appears only when the Ward identity rewrites K terms into Wilson lines,
$\mathsf H_\mu(\hat k)n^\mu=\mathsf H_\mu(\hat k)\hat k^\mu/\bar n\cdot k$; the rewritten terms carry $1/(\bar n\cdot k\pm i0)$ but their
sum has no pole at $\bar n\cdot k=0$ (equals the regular original), so it is prescription independent; single Wilson-line graphs
are not. Collinear PDF: the two choices give $W[w^-,+\infty]W[+\infty,0]$ and $W[w^-,-\infty]W[-\infty,0]$ (same light-like line),
both $=W[w^-,0]$ by the group property (the operator of Def. 2.1). TMD ($\mathbf b_T\ne0$): future/past give different functions,
fixed by the process (Sec. 10, 10.4) — not re-derived here.

**Definition D.19 (soft approximation).** For each soft line $s$ (momentum $k$) of $R$ attached to $\mathsf J_{R,A}$: (i) $k\to\pi_-(k)$
in all factors of $\mathsf J_{R,A}$ (the A jet sees only $k^-=n\cdot k$); $\pi_+(k)$ for $\mathsf J_{R,B}$; a soft line entering
$\mathsf H_R$ (only in subleading regions) is set to 0 in $\mathsf H_R$, except soft lines with both ends in $\mathsf H_R$ (convention
after Def. D.3); factors of $\mathsf S_R$ kept exactly. (ii) projections: at the connection of a soft gluon to $\mathsf J_{R,A}$
insert $n^\mu\bar n_\nu$, i.e. $\mathsf S_\mu\mathsf J_A^\mu\to\mathsf S^-\mathsf J_A^+$; soft quark (endpoint region): $\mathcal P_\pm$.
For $n\cdot k\ne0$, $n^\mu\bar n_\nu=n^\mu\pi_-(k)_\nu/(n\cdot k)$ = Grammer–Yennie K term (3.11); after substitution $\mathsf J_A$
depends only on $\pi_-(k)$, so the Ward identity of Cor. 3.17 applies directly. $\tilde D=(p+\pi_-(k))^2-M^2+i0=p^2-M^2+2p^+k^-+i0$
is the jet propagator itself evaluated after substitution — its $i0$ is inherited; the Wilson-line $1/(k^-\pm i0)$ come from it.

**Lemma D.20 (i0 compatibility and amplitude of the deformation).** A soft momentum $k$ flows through a string of lines of
$\mathsf J_A$, each $l=p+k$ with $p$ A-collinear, and along the flow of $k$ all $p^+$ have the same sign $\eta=\pm1$ (all final-state:
$\eta=+1$), $|k^+|<|p^+|$. Deform $k^-\to k^-+i\eta\kappa$ ($\kappa\ge0$, other components real). Then:
(a) the exact $D=l^2-M^2+i0$, the soft-approximated $\tilde D$ and the $M\to0$ denominators satisfy
$\operatorname{Im}D=2\eta\kappa(p^++k^+)\ge0$, $\operatorname{Im}\tilde D=2\eta\kappa p^+\ge0$: they never vanish along the deformation;
hence it is legitimate for $C_R\Gamma$ built from these (and $k^-$-independent) denominators (this part of (A1)(a) holds).
(b) The soft line's own propagator $k^2+i0$ ($k^2=2k^+k^--k_T^2$) has $\operatorname{Im}k^2=2\eta\kappa k^+$, non-negative only for
$\eta k^+\ge0$. For $\eta k^+<0$ its pole $k^-=(k_T^2-i0)/2k^+$ lies on the deformation side; the contour must return near
$\operatorname{Re}k^-\approx k_T^2/2k^+$; the achievable amplitude is $\kappa\sim k_T^2/|k^+|$, **not** $\kappa\sim|\mathbf k_T|$.
*Proof:* direct computation. — R6: correct.
Consequences: at soft momenta with $|k^+|\gg|\mathbf k_T|$ one only gets $|k^-|\gtrsim k_T^2/|k^+|$, i.e. $|k^+k^-|\gtrsim k_T^2$ =
(A4)(iii) first (excludes Glauber) but not $|\tilde D|\gtrsim|p^+||\mathbf k_T|$; the soft error estimate must use (A4)(iii) second
(Table 4 row 5). Triangle: for $k=(z,\lambda^2/z,\lambda)$, $\lambda^2\ll z\ll1$, the $D_g$ and $D_1$ poles are on opposite sides,
$\sim\lambda^2/z$ apart, and the contour can only cross the axis in that gap. If $k$ flows through A-jet lines with both $p^+>0$
and $p^+<0$ (DY: a soft gluon connecting a spectator's final-state line and the active parton's initial-state line), the two
denominators require opposite deformations: pinch = Glauber pinch of Sec. 9; (A1) fails for single graphs (Ass. D.31, Prop. D.32).

**Definition D.21 (TMD approximation and rapidity regularization).** $q_T\ll Q$ ($\lambda=q_T/Q$), modes A, B collinear and soft
$Q(\lambda,\lambda,\lambda)$ (10.1). $T_R^{(y_A,y_B)}$ as Defs D.18, D.19 with three changes: (i) in $\delta^{(2)}(\mathbf q_T-\sum\mathbf l_T)$
all collinear and soft transverse momenta are kept exactly (only $\mathsf H_R$ factors are projected); (ii) the soft projection $\pi_-$
is replaced by $\pi_-^{(y_A)}(k)=(n_A\cdot k)\bar n$, $n_A(y_A)=(1,-e^{-2y_A},\mathbf0_T)$ (spacelike, $n_A\cdot\bar n=1$, still
idempotent), soft gluon projection $n_A^\mu\bar n_\nu$; B side $n_B(y_B)=(-e^{2y_B},1,\mathbf0_T)$; (iii) in the hard–collinear
approximation the A-collinear entering momentum is projected by $\pi_+^{(y_B)}(k)=(n_B\cdot k)n$ ($n_B\cdot n=1$, idempotent), the
longitudinal term of (D.15) becomes $n^\mu n_{B\nu}$ ($g_T+n\otimes n_B$ still idempotent), i.e. Wilson-line direction
$\bar n\to n_B(y_B)$; B collinear with $\pi_-^{(y_A)}$ and $n_A$. Light-like limit $y_A\to+\infty$, $y_B\to-\infty$.
Relative errors of (ii)(iii): $O(e^{-2(y_A-y_k)})$, $O(e^{-2(y_k-y_B)})$ ($y_k$ gluon rapidity); purpose: subtraction terms (Wilson-line
matrix elements) free of rapidity divergences. Caveats: $\pi_+^{(y_B)}\pi_-^{(y_A)}(k)=-e^{2y_B}(n_A\cdot k)n\ne0$ — (Com)(Abs) only to
$O(e^{-2|y|})$; and since $n_B\cdot k=k^+-e^{2y_B}k^-$, the hard part depends on $k^+-e^{2y_B}k^-$, so the collinear factor of
$T_R^{(y_A,y_B)}$ at fixed $x=k^+/P^+$ is *not* the light-front matrix element (10.2): relative difference $e^{2y_B}k^-/k^+=e^{2(y_B-y_k)}$,
$O(e^{2(y_B-y_P)})$ for typical collinear momenta but not small near $y_B$; it vanishes pointwise only as $y_B\to-\infty$; taking this
limit under the integral (after dividing by the soft factor) is part of Ass. D.34. (Vectors, idempotency, non-commutation formula
verified numerically.)

**Definition D.22 (exclusive approximation).** For a meson jet $\mathsf J_{R,A}$ entering $\mathsf H_R$ through a valence pair (momenta
$k$, $P-k$): in $\mathsf H_R$ replace by $\pi_+(k)=xP$, $\pi_+(P-k)=\bar xP$, and insert the DA projection
$$\mathsf J_{\alpha\beta}\longmapsto\tfrac14(\gamma^-\gamma_5)_{\alpha\beta}\,\mathrm{Tr}\big[\gamma_5\gamma^+\mathsf J\big]\otimes\frac{\delta_{ij}}{N_c}\mathrm{Tr}_c[\,\cdot\,],\qquad\text{(D.16)}$$
idempotent ($\frac14\mathrm{Tr}[\gamma_5\gamma^+\gamma^-\gamma_5]=1$), projecting onto the $\gamma^+\gamma_5$ structure of (11.1) and colour
singlet. Longitudinal gluons: $\mathcal P_A$; soft gluons: Def. D.19. Endpoint region $R_{\rm end}$: one line of the pair soft
($x\to0$ or $\bar x\to0$); $R_{\rm end}<R$. (Normalization and idempotency verified with explicit Dirac matrices; $\gamma^-\gamma_5$ in
$\mathsf H$ is the correct $\not p\gamma_5$ structure since $\not p=p^+\gamma^-$ for $p=(p^+,0,0)$.)

**Lemma D.23 (algebra of approximators).** Suppose global loop momenta $\kappa_1..\kappa_L$ can be chosen ("adapted routing") such
that for every $R$ and every factor $f$ the momentum substitutions of Defs D.18, D.19 set certain light-cone components of the
$\kappa_a$ and external momenta to zero (set $Z_R(f)$). Then (i) $T_R^2=T_R$; (ii) $T_RT_{R'}=T_{R'}T_R$ and
$Z_{RR'}(f)=Z_R(f)\cup Z_{R'}(f)$; (iii) for momentum substitutions (and mass zeroing) (Abs) ⇔ $Z_{R\wedge R'}(f)\subseteq Z_R(f)\cup Z_{R'}(f)$
for every factor, "always true for per-line meets"; for polarization projections the longitudinal (K) part is absorbed exactly, the
transverse $g_T$ part is not: if a "direction-flipping" line $e$ exists ($R(e)=A$, $R'(e)=B$), then $T_RT_{R'}-T_RT_{R\wedge R'}T_{R'}$
= coupling of the transverse polarization of $e$ at that connection. For scalar vertices (Ex. D.47) and one-direction processes (DIS)
there are no flipping lines, (Abs) exact. One-loop graphs, ladders (Fig. 24) and the two-loop planar vertex (Fig. 63, $\kappa=(k_1,k_2)$)
have adapted routings.
*Proof (case analysis):* coordinate zeroing and mass zeroing are commuting idempotents; constant projectors commute/idempotent;
(iii) factor by factor: hard factor of $R_\wedge$ is hard in both $R,R'$; collinear line with $R_\wedge(e)=A$ has $\{R(e),R'(e)\}=\{A,A\}$
or $\{A,H\}$; soft line entering $\mathsf H_{R_\wedge}$: soft in one of $R,R'$, or A in one and B in the other ($\{-,T\}\cup\{+,T\}$ = all);
soft line on a jet factor; polarization: $T_{R'}$ inserts $\mathcal P_B=g_T+\bar n\otimes n$, $T_{R_\wedge}$ the soft projection, product
= longitudinal part, so $T_RT_{R_\wedge}T_{R'}$ and $T_RT_{R'}$ differ by the $g_T$ term; if $T_R$ inserts $\mathcal P_A$ the longitudinal
parts are killed ($\mathcal P_A$ keeps $V^-W^+$, $\mathcal P_B$/soft keep $V^+W^-$). — R6: the projector algebra verified; see ERRORS E5
for an uncovered edge case of the "always true" inclusion.
Not claimed: that the transverse term is suppressed. If $e$ is a minimal (physical) connection of $\mathsf J_{R',B}$ to $\mathsf H_{R'}$
(active gluon carrying finite momentum fraction in a gluon-initiated subprocess) it is leading; if non-minimal (e.g. $k_1$ flipping
between $(C_1,C_1)$ and $(C_2,C_2)$) Lemma 3.7 gives an extra $\sigma(e)/|e^\pm|$, usable only inside grouped expressions. In general
part of Ass. D.14.

external B momentum $p_B$ and the new soft momenta (one-loop graphs are of this type). Then $T_R^{(Y)}T_{R'}^{(Y)}=T_{R'}^{(Y)}$; if $p_B^+\ne0$
(massive or with transverse momentum) only to relative $O(e^{-2Y}p_B^+/p_B^-)$; exact for $p_B^+=0$. In particular the one-loop A-collinear
subtraction is $C_A\Gamma=T_A\Gamma-T_S\Gamma$ (same accuracy); after summing graphs and Ward identity it equals in $b$ space
$\mathsf H^{(0)}[\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub(1)}(y_B)-\tilde S^{(1)}(y_A,y_B)]$.
(b) *(all orders, conditional)* under Ass. D.37, at each order $\alpha_s^N$
$$\sum_{\text{graphs}}\sum_R\int C_R\Gamma=\Big[\mathsf H\,\frac{\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub}(x_A,\mathbf b_T;y_B)\,\tilde f_B^{\rm unsub}(x_B,\mathbf b_T;y_A)}{\tilde S(\mathbf b_T;y_A,y_B)}\Big]^{[N]}\qquad\text{(D.21)}$$
as formal power series ($\mathsf H$ recursively defined by Thm D.46); the $O(\lambda^aL^K)$ difference to the true cross section is Prop. D.35, a
separate statement. I.e. subtracted collinear factor = unsubtracted / soft factor.
*Proof:* (a) $k$ soft in $R'$, A-collinear in $R$; line $f$ hard in $R$, B-collinear in $R'$. $T_{R'}$: $\pi_-^{(y_A)}$ on $k$'s A-jet attachments,
$\pi_+^{(y_B)}(k)=(n_B\cdot k)n$ on $f$ with soft projection; $T_R$: no change on A-jet lines; on $f\in\mathsf H_R$, $k\to\pi_+^{(y_B)}(k)$ (same) and
$p_B\to\pi_-^{(y_A)}(p_B)$ with mass zero and insertion $g_T+n\otimes n_B$. Since $p_B$ is on shell: $(p_B+\pi_+^{(y_B)}(k))^2-M^2=2p_B^-(n_B\cdot k)$,
while $(\pi_-^{(y_A)}(p_B)+\pi_+^{(y_B)}(k))^2=2(n_A\cdot p_B)(n_B\cdot k)$, $n_A\cdot p_B=p_B^--e^{-2y_A}p_B^+$: relative $e^{-2Y}p_B^+/p_B^-$; and
$(g_T+n\otimes n_B)\cdot$soft = soft (verified). Hence $T_RT_{R'}=T_{R'}$. If the B lines also carry a B-collinear loop momentum $q$, $T_R$
projects $q\to\pi_-(q)$ and $T_{R'}$ keeps $q$: equal only for on-shell $q$ — the restriction in (a); general case in Ass. D.37. One loop:
$C_A=T_A(\Gamma-T_S\Gamma)=T_A\Gamma-T_S\Gamma$. (b) induction: define $X_A$ by $\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub}=X_A\tilde S$; by D.37 the subtracted
contributions with A-collinear part of order $N$ sum to $\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub(N)}-\sum_{K=1}^N\mathcal X_A^{(N-K)}\tilde S^{(K)}$ times the rest
($\mathcal X_A^{(M)}$ = subtracted A-collinear contribution of order $M$); induction gives $\mathcal X_A^{(N)}=X_A^{(N)}$; pure soft regions give $\tilde S$;
total $X_AX_B\tilde S=\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub}\tilde f_B^{\rm unsub}/\tilde S$. (Calligraphic $\mathcal X$ vs $X$ distinguished in print — checked at 200 dpi.)

**Assumption D.37 (product structure of Ward identities).** At fixed order, after summing all graphs and all attachments of longitudinal and soft
gluons: (W1) each chain term's approximated integrand factorizes by Thm 3.14 and Cor. 3.17 into "hard ⊗ collinear matrix elements with Wilson lines ⊗
soft matrix element", and the Ward identities apply equally to integrands already modified by other approximators; (W2) grouped by the order of the
A-collinear part, $\sum_{\text{graphs},R}C_R\Gamma$ reorganizes into the form of the proof of Prop. D.36(b). (Prop. D.36(a) is (W2) at one loop;
the general proof would combine the chain expansion with (W1) term by term — not written.)

**Lemma D.38 (the square-root construction is an identity).** For any $y_n$ define
$$\tilde f_A\equiv\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub}(y_B)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde S(y_A,y_n)}{\tilde S(y_A,y_B)\tilde S(y_n,y_B)}},\qquad\tilde f_B\equiv\tilde f_B^{\rm unsub}(y_A)\sqrt{\frac{\tilde S(y_n,y_B)}{\tilde S(y_A,y_B)\tilde S(y_A,y_n)}}.\qquad\text{(D.22)}$$
Then $\tilde f_A\tilde f_B=\tilde f_A^{\rm unsub}\tilde f_B^{\rm unsub}/\tilde S(y_A,y_B)$ exactly (independent of $y_n$). If moreover (T2) holds,

Prop. 10.4 ($d\ln\sqrt{\zeta_A}=-dy_n$). *Proof:* product of the square roots $=\tilde S(y_A,y_B)^{-1}$; inserting (D.20) the $y_A$, $y_B$ dependence
cancels exactly. (Verified symbolically.) Remarks: equal slopes in (D.20) ⇔ existence of $\lim X_A$; $\tilde S$ depending only on $y_1-y_2$ is an exact

also [130]) — used as assumption.

## 7. D.7 UV divergences and renormalization (pp.151–154)

$T_R$ extends $\mathsf J_R$, $\mathsf S_R$ integrals to infinity where the approximation is invalid (lines actually hard) ⇒ new UV divergences of
single $C_R\Gamma$, cancelling in the sum (Prop. D.16). First the UV renormalization of $\Gamma$ itself:

**Assumption D.41 (integrand-level UV renormalization).** (U1) $\Gamma$ is a graph renormalized at integrand level by Zimmermann's forest formula,
$\Gamma=\sum_{\mathcal F}\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal F}(-t_\gamma)\Gamma^{\rm bare}$, $t_\gamma$ the Taylor subtraction in the external momenta of $\gamma$
(massive lines at zero momentum, massless lines with BPHZL auxiliary masses or Euclidean subtraction points [78, 80]), so that $\Gamma$ is a single
function on $\Omega$ and $\int|\Gamma|$ is uniformly bounded near $\epsilon=0$; the passage to $\overline{\rm MS}$ is a finite renormalization (finite
redefinitions of couplings and fields) (**see ERRORS E6**). (U2) For every $R$ and every renormalization part $\gamma$, $T_R$ and $t_\gamma$ commute up
to polynomials in the external momenta of $\gamma$ (finite local counterterms) that give no leading contribution in the power counting of $T_R$.
Partial proof of (U2): (a) if no factor of $\gamma$ is changed by $T_R$: trivial; (b) if $\gamma\subset\mathsf H_R$ (or $\gamma\subset\mathsf J_R$ with only
soft momenta entering through its external lines), $T_R$ acts on $\gamma$ by a linear substitution of its external momenta $p\mapsto\Lambda p$
($\Lambda=\pi_\pm$ or combinations): for Taylor subtraction at zero $t_\gamma(F\circ\Lambda)=(t_\gamma F)\circ\Lambda$ (exact); for a nonzero Euclidean
point $p_0$ they differ by the difference of Taylor polynomials of $F$ at $p_0$ and $\Lambda p_0$, a polynomial of degree $\le\omega_\gamma$; if $T_R$
also sets masses in $\gamma$ to zero, zero-momentum subtraction may be IR divergent → use the latter. Unproven third case: $\gamma$ contains lines of both
$\mathsf H_R$ and $\mathsf J_R$; $T_R$ changes only part of $\gamma$, the UV divergence of the approximated subgraph no longer matches the original
counterterm; (U2) asserts the mismatch is a local finite term. Without (U2) the $\epsilon$-uniform bound of Thm D.10 holds only for graphs without UV
subdivergences (e.g. the one-loop triangle). (R6: the Taylor-commutation argument is correct; the difference of the two Taylor polynomials is finite
because it equals $T_{\Lambda p_0}$ of the subtracted remainder, whose UV degree is negative.)

**Proposition D.42 (factorized form of DIS subtraction terms).** In the setting of Prop. D.28(a) with (Com)(Abs), summed over graphs:
$$\sum_{\text{graphs}}C_R\Gamma=\hat{\mathsf H}_R\otimes\mathsf J_R^{\rm bare},\qquad\text{(D.23)}$$
$\mathsf J_R^{\rm bare}$ = partonic matrix element in $d$ dimensions of the operator of Def. 2.1 (no operator renormalization), $\hat{\mathsf H}_R$ depends
only on projected momenta. *Proof:* only A and H in DIS; $R'<R$ ⇒ $\mathsf J_{R'}\supset\mathsf J_R$, $\mathsf H_{R'}\subset\mathsf H_R$, so every $T_{R'}$ changes only
factors of $\mathsf H_R$ and projections between $\mathsf J_R$ and $\mathsf H_R$, never $\mathsf J_R$'s internal factors; by (D.13)
$C_R\Gamma=T_R\prod_{R'<R}(1-T_{R'})\Gamma$ = "subtracted hard factor at projected momenta" × exact $\mathsf J_R$; K terms → Wilson lines (Thm 3.14,
Prop. 6.2). — correct.
Remark: if some $R'<R$ turns lines of $\mathsf J_R$ soft ($e^+e^-$, event shapes, DY, TMD), $T_{R'}$ changes $\mathsf J_R$ factors and the bare collinear
factor must be replaced by "minus its zero-bin": e.g. triangle $C_{C_1}\Gamma=T_{C_1}\Gamma-T_{C_1}T_S\Gamma$ = "jet minus its zero-bin" (Fig. 70(a));
Prop. D.36 gives $\tilde f^{\rm unsub}/\tilde S$; the general factorized form is Ass. D.37. The natural expectation that UV poles of $C_R\Gamma$ split into
operator divergences of the factors and divergences carried by subtraction terms in the hard part is not proven; only its consequence
(multiplicative renormalization) is used, as an assumption:

**Assumption D.43 (multiplicative renormalization of factors)** for collinear processes: (Z1) in moment space the bare collinear factor renormalizes
multiplicatively, $f_N^{\rm bare}=Z_Nf_N^{\rm ren}$ (matrix in parton species), $Z_N$ a pure $\overline{\rm MS}$ pole series, target independent; for gauge-invariant
twist-2 local operators a theorem of PT [79, 106, 107] provided the matrix elements are between on-shell states (off shell, mixing with BRST-exact and
EOM operators has nonzero matrix elements). (Z2) there exist partonic targets $j$ with an IR regularization (parameter $\lambda$) preserving the Ward
identities used in (D.23), such that Prop. D.28(a)(b) hold and $f^{\rm ren}_{N,\cdot/j}$ is finite at $\epsilon=0$ and invertible order by order.
(Z3) (comparison with DR IR) on-shell massless partonic targets with DR IR satisfy the same factorization identity with the same $\hat{\mathsf H}^{\rm bare}$.
Discussion: the regularization in (Z2) cannot be off-shell external partons (contradicts on-shellness in (Z1); off-shell PDFs are gauge dependent; the
off-shell examples of D.9 only illustrate subtraction structure), nor simply a gluon mass (breaks Ward identities in non-abelian theory); on-shell massive
quarks work as quark targets, but invertibility of $f_{i/j}$ as a matrix needs gluon targets, and no on-shell-gluon regulator preserving Ward identities and
regulating its collinear divergences is known ($g\to gg$ collinear singularity unaffected by quark masses) ⇒ (Z2) is a genuine input. (Z3) is consistent with
(D2) (partonic bare PDFs scaleless in DR, $f^{\rm bare}_{\cdot/j}=\delta$) but also requires the DR partonic amplitudes to be given by the same
$\hat{\mathsf H}^{\rm bare}$ — not derived from (D1)(D2), listed separately. For fragmentation functions (Prop. D.29) moments are not local-operator
matrix elements: (Z1) is an independent input.

**Theorem D.44 (subtraction compatible with renormalization; moment space, conditional).** DIS unphysical forward amplitude, $N$-th moment (index
dropped). Assume Prop. D.28(a)(b), Prop. D.42 and (Z1)(Z2), so that for partonic targets $j$
$$T_{(j)}=\sum_i\hat{\mathsf H}_i^{\rm bare}f_{i/j}^{\rm bare}+r_{(j)},\qquad|r_{(j)}|\le C\lambda^aL^K.\qquad\text{(D.24)}$$
Then (a) $C_i\equiv\sum_k\hat{\mathsf H}_k^{\rm bare}Z_{ki}$ is finite at $\epsilon=0$; (b) $C_i$ is independent of the target and its IR regularization;
under (Z3) it equals the moments of the DR partonic matching coefficients (6.6); (c) order by order in $\alpha_s$
$$C^{[n]}=\lim_{\lambda\to0}\Big[T^{[n]}_{(j)}-\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}C^{[m]}f^{{\rm ren}\,[n-m]}_{\cdot/j}\Big]\qquad\text{(D.25)}$$
(each term in the bracket diverges like $\ln\lambda$, only the combination has a limit; with DR IR drop $\lim_{\lambda\to0}$).
*Proof:* (a) by (Z1) $\hat{\mathsf H}^{\rm bare}Z=(T_{(j)}-r_{(j)})(f^{\rm ren}_{\cdot/j})^{-1}$; LHS independent of $\lambda$ and target, meromorphic near
$\epsilon=0$ ((A1)(b) + pure poles); RHS: $T_{(j)}$ moment of a conserved-current correlator (UV finite, IR regulated); $r_{(j)}$ regular at $\epsilon=0$
(Prop. D.16) and uniformly $\le C\lambda^aL^K$; $(f^{\rm ren})^{-1}$ finite ⇒ LHS bounded on $(0,\epsilon_0]$, no poles. (b) $C$ defined by the LHS;
another regularization with the same $\hat{\mathsf H}^{\rm bare}$ gives the same $C$; in DR $f^{\rm bare}=\delta$, $C=T_{(j)}(f^{\rm ren})^{-1}$ = moments of
(6.6). (c) $Cf^{\rm ren}=T-r$, expand $f^{\rm ren}=\delta+\sum_{k\ge1}f^{{\rm ren}[k]}$, $|r^{[n]}|\le C\lambda^aL^K\to0$. Alternatively replacing every bare
factor in the chain expansion by $Zf^{\rm ren}$ and absorbing $Z$ to the left gives the same linear relation; (D.25) determines $C^{[n]}$ uniquely.
— R6: argument correct (meromorphy + boundedness ⇒ regularity is the same device as Prop. D.16).
Remarks: everything in moment space: PDF renormalization = twist-2 local operator renormalization moment by moment; no Mellin-inversion uniqueness needed;
x space needs Lemma 5.5 / Thm 5.6 of the main text. Prop. D.28(c)'s x-space statement is linked by the same $\hat{\mathsf H}^{\rm bare}$.

**Note D.45 (TMD and soft factor).** For TMD factors, $b$-space multiplicative renormalization and soft-factor renormalization are (T3); the algebra of
Thm D.44 applies verbatim (products in $b$ space). In DR, non-TMD zero-bin integrals are scaleless (Prop. D.30); their "$1/\epsilon_{\rm UV}-1/\epsilon_{\rm IR}$"

## 9. D.9 Explicit examples (pp.155–160)

### Example D.47 (one-loop off-shell triangle: subtraction terms, remainder, region integrals)
Notation of D.1; $\mathcal R=\{S,C_1,C_2,H\}$, $\tilde Q^2=2PP'$.

*Algebra and closed forms.* On each quark line the approximations form a 4-element semilattice $\{1,e,t,h\}$: $e\circ t=t\circ e=h$, $h\circ x=x\circ h=h$,
$x\circ x=x$ (e.g. $T_H$ on $e_1$ sets $m\to0$ giving $h_1$; $T_S$ on $t_1$ replaces $k\to\pi_-(k)$ giving $h_1$) ⇒ (Com); e.g.
$T_{C_1}T_S:(D_1,D_2)\mapsto(e_1,h_2)$, $T_{C_2}T_ST_{C_1}=T_{C_2}T_{C_1}:(D_1,D_2)\mapsto(h_1,h_2)$ = (Abs) ($C_1\wedge C_2=S$). $S$ minimal, all pinch sets
contain the origin. By Cor. D.13
$$C_S\Gamma=\frac1{D_ge_1e_2},\quad C_{C_1}\Gamma=\frac1{D_gh_2}\Big(\frac1{D_1}-\frac1{e_1}\Big),\quad C_{C_2}\Gamma=\frac1{D_gh_1}\Big(\frac1{D_2}-\frac1{e_2}\Big),\quad
C_H\Gamma=\frac1{D_g}\Big(\frac1{t_1}-\frac1{h_1}\Big)\Big(\frac1{t_2}-\frac1{h_2}\Big)=\frac{D_g}{t_1h_1t_2h_2}\qquad\text{(D.26)}$$
(last step $h_i-t_i=k^2=-D_g$), and
$$r=\frac1{D_g}\big[\Delta_1\Delta_2-\Delta_1'\Delta_2'-\delta_1\delta_2\big],\qquad\Delta_i=\frac1{D_i}-\frac1{h_i},\ \ \Delta_i'=\frac1{e_i}-\frac1{h_i},\ \ \delta_i=\frac1{t_i}-\frac1{h_i}.\qquad\text{(D.27)}$$
**Verified** (recursion vs closed forms; product formula). Single chain terms ($T_{C_1}\Gamma$, $T_{C_1}T_S\Gamma$, $T_H\Gamma$) are not absolutely convergent for
$\epsilon>0$ (the first two for no $\epsilon$); each term of (D.26) contains only "one kind" of massless pinch, compensated by the corresponding difference.

*Region integrals without scaleless integrals.* For fixed $(k^+,\mathbf k_T)$ each term of (D.26) decays like $|k^-|^{-2}$; close the $k^-$ contour. For $C_{C_1}$,
$0<k^+<P$: $D_g$ pole $k^-=(k_T^2-i0)/2k^+$ lower, $D_1$, $e_1$ poles upper; lower residue:
$$\int\frac{dk^-}{2\pi}C_{C_1}\Gamma=\frac i2\frac1{2P'k^+}\Big[\frac1{k^+m^2(1-k^+/P)+Pk_T^2}-\frac1{k^+m^2+Pk_T^2}\Big]
=\frac i2\frac{m^2k^+/(2PP')}{[k^+m^2(1-k^+/P)+Pk_T^2][k^+m^2+Pk_T^2]};\qquad\text{(D.28)}$$
for $k^+>P$ the $D_1$ pole moves down, only the second term (tail of $-T_{C_1}T_S\Gamma$) $\propto-1/[k^+(k^+m^2+Pk_T^2)]$; for $k^+<0$ all poles upper, zero.
(D.28) vanishes linearly at $k^+\to0$, while both terms separately diverge like $1/k^+$. $C_S$, $C_H$ similarly ($C_H$: $t_2$ residue for $0<k^+<P$, $h_1$ residue for
$k^+>P$). **Verified numerically** against direct $k^-$ integration with $i\eta$, $\eta=10^{-7}$, at three $(k^+,k_T^2)$ points each for $C_{C_1},C_S,C_H$.

## GAPS (missing or weak) — and how our monograph can do better

G1. **The central input is assumed, not proven.** Ass. D.14 ((H1)–(H3) for general graphs) carries all physics; App. D is a precise reformulation of Collins' argument

convergent $(k^+,\mathbf k_T)$ integral for $0<\epsilon<1$), which sidesteps the 4D contour. R6 checked the key identity: the $d=4$ remainder density
